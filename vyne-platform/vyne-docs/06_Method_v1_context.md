# The VYNE Method
## Institutional Consulting Playbook — Version 1.0

**Status:** Draft for founder review. Derived from the approved volumes (Constitution, Framework & VYNE's 9, Client Experience System, Decision Library, Deliverables Toolkit) and the approved PRD. Where the approved methodology is silent or weak, this document says so explicitly and marks proposals as **[PROPOSED]** rather than silently inventing doctrine.
**Naming note:** referred to throughout as "the VYNE Method." Whether it carries the ™ symbol is a question for trademark counsel — do not use the mark publicly before clearance.
**The test this document must pass:** if the software disappeared tomorrow, a capable consultant holding only this document could still deliver the VYNE experience.

---

# 1. The VYNE Philosophy

VYNE exists to improve the quality of consequential career and business decisions made by financial advisors.

That sentence is the firm. Everything else — the platform, the deliverables, the brand, the fee model — is implementation.

**What VYNE believes:**

1. **The decision is the product.** A transition executed brilliantly in service of a poorly made decision is a failure. A rigorous process that ends in "stay" is a success. VYNE's output is not placements; it is decisions an advisor can defend to themselves five years later.
2. **Complexity, not scarcity, is the modern advisor's problem.** The advisor considering a move today faces more legitimate options than any prior generation — and nearly every party helping them evaluate those options profits from a particular answer. VYNE's job is to organize complexity into a decision, not to add information to the pile.
3. **Neutrality is an operating discipline, not a marketing claim.** VYNE is paid by hiring firms when advisors move. That conflict is real, permanent, and must be named — to ourselves constantly and to advisors early. The Method exists partly to make neutrality *procedural*: direction is defined before destinations are discussed; evidence standards gate recommendations; stay-decisions receive equal production quality. A consultant who relies on personal virtue to stay neutral will eventually fail. A consultant who follows the sequence will find neutrality built into the work.
4. **Trust is earned in the order of operations.** The recruiter's sequence is: opportunity → interest → fit → decision. VYNE's sequence is: reality → direction → constraints → options → decision. The difference is not cosmetic. An advisor who is asked "what are you building?" before "where would you go?" experiences a categorically different relationship — and notices.
5. **The advisor owns the decision.** VYNE informs, structures, models, and recommends. It never decides. The moment a consultant feels responsible for producing a particular outcome, they have stopped consulting and started selling.

**The difference between selling opportunities and guiding decisions, stated plainly:** a seller starts from the inventory (firms paying fees) and works backward to the advisor. A guide starts from the advisor and works forward to whatever the evidence supports — including nothing. Every technique in this playbook is downstream of that distinction.

# 2. Decision Psychology

This chapter exists because transition decisions are not analytical problems with emotional noise around them. They are identity decisions with an analytical layer on top. A consultant who treats the spreadsheet as the decision will lose the advisor at exactly the moment the advisor most needs help.

How each force shows up in advisor transitions, and how the Method answers it:

**Status quo bias.** The advisor's current situation carries no perceived decision risk — even when staying is objectively the riskiest path (deteriorating platform, retiring branch support, unresolved succession). It shows up as endless information-gathering that never converts to evaluation. *Method response:* the Current Reality Record makes the status quo itself an evaluated option with documented costs and risks, not the invisible default. STAY must earn its recommendation with evidence, exactly like MOVE.

**Loss aversion.** Deferred comp, unvested awards, and the retention package loom larger psychologically than equivalent gains from a better platform. Advisors routinely reject moves whose ten-year economics dominate because the year-one forfeiture is vivid. *Method response:* the Modeling discipline presents multi-year economics with every assumption classified, so forfeitures appear in context rather than isolation — and the consultant never argues the advisor out of loss aversion verbally. The model does the reframing; the consultant stays neutral about what the model shows.

**Confirmation bias — in both directions.** An advisor who has privately decided to leave discounts every stay argument; an advisor who fears leaving over-weights every horror story. *Method response:* VYNE's 9 and the Constraint Map force articulation of the case *against* the advisor's leaning before options are compared. The consultant's job in conversation is to be the advocate for whichever side the advisor is neglecting — visibly, and with permission ("my job here is to argue the other side for a moment").

**Information overload.** Recruiters bury advisors in decks; the internet buries them in contradictory anecdotes. Overload produces either paralysis or arbitrary simplification ("I'll just go where my friend went"). *Method response:* the Future-State Mandate is the filter. Once the mandate exists, information is either relevant to a mandate criterion or discarded. The Firm Comparison compares options against *their* mandate, never against a generic feature grid.

**Decision fatigue.** These processes run months. Late-stage advisors make worse micro-decisions and become vulnerable to whoever applies pressure last. *Method response:* the engagement is phased with explicit exit criteria, so no single conversation carries the whole weight; the Decision Completeness Scorecard tells everyone when the decision is actually ready, which licenses the advisor to *not* decide before then — a genuine relief.

**Identity.** "I am a Merrill advisor" is, for many, a twenty-year identity, and leaving is experienced as a small death regardless of economics. Independence decisions are even more identity-laden: employee vs. owner is a self-concept question. *Method response:* the Future-State Mandate deliberately asks who the advisor wants to *be*, not just what they want to earn. Consultants are trained to name identity out loud when they see it operating ("part of what's hard here isn't the economics — it's that you've been part of that firm your whole career"), because unnamed identity forces express themselves as unexplainable stalling.

**Team dynamics.** Multi-advisor teams contain divergent risk tolerances, career stages, and economics. The senior partner's great deal may be the junior partner's bad one. Unspoken team misalignment is the most common cause of late-stage collapse. *Method response:* team decisions require readiness evidence *per stakeholder*, and the Method treats "team alignment" as a scorecard dimension that must be green before a recommendation — not a detail to resolve after.

**Family considerations.** Spouses reasonably fear income disruption, guarantee forfeiture, and reputational risk. An advisor whose family hasn't been brought along will reverse at the finish line. *Method response:* the consultant asks directly, early, and without judgment: "who else needs to be comfortable with this, and what would make them comfortable?" The Decision Record is deliberately written so an advisor can hand it to a spouse.

**Client responsibility.** Advisors carry genuine fiduciary and personal weight about disruption to clients — and also use "my clients" as respectable cover for personal fear. Both are real; distinguishing them matters. *Method response:* client impact is examined through aggregate portability analysis (never client data — §0.2 governs) and honest transition-experience evidence. When the analysis shows clients will be fine and the advisor still hesitates, the consultant gently returns to what the hesitation is actually about.

**Fear of regret.** The deepest one. Not fear of a bad outcome — fear of *knowing it was their fault*. This is why advisors delegate decisions to recruiters, spouses, or inertia. *Method response:* the Decision Record is the Method's direct answer to regret. It documents what was known, what was weighed, what was rejected and why — making the decision defensible to the advisor's future self even if outcomes disappoint. Consultants should say this explicitly: "the goal is that in five years, whatever happened, you can reread this and stand behind how you decided."

**A caution on using this chapter.** These are lenses for understanding, not levers for pushing. The instant a consultant uses loss-aversion framing to *accelerate* a move, VYNE has become the thing it claims not to be. The ethical test: would you use the same technique if it made the advisor more likely to stay?

# 3. The VYNE Engagement Framework

Eleven phases. For each: purpose, objectives, outputs, questions, common mistakes, exit criteria, the advisor's likely emotional state, and the consultant's responsibility. Phases overlap in practice; exit criteria do not.

### 3.1 Discovery
**Purpose:** establish whether VYNE and the advisor should work together, and on what question. **Objectives:** understand the situation at headline level; explain the Method honestly, including how VYNE is paid; assess seriousness and fit; obtain permission to proceed. **Outputs:** advisor record (aggregates only), opportunity opened, decision-type hypothesis, documented restrictions. **Questions to answer:** What prompted this conversation now? What decision might actually be on the table? Who else is involved? What has the advisor already done? **Common mistakes:** pitching firms in the first call; over-promising neutrality instead of explaining the fee model plainly; qualifying so aggressively it feels like an interview. **Exit criteria:** advisor understands what VYNE is and how it's paid; a decision question is articulated; both sides have agreed to proceed. **Advisor state:** curious, guarded, testing whether this is another recruiter. **Consultant responsibility:** be the first person in this process who doesn't want anything from them yet.

### 3.2 Current Reality
**Purpose:** build the evidence-based portrait of the practice today — the trust cornerstone. **Objectives:** capture practice aggregates, economics, structure, strengths, frictions; verify what can be verified; separate fact from the advisor's narrative about the facts. **Outputs:** published Current Reality Record. **Questions:** What is actually true about this business? What is working that must be protected? Where is friction real vs. felt? **Common mistakes:** accepting the advisor's self-description without probing (advisors systematically understate concentration risk and overstate portability); drowning the record in data instead of judgment; requesting documents containing client PII (prohibited — request redacted or aggregate versions per §0.2). **Exit criteria:** the advisor reads the record and says some version of "yes — that's my business, and you've seen things I hadn't said out loud." If they merely say "looks fine," the record isn't done. **Advisor state:** surprised to be studied rather than sold; often moved by the mirror. **Consultant responsibility:** rigor. This deliverable earns the right to everything that follows.

### 3.3 Future-State Mandate
**Purpose:** define success before any destination is named. The Method's ethical signature. **Objectives:** articulate what the next chapter must make possible — economics, control, clients, team, identity, legacy; extract non-negotiables and explicit trade-off tolerances. **Outputs:** published Future-State Mandate. **Questions:** What must be true in five years for this to have been the right decision? Which trade-offs are you unwilling to make? Who do you want to be, not just what do you want to earn? **Common mistakes:** letting the mandate become a wish list with no trade-offs (a mandate that excludes nothing decides nothing); writing it in VYNE's language instead of the advisor's; skipping it because the advisor "already knows what they want" (they know what they're leaving; that is not the same). **Exit criteria:** the mandate contains at least one genuine non-negotiable and at least one accepted trade-off, confirmed by the advisor in conversation. **Advisor state:** engaged, sometimes unsettled — many have never been asked these questions. **Consultant responsibility:** refuse to discuss destinations until this exists. Advisors will push; the discipline is the value.

### 3.4 VYNE's 9
**Purpose:** the structured diagnostic — nine questions spanning what must be protected, where friction lives, what clients need, what the business must make possible, unacceptable trade-offs, failure risks, required information, paths deserving comparison, and what decision the advisor can responsibly own. **Objectives:** convert conversations into a structured profile; surface contradictions between the answers (there are always contradictions — they are the material). **Outputs:** published VYNE's 9 Profile; updated readiness dimensions. **Common mistakes:** administering it as a questionnaire instead of weaving it through conversation; treating it as marketing theater rather than working diagnosis; ignoring contradictions to keep momentum. **Exit criteria:** all nine answered with evidence, contradictions named in the profile. **Advisor state:** this is typically the moment the process starts feeling proprietary and serious. **Consultant responsibility:** listen for the contradiction between question 4 (what the business must make possible) and question 5 (unacceptable trade-offs) — the decision usually lives in that gap.

### 3.5 Constraint Mapping
**Purpose:** identify what stands between the advisor and any candidate future. **Objectives:** document economic constraints (deferred comp, notes, garden leave), legal (protocol status, non-solicits, team agreements), client (concentration, aggregate portability realism), team, operational, personal, and informational constraints; distinguish hard constraints from preferences wearing constraint costumes. **Outputs:** Constraint Map; evidence agenda for unresolved items. **Common mistakes:** taking the advisor's account of their contractual restrictions as legal fact (it is a claim requiring the document, and interpretation belongs to their counsel — VYNE flags, never opines legally); discovering a constraint in month four that a document request in month one would have caught. **Exit criteria:** every constraint has a status: verified, pending evidence, or advisor-accepted risk. **Advisor state:** sobered, sometimes deflated — this phase kills fantasies, which is its job. **Consultant responsibility:** be the person who found the problem in March instead of the person explaining it in September.

### 3.6 Firm Evaluation
**Purpose:** compare the paths and firms that deserve comparison — and only those. **Objectives:** select candidate options against the mandate (including the stay/strengthen option, always); obtain explicit advisor permission per firm before any contact; build comparisons from sourced, dated, confidence-rated intel. **Outputs:** firm options with permission states; published Firm Comparison. **Questions:** Which options genuinely fit the mandate? What does each cost in mandate terms, not just dollars? What don't we know, and how will we find out? **Common mistakes:** comparing six firms when the mandate eliminates four (breadth theater); letting firm marketing enter the comparison unlabeled; contacting a firm before permission (structurally blocked by the platform, but the *discipline* is the consultant's); leaving "stay" off the comparison. **Exit criteria:** every compared option traces to mandate criteria; every claim in the comparison has a source and date; the advisor has confirmed the option set. **Advisor state:** energized and at maximum vulnerability to salesmanship — the firms are now courting them. **Consultant responsibility:** be the un-courted voice in the room. When a firm's pitch conflicts with VYNE's intel, say so with sources.

### 3.7 Economic Modeling
**Purpose:** honest multi-year economics per scenario, with every input labeled. **Objectives:** model the realistic scenarios (including stay); classify every input — verified, advisor-provided, firm-provided, estimated, assumption; run sensitivities on the inputs that matter; present without advocacy. **Outputs:** versioned models; published Economic Model Report. **Common mistakes:** modeling the firm's projections as facts (firm-provided is a classification, not a verification); false precision (a ten-year projection to the dollar is a lie wearing a spreadsheet); burying the assumption that drives the answer; presenting only the scenario the advisor is leaning toward. **Exit criteria:** models pass hand-check tests; the advisor can articulate which assumptions drive which outcomes. **Advisor state:** often surprised — transition economics are less lopsided than recruiters present, and stay scenarios sometimes win. **Consultant responsibility:** protect the classification discipline against everyone, including the founder's own enthusiasm.

### 3.8 Recommendation
Governed fully by Chapter 5. **Purpose:** deliver VYNE's professional judgment — when, and only when, the evidence supports one. **Outputs:** the written recommendation deliverable with reasoning, confidence framing, and dissenting considerations. **Common mistakes:** recommending because the process "should" end in a recommendation; hiding uncertainty to appear authoritative; letting the recommendation arrive as a surprise (it should feel inevitable by the time it's written, because the advisor watched the evidence accumulate). **Exit criteria:** scorecard supports it; founder authored or approved it; the advisor has discussed it live before or alongside publication — a recommendation should never be *first encountered* as a document. **Advisor state:** the peak of the engagement's emotional weight. **Consultant responsibility:** deliver it as judgment, not verdict — "here is what the evidence supports and why; the decision is yours."

### 3.9 Decision Record
**Purpose:** capture the decision, the reasoning, the rejected alternatives, and the open risks — the Method's answer to regret. **Outputs:** published Decision Record. **Common mistakes:** writing it as justification for the chosen path instead of an honest account (the rejected options deserve their real strengths on paper); skipping it for stay-decisions (a stay Decision Record at full production quality is the single strongest proof of VYNE's neutrality); writing it in a voice the advisor's spouse couldn't read. **Exit criteria:** the advisor confirms it reflects their decision and reasoning. **Advisor state:** relief, and frequently pride. **Consultant responsibility:** make sure the document would still read honorably if the outcome disappoints.

### 3.10 Transition
**Purpose:** make the chosen path executable and calm (applies to MOVE; STRENGTHEN paths get an equivalent action plan). **Objectives:** publish the transition timeline; assign and track tasks; coordinate what VYNE legitimately coordinates; know the boundary of VYNE's role (VYNE is not the advisor's lawyer, compliance officer, or the hiring firm's onboarding team). **Outputs:** Transition Timeline; task cadence; placement record on start. **Common mistakes:** going quiet after the decision (the weeks before resignation are peak anxiety and peak reversal risk); drifting into legal or compliance advice; letting the hiring firm's timeline steamroll the advisor's readiness. **Exit criteria:** advisor started; milestones complete; economics recorded internally. **Advisor state:** stressed, executing, needing steadiness more than analysis. **Consultant responsibility:** presence. This phase is where the relationship becomes permanent — or doesn't.

### 3.11 Follow-up (GROW)
**Purpose:** verify the decision is delivering against the mandate; continue the relationship; harvest institutional learning. **Objectives:** scheduled reviews at roughly 90 days and one year against the *mandate*, not against generic satisfaction; de-identified lessons into the Decision Library; referral relationships earned, never demanded. **Outputs:** review conversations; library entries; the next engagement, eventually, for someone. **Common mistakes:** treating follow-up as pipeline farming (advisors smell it instantly); skipping follow-up on stay-decisions, which is where the neutrality claim is either proven or quietly abandoned. **Exit criteria:** none — this phase is the relationship. **Consultant responsibility:** show up when there is nothing to gain.

# 4. The Consultant Mindset

The Method's phases can be taught in a week. The mindset takes longer, and this chapter is where most consulting playbooks lie to themselves. Plain rules:

**When to challenge assumptions.** Challenge when an assumption is doing decisive work — when the decision would change if it were false. Let harmless assumptions pass; challenging everything is performance, not rigor. Always challenge with evidence or a question, never with mere contradiction: "You've said 90% of your book would follow you. What's that based on?" is a challenge. "That seems high" is an opinion.

**When to stay quiet.** After asking a hard question — the silence is where the answer forms; filling it rescues the advisor from thinking. When the advisor is working through emotion — naming it once is helpful; managing it is presumptuous. And when you don't know — "I don't know; I'll find out" is a trust deposit. Guessing is a withdrawal you may never recover.

**How to ask better questions.** Prefer questions whose answers would change something. Ask about the past to understand ("what did you do when the firm changed the grid in 2023?") rather than the hypothetical future the advisor will answer aspirationally. One question at a time. And ask the question under the question: "should I take the RBC offer?" usually contains "am I brave enough to leave?" — you'll eventually need to address the second.

**How to build trust.** There is no technique. There is a sequence: do rigorous work early (the Current Reality Record is the trust engine), tell the truth when it's costly, keep every small promise (the follow-up email, the Tuesday call), and never let the advisor discover anything you should have told them. Trust arrives on its own schedule, and attempts to accelerate it read as exactly what they are.

**How to remain neutral.** Don't rely on character; rely on process. Follow the sequence — direction before destinations. Give the stay case its full strength in every comparison. Track your own leaning and assign yourself devil's advocacy against it. Disclose the fee model before the advisor asks. And watch for the tell inside yourself: the moment you feel *disappointed* by evidence favoring stay, you have a conflict operating, and the correction is to over-weight that evidence in your written work, not to trust yourself to be fair.

**How to avoid becoming a salesperson.** Never argue with the advisor's hesitation. A seller overcomes objections; a consultant investigates them — the objection is data about either the option or the advisor, and both are useful. Related: never celebrate a move-decision more than a stay-decision in front of the advisor. They notice.

**How to disagree respectfully.** State it once, plainly, with reasons, in writing when it matters: "I want to be direct — I don't think this option fits the mandate you wrote, and here's why." Then respect the outcome. An advisor who chooses against VYNE's recommendation is exercising exactly the ownership the Method promises them; the Decision Record captures the disagreement without editorializing.

**How to know when the advisor is not ready.** The signs: new information keeps arriving but positions never update; the stated timeline perpetually recedes; the advisor seeks the same reassurance repeatedly; team or family stakeholders remain unengaged after months. The Method's answer is honest naming, not pressure: "I don't think you're ready to decide this yet, and I'd rather tell you that than march you to a decision you'll reverse." Sometimes the right output is PREPARE or CONTINUE DILIGENCE — and sometimes it is pausing the engagement. A consultant who cannot pause an engagement is running a pipeline, not a practice.

# 5. Recommendation Framework

The most consequential chapter — and the one where the approved methodology is currently thinnest. The five decision states exist in the approved volumes; the evidence standards below make them operational. **Sections marked [PROPOSED] are new doctrine requiring founder ratification.**

**Universal preconditions — no recommendation of any kind until:** the Current Reality Record and Future-State Mandate are published and advisor-confirmed; VYNE's 9 is complete with contradictions addressed; the Constraint Map has no critical unknowns; the Decision Completeness Scorecard shows no red dimension; and economics have been modeled for every seriously considered path, including stay.

**STAY** — appropriate when the current platform substantially satisfies the mandate; identified frictions are tolerable or addressable in place; no compared alternative offers mandate improvement that clearly outweighs transition cost and risk; and no forcing event (platform deterioration, succession cliff, contractual trigger) looms. *[PROPOSED] Evidence bar:* the stay scenario must have been modeled and compared with the same rigor as alternatives — STAY as a conclusion from inertia is not a recommendation, it's an abdication.

**STRENGTHEN** — appropriate when the mandate gap is real but addressable in place: renegotiation, team restructuring, internal succession, platform additions. Requires evidence the current firm will actually engage (precedent, relationships, leverage), not hope that it might. *[PROPOSED]:* every STRENGTHEN recommendation ships with a concrete action plan and a review date — otherwise it is STAY wearing ambition.

**PREPARE** — appropriate when the direction is clear but a constraint gates action: deferred-comp vesting, contractual windows, team alignment in progress, a practice not yet transition-ready. The recommendation names what must become true, by when, and what work happens meanwhile. PREPARE is a working state, not a waiting room.

**CONTINUE DILIGENCE** — appropriate when specific, nameable evidence is missing and obtainable. *[PROPOSED] discipline:* every CONTINUE DILIGENCE carries a defined evidence list and a time bound. Issued twice in a row on the same items, it means either VYNE cannot obtain the evidence (say so) or the advisor is using diligence to avoid deciding (Chapter 4's readiness conversation, not more diligence).

**MOVE** — the highest evidence bar, because it is the recommendation VYNE profits from, and everyone involved knows it. Appropriate when a specific destination satisfies the mandate materially better than stay and strengthen; multi-year classified economics support it net of transition costs and forfeitures; constraints are resolved or explicitly accepted by the advisor in writing; team and family alignment are evidenced, not assumed; and aggregate client impact has been honestly assessed. *[PROPOSED] The mirror test:* before publishing a MOVE recommendation, the founder writes — internally — the strongest honest case for staying. If that case beats the recommendation's rebuttal of it, the recommendation isn't ready. This artifact stays internal, but it must exist; it is VYNE's structural answer to its own conflict of interest.

**When VYNE refuses to recommend.** *[PROPOSED]:* VYNE declines to issue a recommendation when the advisor demands one ahead of the evidence ("just tell me what to do"); when the decision is dominated by factors outside VYNE's competence (legal interpretation, health, marriage); when the advisor has withheld material information; or when the honest answer is that reasonable paths are genuinely equivalent — in which case VYNE says *that*, with the trade-off map, and helps the advisor choose by values rather than manufacturing false differentiation. "The evidence doesn't favor one path; here is precisely what you'd be choosing between" is a legitimate, and sometimes the most honest, deliverable.

**Communicating uncertainty.** Confidence language is standardized and plain: *high confidence* (evidence consistent, assumptions few and sturdy), *moderate confidence* (evidence supports it; named assumptions could change the answer — listed), *provisional* (best current judgment; specific pending items could reverse it — listed with dates). Never express false precision; never bury the driving assumption; always name what would change VYNE's mind. A recommendation that cannot say what would falsify it is advocacy.

# 6. Deliverable Standards

Universal standards first; they outrank everything per-deliverable. Every deliverable: opens with what this document is, why it exists now, and what the advisor should do with it; is written to the advisor in the second person, in plain professional English an intelligent spouse could read; separates fact, analysis, and judgment visibly; labels every number's provenance; contains zero client PII by construction; and would survive being read aloud to the advisor's attorney. **The universal quality bar: could this document only have been written about this advisor?** If the advisor's name could be swapped for another's without the document failing, it is template output, not consulting — rewrite it.

**Current Reality Record.** *Average:* accurate inventory of the practice. *Exceptional:* tells the advisor something true about their business they hadn't articulated — the concentration they've normalized, the capability they undervalue, the friction they've stopped noticing. Tone: respectful, unflinching. Mistake to avoid: flattery. The record that praises everything teaches the advisor that VYNE's analysis is decoration.

**Future-State Mandate.** *Average:* restates the advisor's wishes. *Exceptional:* sharpens them — trade-offs made explicit, tensions between goals named, non-negotiables tested ("you said X is non-negotiable; option B fails it; do you still mean it?"). The mandate is exceptional when the advisor keeps a copy where they can see it.

**VYNE's 9 Profile.** *Average:* nine answers. *Exceptional:* the synthesis across answers — the contradictions and their implications. The nine questions are the skeleton; the finding is the connective tissue.

**Firm Comparison.** *Average:* a feature grid. *Exceptional:* every row traces to a mandate criterion; every claim carries source, date, and confidence; the stay option appears at full strength; and the document says clearly where options are genuinely equivalent instead of manufacturing differentiation. Mistake to avoid: false balance in the other direction — if the evidence favors an option, exceptional work says so.

**Economic Model Report.** *Average:* projections. *Exceptional:* the reader finishes knowing which two or three assumptions drive the answer, what the sensitivities are, and exactly which numbers are verified versus estimated versus a firm's marketing. Ranges beat point estimates; honesty beats precision theater.

**Meeting Summary.** *Average:* minutes. *Exceptional:* what mattered, what was decided, what changed, what happens next — in under a page. Drafted fresh for the advisor; never a sanitized paste of internal notes.

**Transition Timeline.** *Average:* a schedule. *Exceptional:* anticipates the hard moments (resignation day, the first client conversations, the quiet third week) and shows the advisor they've been thought about, not just scheduled.

**Decision Record.** *Average:* documents the choice. *Exceptional:* an honest intellectual history — the rejected paths at their genuine strength, the risks accepted with eyes open, the reasoning that will still read honorably if the outcome disappoints. This is the deliverable most likely to be reread years later; write it for that reader.

# 7. Advisor Experience Standards

What the advisor should feel at each threshold — and the universal negative space.

**After the first call:** *"That was not a recruiter call."* Interested, un-pressured, slightly surprised that VYNE explained its own fee model unprompted. Not: pitched, qualified, or hurried.
**After the first meeting:** *"They're studying my situation, not selling me one."* A sense that questions went somewhere deliberate.
**After the Current Reality Record:** *"They see my business — including things I hadn't said."* This is the moment trust converts from social to professional. If the advisor's reaction is polite approval rather than recognition, the Method has failed at its most important checkpoint — treat that as a fire alarm, not a passing grade.
**After the Firm Comparison:** *"Every option got a fair hearing, including staying."* Clarity about trade-offs, not excitement about a destination. If the advisor finishes the comparison feeling *sold*, rewrite it.
**After the recommendation:** *"This was earned, and it's still my call."* The recommendation should feel like the evidence's conclusion arriving on schedule — never a reveal, never a push.
**After the decision:** *"I can defend this — to my partner, my team, my family, and myself in five years."* Relief and ownership, not persuasion residue.
**During transition:** *"I am not alone in this, and someone competent is watching the map."* Steadiness. The advisor's anxiety is normal; VYNE's calm is the service.
**In Studio, always:** *"This was prepared for me."* Anticipated, oriented, respected. Per the PRD's philosophy: a room prepared before they arrived.

**The advisor must never feel — at any phase:** processed (a record in a pipeline), sold (urgency, scarcity, momentum mechanics), surveilled (their engagement tracked back at them), managed (handled toward an outcome), exposed (anything about their clients anywhere; anything they didn't consent to), or abandoned (silence at anxious moments — the post-decision quiet period is where firms most often fail their clients emotionally, and where VYNE will not).

# 8. Communication Standards

**The universal register:** an investment committee's clarity with a trusted counselor's warmth. Plain declarative sentences. No hype vocabulary — "game-changing," "huge opportunity," "crushing it" and their relatives are structurally banned; they are seller's words and advisors hear them as such. No hedging fog either: "I think X, because Y, and Z would change my mind" is the house sentence-shape.

**Email.** Short, purposeful, and titled honestly. One topic per email where possible. Anything consequential said by phone is confirmed in writing within a day — not for legal cover primarily, but because written confirmation is how advisors learn VYNE keeps its own record straight. Confidential materials follow the approved communication process (Q-13) without exception, including when the advisor is casual about it; VYNE holds the line on the advisor's own confidentiality even against the advisor's convenience. Never client PII in any channel, ever.

**Meetings.** An agenda exists and was shared. The consultant has read everything before walking in — asking an advisor to repeat what they've already provided is a small betrayal. Every meeting ends with what happens next, owned and dated. Internal notes and the advisor-facing summary are separate documents with separate purposes; the summary is drafted within 48 hours.

**Phone and video.** Scheduled beats ambush; the advisor's confidentiality context is checked before sensitive topics ("are you somewhere you can speak freely?"). Video: professional, quiet, undistracted — the advisor is evaluating VYNE's discretion in every frame.

**Reports and presentation.** Per Chapter 6 and the brand system: editorial restraint, findings before mechanics, one idea per page, sources visible. Present *to* the advisor, not *at* them — pause at judgment points; the presentation is a conversation with exhibits, not a performance.

**Confidentiality in speech.** No advisor's situation is ever discussed with another advisor, a firm, or a recruiter in identifiable form — including as social proof ("we're working with a big team at X" is a breach wearing a boast). Firms learn an advisor exists only with permission, structurally and behaviorally. The consultant assumes every industry conversation is eventually repeated.

**Follow-up.** Promised follow-up has a date; the date is kept. Unprompted follow-up — the article relevant to their situation, the regulatory change that affects their plan — is the relationship's compound interest, and it is never a disguised nudge toward movement.

# 9. Quality Standards

Quality at VYNE is a gate system, not an aspiration.

**Review tiers.** Self-review against Chapter 6 (the "only about this advisor" test) → peer review when a second qualified person exists (until then, see the cooling rule) → founder review, which is the approval gate for every advisor-facing artifact and every recommendation, without exception, including work the founder drafted. **[PROPOSED] The cooling rule for a solo founder:** no artifact is approved in the same sitting in which it was finished. Draft today, approve tomorrow. The overnight gap is the poor man's peer review, and it catches more than pride expects.

**Recommendation review is stricter:** the Chapter 5 preconditions verified against the scorecard; the mirror-test artifact written for MOVE recommendations; confidence language checked against the standard vocabulary; the falsifier ("what would change our mind") present.

**Publication standards.** Publication is the point of no return for trust, so the publish step re-checks: correct advisor, correct version, provenance labels intact, no internal residue (notes, rankings, pipeline language), no client PII, brand standards met. The platform enforces the workflow; the standard is that the human treats the publish click as signing their name — because it is.

**When work is delayed.** Work is delayed — and the advisor told honestly why — when evidence is incomplete, when the cooling rule hasn't run, when a material fact changed after drafting, or when the consultant's confidence in the work is performative rather than real. "This isn't ready, and you deserve ready" costs a day and buys a reputation. The deadline never outranks the standard; a firm whose deadlines outrank its standards has chosen its real product.

**When work is rewritten.** Rewrite — not patch — when the "only this advisor" test fails; when structure required apology in the meeting ("what this is trying to say is…"); when new evidence changes the finding rather than a figure; when the tone drifted toward selling. Patching a document with a wrong spine produces a longer wrong document.

**Institutional learning.** Every completed engagement feeds the Decision Library in de-identified form: what the Method predicted, what happened, where the process bent. Quality that doesn't compound is just effort.

# 10. Growing VYNE

**How the Method scales.** The Method is the firm's real IP — the platform is its delivery mechanism. Scaling therefore means reproducing judgment, which is the hardest thing any consulting firm does, and the honest premise of this chapter is that most fail at it. The controls: the Method is written (this document), enforced structurally (the platform's gates mirror its gates), and audited culturally (Chapter 9's reviews are where drift is caught).

**How new consultants learn. [PROPOSED] apprenticeship arc:** read the canon (Constitution, this playbook, the Decision Library) → observe two full engagements end-to-end → draft deliverables under review (expect full rewrites; that is the pedagogy) → lead phases with the founder present → lead engagements with founder review of recommendations only → independent, with recommendation review permanently retained by the firm. No consultant skips stages on the strength of prior recruiting experience — prior recruiting experience is often the thing being untrained.

**How recruiters become consultants — and the honest tension.** Recruiters are paid on placements; consultants are paid to be indifferent to them. A recruiter operating inside VYNE follows the Method's sequence and drafting discipline but does not approve or publish, and does not author recommendations — that separation is not distrust of individuals; it is the same structural humility VYNE applies to itself in the mirror test. A recruiter becomes a consultant by demonstrating, over real engagements, that their work product is indistinguishable in neutrality from the founder's — including visible comfort with stay-outcomes that cost them income. **[PROPOSED]:** promotion to consultant requires at least one engagement in the candidate's record where the right answer was stay, handled well. If the sample contains no stays, the sample is the problem.

**How AI supports but never replaces the Method.** AI drafts from structured records, accelerates research into review queues, and assembles first-pass documents — always entering the human lifecycle at the bottom, always marked, never publishing, never recommending (PRD §9 governs; §0.7 is permanent). The Method-level reason, beyond compliance: the advisor is buying accountable human judgment, and judgment that cannot explain itself in a live conversation with the advisor is not judgment VYNE can sell. AI reduces the cost of rigor; it must never reduce the amount of it.

**The founder's scaling discipline.** The founder's irreplaceable functions, in order: setting the recommendation standard, guarding neutrality economics, and training judgment. Everything else — research, drafting, scheduling, even client conversation coverage — is delegable earlier than it will feel. The classic failure is delegating the standard and keeping the busywork.

# Final Assessment

### Strengths of the VYNE Method
1. **Sequence as ethics.** Direction-before-destinations is simple, teachable, structurally enforced, and genuinely rare in this market. It is the Method's spine and its best answer to the fee-model conflict.
2. **Evidence-gated recommendations.** The scorecard, the classification discipline, and the Chapter 5 bars make "we don't recommend yet" a normal, defensible state — which is both ethically sound and commercially distinctive.
3. **The Decision Record as product.** Selling a defensible decision (rather than a destination) targets the advisor's deepest actual fear — regret — and produces an artifact competitors don't have.
4. **Written, enforceable, auditable.** The Method exists on paper with gates the platform mirrors. Most boutique firms' methodology lives in the founder's head; this one can survive contact with employee number two.

### Potential Weaknesses
1. **It is unproven.** Zero completed engagements. Every phase description above is theory until real advisors bend it — and they will. Treat Version 1.0 as a hypothesis with excellent documentation.
2. **It is slow by design, and the market sometimes isn't.** A retention-package deadline or a firm's collapse compresses timelines below the Method's comfortable minimum. The Method currently lacks an honest "compressed engagement" protocol — deciding what rigor is non-negotiable when there are three weeks, not six months. **This is the largest genuine gap in the document.** [Recommend developing after the first time-pressured engagement, not speculatively.]
3. **The neutrality architecture is procedural, not economic.** Everything here mitigates the fee-model conflict; nothing removes it. Procedural neutrality under economic pressure is a discipline that erodes silently — the mirror test and stay-metrics are tripwires, not cures.
4. **Emotional-labor load.** Chapters 2, 4, and 7 ask for sustained psychological attentiveness that is genuinely exhausting at volume. Nothing here yet addresses consultant capacity limits or the quality decay of an overloaded founder.

### Competitive Advantages
The Method reaches advisors *before* they self-identify as movers (paired with the content strategy); it produces tangible proof-of-process artifacts competitors can't match without rebuilding their economics; its neutrality is demonstrable rather than asserted; and it compounds — every engagement improves the library, the comparisons, and the models, while a relationship-based competitor's advantage retires with its founders.

### Risks as the Firm Scales
Judgment dilution (consultants performing the phases without the mindset — visible first in deliverables that pass structure checks but fail the "only this advisor" test); economic pressure normalizing MOVE (watch the stay-rate as headcount grows — a falling stay-rate with rising revenue is the alarm, not the achievement); the Method ossifying into ritual (phases followed because they're written, not because they serve the advisor); and founder-standard bottleneck inverted (if recommendation review can never be shared, the firm has a ceiling; if it's shared too early, it has a scandal).

### Recommendations Before Institutionalizing This Method
1. **Ratify or amend every [PROPOSED] item** — especially the Chapter 5 evidence bars and the mirror test, which change how you work, not just how you write.
2. **Run the first real engagement against this playbook and mark it up ruthlessly.** Version 1.1 should be written in the margins of a real case. Do not train anyone on 1.0.
3. **Decide the compressed-engagement question in principle now** (what is never cut, even at three weeks?) even if the full protocol waits for a real case.

Does this reflect how I build trust? Would I follow this process myself?* Where the answer is "not quite," the document is wrong, not you. This playbook is a rendering of your intended firm; the intent is yours to correct.
