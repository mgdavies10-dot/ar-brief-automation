# Advisor Engagement Understanding — Internal Counsel-Review Schedule

**Companion to:** `ADVISOR_ENGAGEMENT_UNDERSTANDING.md` v0.1 *(the artifact previously referred to as
the Advisor Working Agreement)* · **Date:** 2026-07-25

**Purpose.** Give counsel a section-by-section map of business intent, the language proposed, the legal
or compliance question it raises, whether counsel approval is required, and whether the provision may
belong in a different instrument (formal agreement · privacy notice · authorization form · retention
policy).

**Standing instruction to counsel.** VYNE has deliberately **not** characterized this document's legal
effect (§1 defers that to counsel). VYNE has also deliberately avoided claims of neutrality,
independence, conflict-freedom, or market completeness, and excludes all fee percentages and
firm-specific commercial terms from every advisor-facing document. Please preserve those constraints.

## Founder business positions — settled before counsel review (2026-07-25)

*These are instructions to counsel, not questions for counsel. Counsel is asked to implement them and to
confirm consistency with VYNE's firm-side agreements.*

### BP-1 · Compensation following withdrawal

- The advisor **may pause or withdraw without owing VYNE a fee.**
- **VYNE's compensation rights arise only under separate agreements with hiring firms.**
- If the advisor later joins a firm VYNE introduced, any compensation payable to VYNE is **governed by
  VYNE's agreement with that firm**, not by anything in this document.
- The advisor is **not responsible for calculating, paying, protecting, or enforcing** VYNE's
  compensation.
- **The Advisor Engagement Understanding must not impose:** an advisor fee · a placement tail ·
  liquidated damages · a fee-protection obligation · or any restriction against the advisor dealing
  directly with a firm.
- **Counsel must confirm consistency** with VYNE's firm agreements, prior-contact rules, procuring-cause
  language, and any firm-side tail — and flag any conflict between the firm-side terms and this
  advisor-side position.
- **Advisor-facing language now in §17:** *"Withdrawing from the engagement does not create a payment
  obligation for you. VYNE may have separate compensation rights under an agreement with a hiring firm if
  a placement later occurs."*

### BP-2 · Authorization upon withdrawal

- **Advisor-facing language now in §17:** *"When an advisor withdraws, all unused firm-specific
  authorizations are automatically revoked unless the advisor confirms otherwise in writing. An
  introduction already completed cannot be undone, but VYNE will not make additional disclosures or take
  further action without renewed authorization."*
- **Counsel review required**, and the provision must be **synchronized with the separate authorization
  artifact** when that artifact is drafted (revocation mechanics, notice to a firm already contacted,
  whether renewed authorization is a new instrument or a reinstatement).

### BP-3 · NDA sequencing

- **Decision: do not require a standalone NDA before every Advisor Orientation.**
- **Before Orientation, VYNE relies on:** a concise confidentiality and privacy notice · no-client-PII
  instructions · need-to-know access language · confirmation that no firm will be contacted · the
  Orientation acknowledgment.
- **A separate mutual NDA may be used when:** the advisor requests it · unusually sensitive information
  must be discussed before the Engagement Understanding · multiple owners, team members, sellers, buyers,
  or outside parties are involved · proprietary practice information must be exchanged early · counsel
  determines the engagement requires it.
- **Constraint:** do **not** describe the NDA as the foundation of ordinary confidentiality practices.
  Confidentiality controls apply from first contact and do not depend on a separate agreement — §7 now
  states this and offers an NDA on request.

### BP-4 · Electronic acknowledgment and version capture

**Operating requirement — acceptance must record:** advisor identity · **document version** · date and
time · acceptance method · **delivery of a copy to the advisor** · acknowledgment of material later
amendments where required.

**Counsel to determine** the exact electronic-consent, enforceability, retention, and state-law
requirements (E-SIGN/UETA and any state variations). *Version capture is also being added to the
engagement-record standards as an operating control, independent of the legal analysis.*

**Legend — Approval:** **R** = counsel approval required before production use · **A** = advisory review
(business language, low legal risk) · **M** = may need to move or be mirrored in another instrument.

## Section-by-section schedule

### §1 · What this document is — **R · M**

- **Business intent.** Set expectations in plain English without VYNE asserting the document's legal
  character.
- **Proposed language.** *"This document explains how the VYNE engagement works and what each party
  should expect… It is subject to the formal terms governing the engagement. Counsel will determine
  whether it operates as, accompanies, or is incorporated into the engagement agreement."*
- **Legal question.** **Does this document create binding obligations, and is that intended?** Should it
  (a) stand alone as the engagement agreement, (b) accompany a separate terms document, or (c) be
  incorporated by reference? Does describing obligations ("we will," "we ask") create enforceable duties
  or reliance regardless of intent? Is an integration/entire-agreement clause needed elsewhere?
- **May move to:** a formal engagement agreement, with this retained as a plain-English companion.

### §2 · The decision relationship — **A**

- **Intent.** Establish that the work is decision-oriented, and that "stay" or "wait" is a complete
  outcome.
- **Language.** *"We measure ourselves by whether your decision improved."*
- **Legal question.** Does *"help you make it well"* imply a professional standard of care or a
  fiduciary-like duty VYNE does not intend to assume? Is a standard-of-care provision needed elsewhere?
- **May move to:** standard-of-care / limitation provisions in the formal agreement.

### §3 · What you should expect from VYNE — **A · M**

- **Intent.** Concrete service expectations; a felt promise rather than a list of features.
- **Language.** Structured process · reasoning shown · honesty about unknowns · a written record you keep
  · no pressure.
- **Legal question.** Are these **service commitments** that create contractual obligations? Is *"a
  written record you keep"* a deliverable commitment that should be scoped (what, when, in what form)?
- **May move to:** scope-of-services in the formal agreement.

### §4 · What VYNE will do — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Describe the method, including the calibrated-conclusion standard.
- **Language.** *"the strongest conclusion the evidence supports — no stronger, and no weaker."*
- **Legal question.** Does this create a **measurable performance standard** VYNE could be held to?
  Should the deliverable be defined by process rather than by outcome quality? Confirm that "evaluate
  legitimate alternatives" does not imply a duty of exhaustive market search.
- **May move to:** scope of services; disclaimer of exhaustive-search duty.

### §5 · What VYNE will not do — **R**

- **Intent.** Professional-boundary and scope limitation, stated affirmatively and early.
- **Language.** No law/tax/accounting/valuation/compliance/filings; no transition mechanics; no firm
  contact without permission; **no claim to have reviewed every firm**; **explicit refusal to claim
  unbiased/neutral/conflict-free**; no "best firm."
- **Legal question.** Is this sufficient as an **unauthorized-practice** and scope disclaimer? Does the
  affirmative "we are compensated in a particular way… which is a different and more honest claim"
  create any risk in a disclosure context, or does it help? Any state-specific recruiter/placement
  licensing or disclosure obligations engaged?
- **May move to:** disclaimers section; consider mirroring on the public website.

### §6 · What VYNE asks from you — **A · M**

- **Intent.** Advisor responsibilities, framed as collaboration rather than obligation.
- **Language.** Accurate information · correct misunderstandings · notify changes · disclose prior firm
  contact · **no client PII** · involve other decision-makers · obtain specialist advice · **make the
  final decision** · separate written authorization before firm contact.
- **Legal question.** Should these be **advisor representations** with consequences for breach (e.g.
  VYNE's reliance on accuracy; effect of inaccurate information on conclusions)? Does *"obtain specialist
  advice where required"* adequately allocate that responsibility?
- **May move to:** client-representations and reliance provisions in the formal agreement.

### §7 · Confidentiality and information handling — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Truthful confidentiality — restricted access on a need-to-know basis, **not** an absolute
  "never leaves" promise.
- **Language.** Restricted to authorized VYNE personnel and approved providers/professional advisers with
  a legitimate need to know · nothing to a hiring firm without explicit firm-specific authorization ·
  legally required disclosure under counsel direction · no client PII requested or retained · advisor
  identity and advisor-provided information distinct from client PII, handled under this engagement's
  confidentiality and privacy controls.
- **Legal question.** **Is this an adequate confidentiality undertaking, and is it consistent with a
  separate NDA if one exists?** Does naming "approved providers" require a subprocessor/vendor list or
  flow-down obligations? Is the legally-required-disclosure carve-out adequately drafted (notice to
  advisor where permitted)? Does VYNE need a mutual or one-way NDA at Orientation, i.e. **before** this
  document?
- **May move to:** NDA and/or confidentiality article; privacy notice; vendor/subprocessor schedule.

### §8 · No client PII — **R · M**

- **Intent.** A hard operational rule the advisor can rely on, plus what happens if PII arrives anyway.
- **Language.** No client names, account numbers, statements, addresses, DOBs; aggregate description
  only; *"if client information reaches us inadvertently, we will tell you and we will not retain it."*
- **Legal question.** **Does the inadvertent-receipt commitment create an obligation VYNE can
  operationally meet** (detection, notification timing, deletion, backups)? Are Reg S-P, GLBA, state
  privacy statutes, or the advisor's own firm obligations engaged even by aggregate data? Is "we will tell
  you" an incident-notification commitment with a timing standard?
- **May move to:** privacy notice; information-security policy; incident-response procedure.

### §9 · How recommendations are formed — **A**

- **Intent.** Make the evidence discipline legible; establish that a conclusion is not final because it
  was written.
- **Language.** Verified / firm-claimed (attributed and dated) / assumed / judgment / unknown /
  alternatives / confidence. *"It becomes recommendation-ready only after we have worked through it with
  you."*
- **Legal question.** Does attributing and dating firm claims adequately protect against a
  **misrepresentation** claim if a firm's public claim proves false? Should a "we do not independently
  verify firm statements except as noted" qualifier be added?
- **May move to:** reliance/verification disclaimer.

### §10 · You control every introduction — **R · M**

- **Intent.** The Advisor Submission Standard, stated as an advisor protection.
- **Language.** Explicit, written, firm-specific authorization, signed each time; general willingness is
  not authorization; agreement with a recommendation is not authorization; one firm's authorization does
  not extend to another; **no same-day authorization request**.
- **Legal question.** **Does the separate authorization form need specified minimum contents** (firm
  name, information scope, date, duration, revocation)? Should authorizations **expire**? How is
  revocation handled after transmission? Does the no-same-day practice need to be a term or remain an
  internal standard? Interaction with **procuring-cause** and lead-ownership disputes.
- **May move to:** the firm-specific authorization form (its own artifact) — this section should
  **cross-reference**, not restate, its terms.

### §11 · Legitimate outcomes — **A**

- **Intent.** Make no-fee outcomes explicit and checkable. *"Several of these pay us nothing. We say so
  plainly here because you should be able to hold us to it."*
- **Legal question.** Any risk in inviting the advisor to "hold us to it" — does it create an enforceable
  standard? *(Business view: the sentence is doing important trust work; VYNE would prefer to keep it if
  counsel permits.)*

### §12 · How VYNE is compensated — **R**

- **Intent.** Required disclosure using approved language only; state the incentive and the control,
  never disinterest.
- **Language.** Approved disclosure verbatim; *"compensation plays no part in how we assess whether an
  option fits you… appears in no comparison, no ranking, and no reason we give you"*; heightened-risk
  situations documented internally before issuance.
- **Legal question.** **Is this disclosure adequate for applicable law and any firm agreements?** Are
  there jurisdictions or firm contracts requiring more specific disclosure (including amounts)? Does the
  reference to internal documentation of heightened-conflict situations create a **discoverable
  obligation** VYNE must then always be able to produce? Does any firm agreement prohibit or require
  disclosure of the arrangement's existence?
- **Confirmed excluded:** percentages, amounts, step-ups, payment timing, clawbacks, firm-specific terms.
- **May move to:** a standalone compensation-disclosure statement mirrored on the website.

### §13 · Graduated disclosure — **A**

- **Intent.** Advisor-controlled information depth; conclusions cannot outrun evidence.
- **Legal question.** Does presenting Level 3 as enabling "a specific recommendation" imply a
  **guaranteed deliverable** at that level? Recommend confirming the conditional framing is adequate.

### §14 · Conclusions, changes, and uncertainty — **R**

- **Intent.** Point-in-time judgment; evolution expected; material reversals explained.
- **Language.** *"describe our judgment at a point in time… remain subject to material new information or
  changed circumstances."*
- **Legal question.** **Is this an adequate limitation on reliance and on any duty to update?** Does VYNE
  have an ongoing obligation to notify the advisor if it later learns something material after the
  engagement concludes? Recommend an explicit no-duty-to-update provision if that is the intent.
- **May move to:** reliance limitation / no-duty-to-update clause.

### §15 · Professional boundaries and specialists — **R**

- **Intent.** Route regulated questions out; disclaim responsibility for specialists' work.
- **Language.** *"Those specialists remain responsible for their own advice and work."*
- **Legal question.** Does *"may help you find appropriate specialists"* create **referral liability** or
  an implied endorsement? Should referrals be documented as non-endorsements? Are referral fees
  contemplated — if so, disclosure obligations attach *(open item in Commercial Principles)*. Is the
  unauthorized-practice disclaimer sufficient?
- **May move to:** referral-disclosure provision; non-endorsement language.

### §16 · Advisor-facing Records and permitted use — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Advisor gets a usable, shareable document; VYNE retains its methodology; the Record does
  not reach hiring firms uncontrolled.
- **Language.** Confidential-use copy; named permitted recipients; VYNE retains methodology/frameworks/
  templates/analytical structure/internal records; **no public posting, commercial reproduction, or
  transmission to a hiring firm without written approval**; firms receive an approved excerpt or separate
  firm-facing summary.
- **Legal question.** **Is this an adequate IP and licence construction** — is the advisor's right a
  licence, and is its scope clear? **Is the hiring-firm restriction enforceable and practical?** What is
  the consequence of breach? Does the Record contain anything VYNE would not want produced in a dispute
  between advisor and firm? Should the Record carry a legend restating the permitted use?
- **May move to:** IP/licence article; a legend on the Record itself.

### §17 · Pausing, withdrawal, and conclusion — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Symmetrical, honest exit terms; outstanding authorizations resolved; closure documented.
- **Language.** Advisor may pause or withdraw at any time without explanation; VYNE may pause, narrow,
  decline, or conclude on stated grounds; outstanding firm authorizations identified and status
  confirmed; possible Decision Status and Closure Record; retention governed by policy.
- **Now also states (BP-1, BP-2):** withdrawal creates **no payment obligation for the advisor**; VYNE's
  compensation rights arise only under firm agreements; the advisor need not calculate, pay, protect, or
  enforce VYNE's compensation and is not restricted from dealing directly with a firm; **unused
  firm-specific authorizations are automatically revoked on withdrawal** unless the advisor confirms
  otherwise in writing, with completed introductions acknowledged as not undoable and no further
  disclosure without renewed authorization.
- **Legal question.** **Does VYNE's ability to decline or conclude need defined notice?** For a
  **pending** submission at withdrawal: how is a firm already contacted notified, and is renewed
  authorization a new instrument or a reinstatement? **Confirm the advisor-side position in BP-1 does not
  conflict with firm-side procuring-cause or tail terms** — if a firm's agreement would make VYNE's fee
  contingent on advisor conduct VYNE has just disclaimed any right to require, flag it. Any
  post-engagement obligations (confidentiality survival)?
- **May move to:** termination and survival provisions; **procuring-cause/tail terms belong in firm
  agreements, never here**; revocation mechanics to the authorization artifact.

### §18 · Records and retention — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Truthful placeholder: retention exists, is policy-governed, and legal holds override. **No
  period stated.**
- **Legal question.** Counsel to set: separate periods for advisor-facing Records vs. internal
  workpapers; email and CRM retention; authorization records; declined engagements; deletion requests;
  backups; legal-hold procedure. Are there **statutory or regulatory retention minimums** applicable to
  VYNE's activity?
- **May move to:** the retention and destruction policy (its own document, required before production
  use).

### §19 · Acknowledgment — **R · M**

- **Intent.** Record the seven understandings that matter most, in the document's own voice.
- **Language.** Seven statements + *"Acknowledgment: I understand and agree."* + name and date.
- **Legal question.** **Is "I understand and agree" an adequate manifestation of assent**, and does the
  document need signature blocks for both parties? **Is electronic acceptance valid and evidentially
  sufficient** (E-SIGN/UETA; audit trail; timestamp; copy delivery to the advisor; version capture — the
  advisor must be shown to have accepted *this* version)? Should the acknowledgment be separated from the
  explanatory text so assent attaches to defined terms?
- **May move to:** signature/e-signature execution block; version-capture requirement in the engagement
  record standards.

## Critique

- **Too legalistic?** No — arguably the opposite risk. The draft is plainly written and reads as a
  professional explanation. **The genuine exposure is that its warmth may create expectations counsel
  would want qualified** (e.g. "we measure ourselves by whether your decision improved" in §2, "the
  strongest conclusion the evidence supports" in §4). VYNE's business preference is to keep the voice and
  let counsel add precision **elsewhere** rather than hardening this document into boilerplate.
- **Too long?** At roughly 3 pages it is at the top of target. §§3–6 carry some conceptual overlap
  (expectations / will do / will not do / asks). **If a trim is needed, §3 could fold into §4**, but the
  founder's section order was explicit, so it is retained as specified.
- **Too protective of VYNE?** The balance is reasonable: §5 and §15 protect VYNE, while §6 asks
  something of the advisor — but §§7, 10, 11, 13, 16, 17 all constrain VYNE in the advisor's favour, and
  §17 gives the advisor an unconditional exit while VYNE's grounds are enumerated. **One asymmetry worth
  naming:** §17 is silent on whether a compensation entitlement survives withdrawal if a placement later
  occurs with a firm VYNE introduced. That silence currently favours the advisor's reading; counsel will
  likely want it addressed, and the founder should decide the business position before it is drafted.
- **Too vague to establish trust?** The specific, checkable commitments are the strength — firm-specific
  written authorization (§10), no-fee outcomes named (§11), Level 1 default (§13), named permitted
  recipients (§16), unconditional advisor withdrawal (§17). **Two places remain genuinely vague:**
  §7's "approved providers" (unnamed) and §18's retention (no period). Both are honest placeholders
  rather than evasions, but an advisor may read them as soft.
- **Provisions that belong elsewhere?** Flagged **M** throughout. The clearest candidates: §10's
  authorization mechanics → the authorization form; §18's retention → the retention policy; §8's
  incident handling → privacy/security documents; §16's licence construction → an IP article; §19's
  execution mechanics → a signature/e-signature block. **§17's compensation-survival question belongs in
  the formal agreement and in firm-side terms, not here.**
