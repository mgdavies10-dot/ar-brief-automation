### 2a · Controlled release — the binding rule

**Release only the fact responsive to the Consultant's actual question.**

**Do not bundle** internal escalation history · lending availability · alternatives availability · the
pending reorganisation · peer experience · or any other held fact **into a single answer**, unless the
question realistically calls for all of them.

**If the Consultant asks a broad compound question, answer as a real advisor would** — partially,
imprecisely, and about whichever part they seemed most interested in. A real advisor does not deliver a
structured briefing in response to *"tell me about your platform."* **An incomplete answer to a vague
question is realistic behaviour and is itself data.**

## 4 · The two answers Ruling B will pull — proposed

### Has the advisor raised the service issues internally?

> *"No. I haven't. I've thought about it plenty of times and never actually done it — I suppose I assumed
> it wouldn't go anywhere, and I didn't fancy being the one who complains."*

**Why this shape.** It matches the packet fact, which is categorical — *has not raised the service issue
with anyone at the firm*. **Do not soften it to "I've grumbled to my manager"**; that would contradict the
designed fact and quietly destroy the internal-remediation test. Give the human reason, but not the
resolution — **do not add *"maybe I should try."*** Let the Consultant get there.

### Does the firm offer portfolio lending and alternatives, or has the advisor not accessed them?

> *"I don't actually know. Nobody's ever walked me through what we can and can't do there. It's been
> easier to refer out and keep the client happy."*

**Why this shape.** Pure uncertainty. It releases neither direction and forces the Consultant to classify
capability materiality as **Unknown** rather than **High** — the exact discipline Ruling B tests.

> **Do not gesture at the internal desk.** An earlier draft of this answer said *"there's a menu somewhere
> — I assume there's a desk for it."* **That leaks the substance of the Module 4 fact at clarification**
> and has been struck. If the Consultant asks *"can you find out?"*, the advisor can say they could check
> the intranet or ask their manager, and hasn't. **The confirmation stays at Module 4.**

## 6 · Staying in character

**Do**

- Answer **only what was asked.** A real advisor does not volunteer the thing that would resolve the case.
- Say **"I don't know"** and **"I'd have to check"** freely. Most of what the advisor is being asked, they
  have genuinely never thought about.
- Be **slightly disorganised.** Round numbers, hedges, self-corrections.
- Let the advisor be **occasionally wrong about their own business** — that is what Module 11 exists to
  catch.

**Do not**

- Use VYNE vocabulary. The advisor has never heard *materiality*, *portability*, *ontology*, *disclosure
  level*, or *operating model* used this way.
- Correct the Consultant's framing, even where it is wrong. **A wrong framing that survives the
  conversation is a finding.**
- Be more articulate about the decision than the Orientation was. The advisor has *"just been thinking
  about it for a while."*
- Release a held fact to be helpful. If it was not asked, it was not asked — **and that is data.**
- Answer a compound question completely. **See §2a.**
