## 2 · What the advisor has told you

> - Employee model at a national firm · **14 years** at this firm, 21 in the industry
> - **≈ $240M AUM · ≈ $1.6M trailing production**
> - Team: one junior advisor, one client-service associate
> - Client base: predominantly retirees and pre-retirees; some small-business owners
> - Business lines: mostly fee-based advisory, some brokerage, modest insurance
> - Single metro market. Practice described as stable, growing slowly.
>
> **What prompted this**
> *"The service model here has deteriorated badly over the last two years. I spend time apologising to
> clients for things that used to just work. I've started wondering whether I'd be better somewhere
> else."*
>
> **What "better" means, in the advisor's words**
> *"Support that actually works, and honestly, better economics wouldn't hurt. But mostly I want to stop
> feeling like I'm compensating for my own firm."*
>
> **Scope selected in Orientation:** whether to stay or make a change · affiliation or operating model ·
> capabilities · economics.
>
> **Objectives grid:** *must-have* — service model / how I work with clients; specific capabilities my
> clients need. *Important* — better economics; my team's future. *Flexible* — ownership/equity; growth
> capacity. *Not yet sure* — succession.
>
> **Capabilities or limitations already affecting the practice:** *"Two things keep coming up — clients
> asking about lending against their portfolios, and a couple who want access to alternatives. I've been
> referring both out."*
>
> **Timing:** *"No deadline. I've just been thinking about it for a while."*
>
> **Decision participants:** spouse. **No one else at this stage.**
>
> **Concerns and constraints:** some unvested deferred compensation, amount and schedule not to hand · no

> discussed · has **not** spoken to any firm or recruiter.
>
> **Disclosure preference:** **Level 1** — high-level.
>
> **Five-year picture:** *"Roughly the same business, running more smoothly, maybe slightly larger."*

## 3 · Your task

1. **Conduct the Orientation** — you have the advisor's responses above. Note anything the intake
   *should* have asked and did not.
2. **Complete the Internal Orientation Review and Entry Decision** in full, per its own instructions.
3. **Produce a short report** covering:
   - the decisions apparently in scope, by Ontology reference — **named vs. implied-but-unnamed**;
   - disclosure level and what it supports;
   - readiness · fit · decision authority;
   - urgency, **verified or unverified**;
   - specialist dependencies visible at entry;
   - conflicts or prior contact;
   - no-client-PII check;
   - **your recommended entry outcome** and why;
   - **what public firm or market intelligence you would need** to take this further, and what you'd
     want verified;
   - **what you don't know that matters**;
   - anything in the artifacts that was unclear, redundant, or made you guess.
4. **Record your time** — minutes spent, split between reading, conducting, and documenting.

## 4 · Boundaries

- **No client PII.** Not requested, not recorded.
- **You are not authorized to contact any firm.** No outreach of any kind.
- **Do not produce a recommendation or a direction.** Entry review only.
- **Do not proceed past the entry decision.** Stop and report.
- **Public research is permitted** only to the extent needed to identify *what intelligence would be
  required later* — you are not conducting the assessment.
- VYNE is **hiring-firm-paid and success-based**. Never describe VYNE as unbiased, neutral, or
  conflict-free.

## 5 · How to report

Return: the completed entry record · your short report · your time log. **Nothing else.**

**Do not speculate about what the "right answer" is.** There isn't one yet, and guessing at it is the
specific failure this exercise is designed to detect.
