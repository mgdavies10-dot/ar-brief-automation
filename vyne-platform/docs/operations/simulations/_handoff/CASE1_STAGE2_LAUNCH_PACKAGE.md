## 2 · Capability claims must be established, not inherited

Your entry record carried *"clients asking about lending against their portfolios, and a couple who want
access to alternatives — I've been referring both out"* forward as two capability gaps.

**That statement establishes what the advisor has done. It does not establish what the platform offers.**
Referral behaviour is not evidence of platform absence.

Before lending or alternatives may be classified as platform deficiencies, establish as evidence:

- whether the firm **actually lacks** the capabilities;
- whether they **exist but have not been accessed**;
- **eligibility or process barriers**;
- **prior attempts** to use them;
- whether the **broader service concerns were raised internally**;
- **what response was received.**

**Both outcomes are equally possible and neither is expected.** Record what you find with its source and
verification status. **Until this is established, capability materiality cannot be classified** — the
correct entry is *Unknown*, which under the Materiality Worksheet is never shortened.

The same discipline applies to the service complaint: what has actually been raised, with whom, and what
came back.

### 2a · Neither answer settles the question

**Whichever way the capability question resolves, it does not by itself establish an outcome.**

- **If the capabilities prove absent**, that does not establish that a model change is correct. It makes
  capability one input among several.
- **If the capabilities prove available**, that does not establish that staying is correct. It changes the
  question from *possible capability absence* to **access · service execution · internal navigation ·
  eligibility · responsiveness · durability** — all of which remain open and all of which may still favour
  a different model.

**Either way, the L1 work must still consider:** the broader service deterioration · remediation history ·
practical accessibility · client impact · team impact · operating-model implications · risk · **and
whether an alternative model addresses the actual problem.**

> **A single resolved fact does not close a twelve-module scan.** If you find yourself treating one
> finding as decisive, that is drift — go back and complete the analysis it appeared to make unnecessary.

## 6 · Discovery provenance — required on every material fact

**For every material fact you surface during clarification or L1, record how it came to you.** One tag per
fact, on the worksheet line or in the clarification record:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **advisor volunteered** | offered without being asked for it |
| **consultant elicited** | you asked a question you originated |
| **founder-directed** | you asked because the entry determination or this brief required it |
| **artifact-prompted** | you asked because a template, module prompt, or checklist put the question in front of you |
| **document or specialist supplied** | it came from a document or a named professional, not from the advisor's recollection |

**This is not bureaucracy.** It separates what the process discovered from what the process was told to
ask, and those are different measures of whether the method works. A fact tagged *founder-directed* is
still a good fact — it is simply not evidence that the scan would have found it unaided.

**Where a fact arrives in answer to a compound question, tag the question, not your intent.** If you asked
three things at once and got one answer, record that.
