# L1 Assessment Guide

**Artifact:** 4A · Operating Artifact Sprint · **Version:** v0.2 · **Date:** 2026-07-25
**Status:** **APPROVED as a controlled operating draft** (founder, 2026-07-25; revisions applied) · **Audience:** the VYNE consultant conducting the assessment
**Reads from (frozen):** Practice Transition Assessment (modules 1–12) · Assessment Architecture ·
Advisor Decision Ontology · Advisor Orientation + Internal Orientation Review.

> ## Governing premise
> **Every assessment area is acknowledged. Only material areas are deeply assessed.**
>
> ## Governing rule
> **L1 organizes what is known, unknown, assumed, and decision-relevant. It does not produce a
> directional recommendation.**

**What the L1 assessment is:** confidential · consultant-led · a **light scan across all twelve PTA
areas** · informed by the Orientation and the Internal Orientation Review · designed to determine
**materiality, evidence gaps, scope, and next steps.**

**What it is not:** a second comprehensive advisor questionnaire · a firm-comparison exercise · a scoring
model · an automated recommendation · a portability calculator · a stay-or-move test · a commercial
qualification exercise · a request for client-level information · a substitute for specialist advice.

---

## 1 · Opening — and reusing what the advisor already told us

**Do not re-ask what the Orientation already answered.** Re-asking signals that the advisor's time was
wasted and that nobody read it. Re-confirm only where: confirmation is genuinely necessary · the
information looks inconsistent · circumstances have changed · or the advisor wants to revisit it.

### Suggested opening — say this in your own words

> "Before we start, a few things about how this works.
>
> I've read your Orientation, so I'm not going to make you repeat it. I'll reference what you told us,
> and if I've misunderstood something — or something has changed — please stop me and correct it. That's
> genuinely useful, not an interruption.
>
> This first pass is deliberately high-level. We touch every area of your business once, lightly, because
> the point right now isn't to analyze everything — it's to work out **what actually matters** to your
> decision before we ask you for anything more. Most areas won't need to go further.
>
> Whether we go deeper than this level is **your** decision, made when a specific question requires it.
> Not because we reached a later stage.
>
> Two standing rules: **please don't give me any client-identifying information** — I don't need it and
> won't keep it. And nothing today authorizes us to contact any firm about you. That's always a separate,
> written decision naming the specific firm."

## 1a · Duration — launch operating targets

> **Target advisor-facing L1 scan: 45–60 minutes.**
>
> **At 75 minutes, the consultant must pause the scan**, preserve what has been learned, identify
> unfinished areas, and **schedule a focused follow-up rather than rush the remaining modules.**
>
> **Target internal worksheet completion and handoff: 20–30 minutes.**
>
> **Initial total consultant-time benchmark: 75–90 minutes per L1 assessment.**

**These are launch operating targets, not permanent doctrine.** They exist to keep a twelve-module scan
from becoming an interrogation, and they are expected to change.

**After the first three simulated or real engagements, review:** advisor conversation time · internal
documentation time · which modules consistently take longest · which questions produce little decision
value · how often the shortened immaterial-module path is used.

**Any adjustment is recorded as an operating-version change** — not as an amendment to frozen
methodology, unless a genuine methodological defect appears.

## 2 · Sequence — compensation-separated

Run the modules in this order. This is the **compensation-separated sequence** (Assessment Architecture
§7): objectives, current practice, capability requirements, and material risks are documented **before
firm-specific options are ranked or recommended.**

```
  FIRST:  1 Objectives → 2 Current practice → 4 Capability compatibility → 10 Risk
  THEN:   3 Book portability → 5 Transition economics → 6 Enterprise continuity & value
          → 7 Client impact → 8 Team impact → 9 Operating-model impact
          → 11 Due diligence & verification
  LAST:   12 Integrated conclusion  (materiality map + scope — NOT a direction)
```

> **Firm-specific compensation information is not an assessment input.** It appears in **no** materiality
> classification, **no** confidence assessment, **no** fit rationale, and **no** recommendation logic. If
> you know it, you set it aside here. *(Governing principle: compensation may affect VYNE's commercial
> operations; it must never improve an option's advisor-fit assessment.)*

## 3 · The twelve-module light scan

*Each module: three to five prompts. If a module is plainly immaterial, acknowledge it, classify it, and
move on — that is the design working, not a gap.*

### Module 1 · Advisor objectives *(→ Ontology **A**)*

**Purpose.** Establish what the advisor is actually trying to decide, and the yardstick every option will
later be measured against. Nothing downstream is meaningful without this.

**Already available from Orientation:** §1 (what prompted this), §2 (scope selections), §4 (objectives
grid, "what does better mean," five-year picture, decision participants).

**L1 prompts**
1. "Your Orientation says the decision you're weighing is ___. Is that still how you'd frame it — or has
   it shifted?"
2. "You wrote that 'better' means ___. Say more about that — what would actually be different?"
3. "Of the must-haves you marked, which one would you not trade away for anything else?"
4. "What's your honest timing, as opposed to the timing you feel pressure about?"
5. "Who else needs to be comfortable with this decision?"

**Listen for:** an objective that contradicts the stated destination · a "must-have" that is really a
preference (or the reverse) · an unnamed decision underneath the named one · timing driven by someone
else's calendar.

**Can conclude at L1:** the decision(s) genuinely in scope; the advisor's objectives and priorities as
**stated**; decision participants; the timing picture.

**Defer to L2/L3:** nothing — **objectives are fully in scope at L1** and are the one area that must be
complete before anything else can be assessed.

**Specialist triggers:** none typically.

**No-PII guardrail:** personal and family detail only as the advisor volunteers it, and only to the depth
the decision requires.

> **Do not assume the objective is a move.** The advisor may have selected "move" language while
> describing an ownership, succession, or capability problem.

### Module 2 · Current-practice profile *(→ Ontology **A/D** baseline)*

**Purpose.** The factual baseline every option is compared against. Aggregate only.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (channel, role, tenure, AUM and production bands, team, client
types, business lines, footprint, growth direction).

**L1 prompts**
1. "Anything in the profile you gave us that needs correcting or updating?"
2. "How is your revenue actually composed — roughly, by type?" *(advisory / brokerage / planning fees /
   insurance / lending / other)*
3. "How concentrated is the practice — roughly what share sits with your largest relationships?"
   *(bands, no names)*
4. "What's grown or shrunk in the last two or three years?"

**Listen for:** revenue composition that constrains portability · concentration the advisor hasn't
framed as risk · a structure that differs from how the advisor describes it.

**Can conclude at L1:** scale, shape, channel, tenure, team, client types, business lines, geography,
**broad** revenue structure.

**Defer to L2/L3:** segment-level revenue detail · account-type composition · household-level structure ·
anything requiring records.

**Specialist triggers:** none typically.

**No-PII guardrail:** **no client names, account information, statements, or household-level data.**
Concentration is expressed as a band, never as "my top client is ___."

### Module 4 · Capability compatibility *(→ Ontology **B** and **C**)*

**Purpose.** Establish what the business actually needs before any firm or model is considered.
**Capability requirements drive the model; the model drives the firm.**

**Already available:** Orientation §2 (capabilities scope), §5 (capabilities/services/limitations already
affecting the practice), §3 (client types, business lines).

**L1 prompts**
1. "Which capabilities are genuinely central to how you serve clients today?"
2. "Where does your current platform get in your way — or where do you work around it?"
3. "What have you had to turn away, refer out, or apologize for?"
4. "What's valuable but not essential — nice to have rather than must have?"
5. "Anything you're not sure whether you need?"

**Listen for:** a capability gap that is really the whole decision · a "must-have" the advisor rarely uses
· a workaround so habitual the advisor no longer sees it as a constraint.

**Can conclude at L1:** must-have vs. valued-but-not-essential capabilities; current gaps; areas of
genuine uncertainty; **the capability profile a viable option must satisfy.**

**Defer to L2/L3:** whether a *specific* platform satisfies a *specific* requirement; build/buy/outsource
analysis; cost of replacement.

**Specialist triggers:** trust, tax, lending, or insurance capabilities where the underlying question is
regulatory rather than commercial.

**No-PII guardrail:** describe client *needs* by type, never by client.

> **Do not compare specific firms here** unless firm comparison is separately in scope later. At L1 the
> output is a **requirement set**, not a shortlist.

### Module 10 · Risk *(→ Ontology **K**)*

**Purpose.** Issue-spot early, so no conclusion is later built on an unresolved legal or regulatory
question. **Issue-spotting only.**

**Already available:** Orientation §5 (covenants, deferred comp, prior contact, open legal/tax/regulatory
questions), Internal Orientation Review §5–§7.

**L1 prompts**
1. "What agreements are you subject to — non-solicit, non-compete, garden leave, Protocol status? Do you
   have copies?"
2. "Has anyone at your firm indicated they'd contest a departure?"
3. "Who have you already spoken with — any firm or recruiter?"
4. "Any licensing, registration, or regulatory matters we should know about — yours or your team's?"
5. "Any deadline that is genuinely fixed, and how do you know?"

**Listen for:** an advisor describing their contract from memory · a covenant question treated as settled
that isn't · prior contact the advisor considers irrelevant · a "deadline" that is actually a preference.

**Can conclude at L1:** which risks exist and which are material to the decision; which are legal
dependencies requiring counsel; verified vs. unverified deadlines.

**Defer to L2/L3:** anything requiring the actual documents.

**Specialist triggers — expect these here.** **Counsel** for any covenant, solicitation, Protocol, or
employment-terms question about *this advisor's* situation. **Compliance** for registration or
supervisory matters. **Tax/CPA** where treatment affects viability.

**No-PII guardrail:** no client data; also avoid recording employees' personal details.

> **Do not interpret the advisor's contract**, and do not give legal, tax, valuation, or compliance
> advice. Name the issue, name who must answer it, and move on.

*The remaining modules follow, after the four above are documented.*

### Module 3 · Book portability *(→ Ontology **G**)*

**Purpose.** Understand what would realistically transfer — and separate what *can* move from what *will
stay and keep producing*.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (client types, business lines), §5 (portability concerns, product
or platform constraints).

**L1 prompts**
1. "How did these relationships originate — you, the firm, an inheritance, a referral network?"
2. "How concentrated is revenue in your largest relationships?" *(bands)*
3. "What sits in products, programs, or account types that may not travel?"
4. "Have you or anyone on your team moved before? What actually happened?"
5. "How much does the firm's brand, platform, or your team do the work of the relationship?"

**Listen for:** relationships that belong to the institution rather than the advisor · a confident
portability belief with no basis · concentration that makes the average irrelevant.

**Can conclude at L1:** concentration; relationship characteristics; **broad** product and account
constraints; prior-move experience; brand/platform/team dependency; **the major unknowns.**

**Defer to L2/L3:** any estimate. **A range requires L2; a specific figure requires L3.**

**Specialist triggers:** counsel where portability turns on solicitation rules.

**No-PII guardrail:** never client names. Concentration and origination described in aggregate.

> **Reinforce, in the advisor's hearing:** *"Asset portability is not the same as revenue portability."*
> What can move is not what will stay and keep producing.
>
> **Do not produce a portability percentage at L1.** Not a number, not a range, not a "most of it."

### Module 5 · Transition economics *(→ Ontology **F**)*

**Purpose.** Identify the **categories** of economic consequence — not compute them.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (production band), §4 (economics priority), §5 (deferred comp).

**L1 prompts**
1. "What's unvested or at risk if you left — roughly, and roughly when does it vest?"
2. "What would it cost you to move, beyond the obvious?"
3. "How would your payout or revenue structure change under the models you're considering?"
4. "What does the long-term picture need to look like for this to be worth doing?"
5. "Are there ownership or equity consequences either way?"

**Listen for:** a headline transition number doing all the work · forfeiture the advisor hasn't
quantified · long-term economics never modelled against near-term cash.

**Can conclude at L1:** the **categories** in play — deferred compensation, forfeiture exposure,
transition costs, broad revenue/payout considerations, long-term economics, ownership implications — and
which are material.

**Defer to L2/L3:** any net figure, any range, any scenario.

**Specialist triggers:** **CPA/tax counsel** for treatment of forfeiture, notes, or deal structure.

**No-PII guardrail:** no client-level revenue attribution.

> **Do not calculate personal tax outcomes. Do not present a transition deal as the decision. Do not
> request or reference VYNE's fee information** — it is not an input here or anywhere in the assessment.

### Module 6 · Enterprise continuity and value *(→ Ontology **D**, **E**, **I**, **J**)*

**Purpose.** Establish what the advisor owns, what they want to own, and whether this is a growth,
value-building, or exit decision.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (role, growth direction), §4 (ownership/equity, succession
priorities), §5 (pending succession or retirement).

**L1 prompts**
1. "What do you own today — and what would you want to own?"
2. "How do you think about succession, and on what horizon?"
3. "Is building enterprise value a live goal, or is income the priority?"
4. "Any growth plans — recruiting, acquiring, merging?"
5. "What happens to this business if something happens to you?"

**Listen for:** a move question that is actually a succession question · an ownership goal inconsistent
with the channel being considered · continuity risk nobody has addressed.

**Can conclude at L1:** ownership goals; succession posture; enterprise-value priorities; growth plans;
continuity risks; acquisition/sale/merger interest.

**Defer to L2/L3:** value drivers analysis; any valuation range; deal structure.

**Specialist triggers:** **valuation specialist** for any formal value question; **M&A counsel** for
structure; **counsel** for continuity documents.

**No-PII guardrail:** none specific beyond the standing rule.

> **Do not issue a formal valuation**, and do not offer a value range at L1. *"How practices like yours
> are generally valued"* is education; *"what yours is worth"* is L2/L3 with a specialist.

### Module 7 · Client impact *(→ Ontology **G**)*

**Purpose.** Treat client impact as a **decision criterion**, not merely a transition-management task.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (client types), §5 (capabilities/limitations affecting clients).

**L1 prompts**
1. "What would your clients actually experience if something changed?"
2. "Which client relationships would be most sensitive to a change, and why?" *(by type, not name)*
3. "What in your service model depends on your current firm?"
4. "How complex would the communication be — and who would need to hear it first?"

**Listen for:** service dependencies the advisor treats as their own but that belong to the firm · client
concerns the advisor is avoiding naming · concentration that turns a general risk into a specific one.

**Can conclude at L1:** client-service dependencies; likely client concerns; concentration risks;
communication complexity; high-level service-model effects.

**Defer to L2/L3:** segment-level impact analysis; communication planning.

**Specialist triggers:** counsel where client communication intersects solicitation rules.

**No-PII guardrail:** **client types and segments only — never identities or client-level data.** If the
advisor starts naming clients, redirect: *"describe the type rather than the person."*

### Module 8 · Team impact *(→ Ontology **H**)*

**Purpose.** Establish who moves, who decides, and what the team constrains.

**Already available:** Orientation §3 (team size and structure), §4 (team's future, decision
participants), §5 (team dependencies).

**L1 prompts**
1. "Walk me through the team — roles, not names, unless you want to."
2. "Who would come with you, who is uncertain, and who decides for themselves?"
3. "Is anyone on the team a partner, owner, or successor?"
4. "Are there compensation or ownership issues that would surface?"
5. "Who should be part of a future conversation before we reach a recommendation?"

**Listen for:** a team member whose consent is effectively required · retention risk the advisor
underestimates · an ownership expectation that has never been documented.

**Can conclude at L1:** team roles; decision authority; retention dependencies; high-level compensation
or ownership issues; who should participate in consultation.

**Defer to L2/L3:** individual economics; retention structures; team agreements.

**Specialist triggers:** **counsel** where a team member has their own covenants or ownership claim;
employment counsel where relevant.

**No-PII guardrail:** **do not collect unnecessary employee personal information.** Roles and
decision-authority only.

### Module 9 · Operating-model impact *(→ Ontology **C** and **M**)*

**Purpose.** Understand how much business the advisor actually wants to run.

**Already available:** Orientation §2 (affiliation/operating-model scope), §4 (autonomy and control,
simplicity), §5 (frustrations).

**L1 prompts**
1. "How much control do you actually want — and over what specifically?"
2. "How do you feel about being responsible for compliance, technology, staffing, and vendors?"
3. "What support do you rely on today that you'd rather not rebuild?"
4. "Do you want to run a business, or serve clients with less friction? Both are legitimate answers."
5. "How much disruption can the practice absorb?"

**Listen for:** independence appetite stated but not felt · a desire for control that is really a desire
for a specific capability · underestimation of operational load.

**Can conclude at L1:** desired control; supervision preferences; support needs; tolerance for
operational responsibility; independence appetite; broad implementation complexity.

**Defer to L2/L3:** operating-model design; outsourcing decisions; implementation sequencing.

**Specialist triggers:** **compliance** for supervisory structure; implementation providers for build
questions.

**No-PII guardrail:** none specific.

> **Do not assume independence is superior.** Employee, supported, and independent models are all
> legitimate destinations, and the answer follows the advisor's objectives and capability requirements —
> not a preference of ours.

### Module 11 · Due diligence and verification *(→ Ontology **L**)*

**Purpose.** Establish **what is actually established** — and what merely sounds established.

**Already available:** everything gathered so far, plus Internal Orientation Review §6.

**L1 prompts**
1. "Which of the things we've discussed do you have documents for?"
2. "Which are you working from memory or from what you were told?"
3. "Has anyone at a firm made claims we'd want to check?"
4. "Is there anything you've heard that contradicts something else you've heard?"

**Listen for:** an advisor-reported "fact" that is a firm's claim repeated · contradictions the advisor has
absorbed without noticing · confidence unsupported by any source.

**Can conclude at L1:** which material statements are **verified**, which are **advisor-reported**, which
remain **assumptions**; what documents or sources may eventually be needed; which firm claims would
require verification; any contradictions.

**Defer to L2/L3:** the verification work itself.

**Specialist triggers:** counsel for document review; compliance for regulatory verification.

**No-PII guardrail:** documents requested must never include client-identifying material; account
statements are not an L1 or L2 request.

> **Do not turn L1 into full diligence.** The output is a **verification map**, not verified facts.

### Module 12 · Integrated conclusion *(→ **★** Strategic Outcome — at L1, scope only)*

**Purpose.** Convert the scan into a **materiality map and a scope recommendation.** Nothing more.

**L1 may produce only:**
decisions in scope · material areas · known facts · assumptions · unknowns · evidence gaps · specialist
dependencies · recommended assessment scope · recommended disclosure progression · the purpose of the
next consultation.

> ### L1 must NOT produce
> a directional recommendation · a "lean" toward a firm or model · a stay-or-move score · a portability
> percentage · a best-fit firm · a Level 2 or Level 3 conclusion.
>
> **The strongest conclusion available at L1 is Level 1 — Decision-Support.**

### The scope-setting boundary

> **L1 may revise the questions and decisions in scope. It may not rank the resulting outcomes or state
> which direction appears preferable.**

**Permitted:** *"This is not only a firm-change question. Succession, ownership, and covenant risk must
also be evaluated."*

**Prohibited:** *"Because succession is the real issue, moving is probably not the answer."*

> **A scope change identifies what must be assessed. A recommendation tells the advisor what to do.**

Reframing what is genuinely in scope is the most valuable thing an L1 scan does — an advisor who came in
asking one question often needs three evaluated. But the moment a scope change acquires a direction
("…so probably X"), it has stopped being scope and become an untested recommendation.

**Drift check before you close.** Read what you have written and ask: *does any sentence here tell the
advisor which way to go?* If yes, it does not belong at L1. Reframe it as **"what we would need to know
in order to conclude."**

## 4 · Operating rules

1. **Do not re-ask** what the Orientation answered — unless confirmation is necessary, information looks
   inconsistent, circumstances changed, or the advisor asks to revisit it.
2. **This should feel like a structured professional conversation, not an interrogation.** The prompts are
   preparation, not a script. Follow the advisor where the material is.
3. **The worksheet is completed by VYNE, not handed to the advisor as a form.**
4. **Record only** what could materially affect: the decision · assessment scope · confidence · a
   specialist dependency · VYNE's accountability.
5. **Do not record:** sales-readiness judgments · commercial attractiveness · likelihood to move ·
   personality speculation · irrelevant family details · closing probability · recruiter-style lead
   scores.
6. **No client PII.**
7. **Any client PII the advisor volunteers is not copied into the worksheet and is not retained.**
8. **A specific firm may be noted if the advisor raises it** — but L1 does not become a firm-ranking
   exercise.
9. **Record any known prior firm or recruiter contact.**
10. **Record anticipated gate triggers, but do not perform the Higher-Conflict Decision Gate** merely
    because one may arise later. The gate applies at the **recommendation lifecycle stage** under the
    frozen method.
