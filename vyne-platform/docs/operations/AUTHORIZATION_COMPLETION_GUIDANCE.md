## 2 · Purpose and precondition matrix

| | **A · Anonymous DD** | **B · Identified DD** | **C · Introduction** | **D · Submission** | **E · Term advocacy** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Record required** | **no** | **no** | **yes** | **yes** | follows the underlying purpose |
| **Gate required** | no *(unless triggered by other facts)* | no *(same)* | **Not required / Not triggered / Cleared** | **same** | same |
| **Cooling** | n/a unless triggered Gate | same | **1 business day if Gate triggered** | **same** | same |
| **Advisor identity disclosed** | **no** | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| **Recommendation implied** | **no** | **no** | **no** | **no** | **no** |
| **Typical information scope** | none advisor-specific | limited profile | profile + contact | authorized profile for terms | as already authorized |
| **May VYNE bind the advisor** | **no** | **no** | **no** | **no** | **no** |

> **The critical boundary:** A and B exist so VYNE can **gather firm-specific evidence before a
> recommendation is possible.** C and D exist only **after** the decision has narrowed. **Moving from
> due diligence to submission is a scope change requiring a new authorization** (§14) — it is never a
> continuation of the same instrument.

## 3 · Permission and information-scope matrix

| Permission *(§5)* | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contact the firm | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Identify the advisor | ✘ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Provide advisor contact information | ✘ | ○ | ✔ | ✔ | ○ |
| Request general information | ✔ | ✔ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Request advisor-specific terms | ✘ | ○ | ○ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Arrange a meeting | ✘ | ✘ | ✔ | ○ | ○ |
| Follow up within purpose | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ |
| Communicate approved questions/preferences | ✘ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ✔ |
| Request clarification or revised terms | ✘ | ✘ | ✘ | ○ | ✔ |

**✔** typical · **○** only if separately selected · **✘** incompatible with the purpose

**Information treatment.** Every category in §6 carries four states — **not authorized · exact · range
only · redacted/aggregated.** *Range* and *redacted* exist because an advisor will often authorize
scale without authorizing precision, and **that distinction is the difference between a useful inquiry
and an unnecessary disclosure.**

## 4 · Completion guidance

1. **Authorization vs. recommendation.** Authorization permits specified action. It does **not** prove
   VYNE recommends the firm, and it does **not** prove the advisor selected it.
2. **Authorization vs. advisor decision.** The advisor may authorize due diligence **while undecided**,
   may authorize a meeting **without committing to transition**, and may revoke.
3. **Authorization vs. legal agency.** VYNE may communicate and advocate within scope. **VYNE cannot
   bind the advisor.**
4. **One firm, one instrument.** No blanket authority · no unlisted affiliates · no informal carryover.
5. **Minimum necessary.** Permission is a **ceiling**, not a requirement to disclose everything
   permitted.
6. **Client PII.** Never authorized · never retained or transmitted through this process.
7. **Scope expansion.** **Stop.** Issue an amended or new version. Obtain new advisor acceptance. **Then**
   act.
8. **Revocation.** Accept any clear instruction · confirm in writing · stop future action · **do not
   promise recovery** of information already shared.
9. **Expiration.** No automatic renewal · no follow-up after expiration without renewed authority.
10. **Compensation.** Approved general disclosure only · no exact VYNE terms · **the advisor owes no fee
    merely by signing.**

## 5 · Four worked examples

### 5.1 · Anonymous due-diligence inquiry — no Record required

**Situation.** The advisor is considering supported independence. **No firm recommendation exists yet**,
and VYNE cannot responsibly reach one without knowing what a specific platform actually offers on
transition support, custody, and service.

**Authorization:** purpose **A** only. Firm: one named platform. **Advisor identity not authorized.**
Information authorized: **none advisor-specific** — VYNE requests general program-level information.
Recipients: firm recruiting or transition personnel. Expiration: 60 days.

**§4 basis — Limited Due-Diligence.** *Assessment basis:* capability requirements identified in the L1
scan. *Information required:* transition support model, custody arrangements, service structure, and
platform fee structure at program level. *Why firm contact is needed:* the platform's public materials
do not establish these, and the comparison cannot be completed without them.

**Explicitly recorded:** *no directional recommendation is implied*, and **the information obtained may
make this option look better or worse.**

**Gate:** not applicable — no recommendation exists to gate. **Cooling:** none.

> **This is the example that justifies the founder's first correction.** Under my original outline —
> requiring a Record before any authorization — **this inquiry could not have happened**, and VYNE would
> have been unable to obtain the evidence needed to *reach* a recommendation.

### 5.2 · Identified submission after a triggered Gate

**Situation.** Full Recommendation Record issued. Gate **triggered** *(advisor was also weighing
staying)* and **cleared**. One full business day has passed since Gate completion.

**Authorization:** purposes **C · D · E**. Firm: one named employee firm.

**Permissions:** contact · identify the advisor · provide contact information · request advisor-specific
terms · arrange a meeting · follow up · communicate approved questions · request clarification or revised
terms.

**Information:** name **exact** · contact **exact** · title and history **exact** · licensing **exact** ·
current affiliation **exact** · high-level practice profile **exact** · AUM **range only** · trailing
production **range only** · team size and roles **exact** · objectives and capability requirements
**exact**. **Client demographics: not authorized.** No documents authorized.

**§4 basis — Introduction/Submission.** Record v1.0 · **Gate: Cleared** · cooling **satisfied** ·
advisor had review opportunity.

**§3E advocacy selected** — VYNE may convey the advisor's stated preference on transition timing and
request revised terms. **VYNE may not accept or reject anything.**

**Note the AUM and production treatment:** the advisor authorized **range only**. VYNE requests terms
using ranges. **A firm asking for exact figures does not change the authorization** — that would be a
§14 scope change.

### 5.3 · Scope expansion refused pending new authorization

**Situation.** Initial authorization allowed identity and high-level profile. **The firm asks for
detailed revenue mix and a redacted team document.**

**Neither was authorized.** §6 shows revenue mix **not authorized**; no document was listed by title and
version.

**What VYNE does:**

1. **Does not share them.** *(10B §6: "No action occurs while an expansion is awaiting authorization.")*
2. Tells the firm the information is not currently authorized — **without** characterizing the advisor's
   position or implying reluctance.
3. Presents an **amended authorization (v2)** to the advisor, describing exactly what the firm requested,
   what it would be used for, and the treatment options — *exact, range only, or redacted.*
4. **The advisor may approve, narrow, or decline.** Declining is a complete answer and requires no
   justification.
5. If approved, the advisor accepts **v2 specifically** — v1's acceptance does not carry forward.

> **This is where the artifact does its real work.** The path of least resistance is to send the document
> and mention it later. **§14 makes that a violation rather than an oversight.**

### 5.4 · Revocation

**Situation.** The advisor revokes **by phone**, mid-engagement, after one introduction has already been
completed.

**What VYNE does:**

1. **Accepts the verbal instruction** — §15 accepts *any clear written or verbal instruction*.
2. **Documents receipt** (date, time, method, instruction) and **confirms in writing.**
3. **Stops all future contact, disclosure, requests, and follow-up.** Outstanding follow-up is cancelled.
4. **Identifies what was already disclosed** and to whom.
5. **Asks the firm not to further use or distribute the information** where practical — **and does not
   promise deletion or recovery.**
6. **Records honestly** that the completed introduction **cannot be undone.**
7. **All other unused authorizations across the engagement are automatically revoked** *(engagement
   withdrawal rule)* unless the advisor expressly reconfirms them.
8. **The authorization stays revoked** — it cannot be revived informally; a new version is required.

> **What VYNE must not write:** *"the firm has deleted the information."* **Unless confirmed, that is a
> claim VYNE cannot support** — and 10B §9 bars it explicitly.

## 6 · Critique

- **Is one page realistic?** **For 10A, mostly — but not reliably.** §6's information table alone is
  fifteen rows with four treatment states. A due-diligence authorization *(5.1)* fits comfortably; a full
  submission with advocacy *(5.2)* will run to two. **My assessment: treat one page as the target for
  due-diligence instruments and accept two for submission**, rather than compressing the information
  table, which is the substance of the control.
- **Advisor friction.** **This is the most legitimate concern in the whole sprint.** An advisor who wants
  a meeting arranged now faces a named-firm instrument, granular permissions, a fifteen-row information
  table, recipient scope, an expiration date, and eleven acknowledgments. **A recruiter would send an
  email.** The defence is that every one of those fields exists because its absence is how advisors get
  submitted to firms they did not choose, on information they did not approve — **but the friction is
  real and will be felt at exactly the moment the advisor is most impatient.**
- **False sense of control after information reaches a firm.** §8 and §15 are honest — VYNE *requests*
  limitation and **cannot guarantee** handling, and cannot recall what was sent. **Residual risk:** an
  advisor may read granular permissions as implying VYNE controls downstream use. **The honesty is in
  the artifact; whether it registers is a delivery question**, and it should be said aloud, not left to
  the footer.
- **Recipient-scope complexity.** §8's categories are conceptually right and **practically fuzzy** —
  "legal, compliance, or due-diligence personnel reasonably necessary" is a judgment VYNE cannot verify
  at the firm. **It is a request, not a control**, and the artifact says so.
- **Expiry burden.** Sixty days will expire mid-process in slower engagements, generating renewals.
  **10B §8 bars auto-renewal deliberately** — the burden *is* the control. **Watch in pilots whether
  renewals cluster**, which would suggest 60 days is wrong for this work rather than that advisors are
  slow.
- **Founder administration burden.** 10B has twelve sections and a per-disclosure activity log. For a
  submission with several exchanges, that is the heaviest ongoing record in the system. **Mitigation:**
  most rows are short and reference rather than duplicate. **Honest risk:** the activity log is the first
  thing that will be filled in retrospectively, and a retrospective log is much weaker evidence than a
  contemporaneous one.
- **Is 60 days appropriate?** **Defensible as a starting point, arbitrary in origin.** It is long enough
  for a normal inquiry-and-response cycle and short enough that stale authority does not accumulate.
  **It should be measured, not defended** — §13 already marks it provisional.
- **Risk of authorization as sales pressure.** The genuine danger: *"we just need you to sign this so we
  can move forward."* **§16 is the control**, and the anti-workaround clause is the important half —
  **VYNE may not suggest advisor initiation as a route around cooling.** **Residual risk:** §16 governs
  VYNE's conduct, and a self-administered conduct rule has the same limitation as the Gate.
- **Premium or legalistic?** **This is the most legalistic artifact in the system, and appropriately
  so** — it is the instrument through which an advisor's identity and business information leave VYNE's
  control. **The premium quality has to come from how it is presented**, not from removing fields.
  Delivered well, it reads as *"this is how seriously we take your name"*; delivered badly, it reads as
  paperwork.

## 7 · Recommendation

**Approve with revisions.** The artifact resolves the two corrections the founder identified. **Limited
due-diligence authorization can now exist before any Record** — without it, VYNE could not obtain
firm-specific evidence and could never responsibly *reach* a recommendation. **Term advocacy is
permitted when selected**, while the actual boundary is drawn where it belongs: **communicate, ask,
clarify, and advocate — but never accept, reject, execute, or bind.** Everything else holds: one firm per
instrument, granular permissions, client PII unwaivable, affiliates excluded unless named, automatic
expiry, scope expansion requiring fresh acceptance, revocation honest about what cannot be undone, and
triggered-Gate cooling enforced against advisor urgency.

**Three revisions I would want:**

1. **Split the page target by purpose** — one page for due-diligence instruments, two accepted for
   submission with advocacy. Compressing §6's information table to hit one page would damage the control
   that matters most.
2. **Add a short advisor-facing plain-language preamble** — three or four sentences explaining *why*
   the instrument is granular, delivered before the fields. The friction is unavoidable; **the
   explanation is what turns it from bureaucracy into evidence of seriousness**, and right now the
   document opens with identity fields.
3. **Decide whether the activity log must be contemporaneous.** 10B §4 does not say when it is completed.
   A retrospective log is materially weaker evidence, and a stated norm — *logged before the next
   disclosure under the same authorization* — would close it without adding fields.

**One item for founder attention:** worked example **5.3** is the one to read. It is the moment the
system costs VYNE something real — a firm asks for information, the advisor has not authorized it, and
the correct action is to **stop and go back to the advisor** rather than send it and mention it later.
**Whether that actually happens under time pressure is the whole test of Artifact 10**, and no amount of
drafting settles it.
