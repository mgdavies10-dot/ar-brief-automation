## 3 · Worked example — continuing engagement E-26-031

*(The L1 example: advisor entered asking whether to move; succession and ownership entered scope; the
covenant may not have survived a firm acquisition; capability frustration proved less material than
first believed.)*

**Brief §1.** Format: *initial assessment consultation.* Disclosure **L1**; conclusion level supported
**1 — decision-support**. Participants: advisor. **Decision authority: not yet established** — the
junior advisor's succession expectation is undocumented, so whether the advisor can decide alone is
genuinely open. *That single field changed how the session was planned.*

**Brief §2.** *"This consultation is intended to test whether the decision in play is broader than the
one the advisor came in with, and to determine which areas warrant L2."* Success would be: **scope
confirmed or corrected**, the succession assumption tested, the covenant dependency understood, and a
depth decision the advisor chooses. **Not** a direction.

**Brief §3.** Advisor's stated question: *"Should I move?"* "Better" in the advisor's words: *"a platform
that doesn't cost me clients and a way to eventually step back."* **Scope revision from L1:** succession,
ownership, and covenant risk now also in scope. *The boundary line matters here — the brief says scope
was revised, and says nothing about what that implies for direction.*

**Brief §4.** Already available: channel, tenure, bands, team, client types, concentration band,
origination mix. **Genuinely unanswered:** whether the junior advisor's expectation matches the
advisor's, and what the covenant actually says.

**Brief §5 — what must be tested (four).**
1. *Assumption:* the junior advisor expects equity or succession. **Why:** changes both the team and
   succession picture. **Test:** ask what has actually been said, by whom, and when.
2. *Advisor-reported fact:* the non-solicit is "about a year." **Why:** several comparisons depend on it.
   **Test:** establish whether a copy exists and whether the acquisition changed it.
3. *VYNE interpretation:* the frustration is service-related rather than capability-related. **Why:** if
   correct, a firm change may not address the cause. **Test:** offer the reading and invite disagreement.
4. *Unknown:* concentration in the top eight relationships and its origination. **Why:** the gap between
   asset and revenue portability is unusually wide here. **Test:** explore origination and dependency.

**Brief §6 — principal unknowns.** (a) What the covenant actually restricts — resolved by counsel review
of the executed agreement; **not practical now**, specialist. (b) Whether the successor's expectation
matches — resolved by a conversation the advisor has not yet had; **practical now**, but the advisor's
to have.

**Brief §7 — contradiction.** Advisor recalls a one-year non-solicit; the firm was acquired 18 months ago
and the agreement may have been replaced. *If the advisor's recollection is right, several options open
sooner; if the replacement governs, timing and possibly viability change.*

**Brief §8 — alternatives.** *In scope:* stay · move · internal succession · sale to an external buyer.
*Provisionally set aside:* launching an independent RIA — **why:** the advisor's stated preference is to
step back, not take on more business responsibility; **returns to scope if:** the succession conversation
fails and ownership becomes the priority. *Not yet assessed:* merger with another practice.

**Brief §9.** Counsel — affects any directional conclusion dependent on the covenant; **prerequisite for
that issue, unaffected areas continue.** Valuation and counsel later for succession/equity. CPA for
forfeiture treatment.

**Brief §10.** Flagged: **advisor may be considering staying**; a non-compensating alternative (internal
succession) may prove stronger. **Gate not performed.**

**Brief §11 — opening.** *"Before I share where our thinking has landed, tell me what's happened since we
last spoke — and what you've been turning over."* Follow-ups: *"What do you think we may be missing?"* ·
*"Has anything changed with the team?"* · *"What would make this hour useful to you?"*

**Brief §12.** Intended: **deepen selected areas to L2** — pending the advisor's choice.

**Brief §13.** All seven initialled. The one requiring genuine thought: *no unsupported direction* — the
consultant's private view is that succession is the real issue, and **that view appears nowhere on the
brief** except as testable item 3, phrased as an interpretation to be challenged.

**Issue sheets prepared: two** — the succession expectation, and the covenant. Not the concentration
question, which is handled from the brief.

**No direction is prepared.** The session's purpose is to test scope and determine L2 depth.

## 4 · Critique

- **Too much preparation?** A thirteen-section brief plus optional issue sheets plus a checklist is real
  work. **Defence:** the brief is one page and mostly short entries; issue sheets are exceptional, not
  routine; the checklist is a completed check, not new analysis. **Honest risk:** for a *focused issue
  session* (Consultation Arch §9), the full brief is disproportionate — a shorter form for follow-up
  sessions is probably needed and is deliberately not invented here.
- **Risk of scripting the conversation?** The most serious risk, and structurally mitigated: §11 opens
  with the advisor speaking first, §13's last check is *"can be used conversationally — it is not a
  script,"* and the brief is explicitly *"a prompt sheet, not an agenda to walk through."* **Residual
  risk:** §5's numbered test list will tempt a consultant to work through it in order. The frozen
  architecture already says the advisor experiences Listen · Reflect · Test · Decide · Commit — but
  nothing here *prevents* a consultant reading §5 top to bottom.
- **Duplication with L1?** Real but bounded. §3, §5, §6, §7, §8, and §9 all draw from the L1 Handoff —
  which is the point: the handoff is the transfer, the brief is the session plan. **The genuine risk is
  transcription** — copying the handoff into the brief without *selecting*. §5's three-to-five cap is the
  only forcing function, and it is the field most likely to be over-filled.
- **Burden for founder-led launch?** With the L1 benchmark at 75–90 minutes, adding preparation of
  perhaps 20–40 minutes puts a single engagement's first cycle near two and a half hours before any
  consultation occurs. **That is material at launch capacity** and should be measured in the simulated
  cases rather than estimated here.
- **Risk of preparing a recommendation too early?** This is where the artifact does its most useful work
  — and where it is most vulnerable. A consultant who has completed L1 *will* have a view. The brief
  gives that view exactly one legitimate home: **§5 as an interpretation to be tested**, never §2, §3, or
  §12. **Residual risk:** an interpretation stated in §5 as *"the frustration is service-related"* is one
  short step from a direction, and only the consultant's discipline separates them.
- **Is one page realistic?** **Mostly, and not always honestly.** Thirteen sections fit on one page only
  if entries stay short — which is the intent. But §5 with five items, §7 with a contradiction, and §8
  with three alternative categories will spill for a complex engagement. **My assessment: one page is the
  right target and the right discipline, but it will be two pages in genuinely complex matters, and
  pretending otherwise would invite either cramped writing or content pushed into issue sheets that
  don't warrant them.** Better to state the target as a discipline than to enforce it as a rule.

## 5 · Recommendation

**Approve with revisions.** The three components implement the governing premise: the advisor experiences
a five-part conversation while the consultant works from real structure, and depth is genuinely displaced
into optional sheets rather than loaded onto the brief. Every frozen constraint is preserved — scope
without direction, gate flagged not performed, alternatives non-exhaustive, no fee terms, no PII, no
implied authorization, temporary notes separated from the record.

**Three revisions I would want:**

1. **State the one-page target as a discipline, not an absolute** — with an explicit note that a complex
   engagement may run to two pages, and that the correct response is *sharper selection*, not smaller type
   or spurious issue sheets.
2. **Add a short-form brief for follow-up and focused-issue sessions** — the full thirteen sections are
   disproportionate for a single-issue follow-up, and a consultant facing that mismatch will either skip
   preparation or complete it perfunctorily. *(Sections 1, 2, 5, 6, 12, 13 would likely suffice.)*
3. **Add one line to §5 guarding against transcription** — something to the effect that items are
   *selected* from the worksheet, not copied, and that if all five slots are full the session purpose in
   §2 is probably too broad.

**One item for founder attention:** the cumulative time from Orientation review through consultation
preparation is now the binding constraint on launch capacity — not any single artifact. Worth measuring
end-to-end in the simulated cases rather than per-artifact.
