## 2 · Critique

- **Too long?** This is the most legitimate concern. As drafted it is roughly a 4–5 page read with
  ~35 substantive prompts. **Defence:** most are checkboxes or bands, and free text is concentrated in
  five questions that carry most of the value (§1.1, §1.4, §4 "what does better mean," §4 five-years,
  §5 frustrations). **Honest risk:** it will still feel long to a busy advisor at 9pm. *If a trim is
  wanted, the highest-value cut is §4's eleven-row objectives grid — it could drop to six rows without
  material loss.*
- **Too invasive?** Tested against every prohibition: **no client names, account numbers, SSNs,
  addresses, statements, or book export**; no detailed transition economics; personal/family
  explicitly advisor-controlled and optional; deferred comp asked as *"roughly when does it vest"*
  rather than dollar amounts. **Residual concern:** §5's prior-contact question is the one item that may
  feel intrusive on first read — it is prefaced with *"this protects you,"* which is true, but a live
  conversation would carry it better than a form.
- **Too recruiter-like?** The structural defences are real: no firm names anywhere, no "what would it
  take to move you," no deal or package questions, no compensation-expectation field, and **stay / wait /
  succession / wind-down are first-class scope options.** The single most anti-recruiter element is §9's
  inclusion of **decline** as a named outcome. **Residual risk:** §3's bands (AUM, production) are the
  same fields a recruiter would ask for — the difference is what VYNE does *not* ask alongside them, which
  the advisor cannot yet see.
- **Too formal?** §8's checkbox acknowledgment is the most form-like element in an otherwise
  conversational document, and it sits jarringly close to §7's plain-spoken compensation language.
  **Mitigation available:** it could be reframed as a short confirming paragraph the advisor signs. Kept
  as checkboxes for now because each item is a discrete understanding VYNE needs on record — but this is
  a genuine style fork.
- **Insufficient to make an entry decision?** Checked against 1B's ten inputs: **decisions in scope
  (§2), disclosure (§6), readiness (§1), fit (§2–5), urgency (§1.4), specialist dependencies (§5),
  conflicts/prior contact (§5), PII (§8), capacity (internal)** — all sourced. **One genuine gap:**
  nothing establishes whether the advisor is the **sole decision-maker** (vs. a partner, spouse, or team
  needing to agree). That matters for readiness and for who should attend consultations. *Recommend
  adding one line to §4 or §5: "Who else is part of this decision?"*
- **Data that should be deferred?** Two candidates. **(a)** §3 *major business lines* and *client types*
  are arguably L2 depth — though both are needed to identify capability requirements, which is the
  bridge to everything downstream, so I'd keep them. **(b)** §5 *product/platform constraints* is the
  weakest fit at L1 — an advisor often cannot answer it without records, and it may read as a data
  request. *Recommend softening to "anything you already know won't transfer easily" or deferring to
  assessment.*

## 3 · Recommendation

**Approve with revisions.** The Orientation accomplishes all five required purposes, honors every
prohibition, and reads as a confidential professional intake rather than a lead form. The three
disclosure commitments (§6) and the six named next steps including decline (§9) are the elements most
likely to differentiate it in an advisor's experience.

**Three revisions I'd make before it goes to a real advisor:**

1. **Add "Who else is part of this decision?"** — the one genuine gap for the entry decision, and it
   also determines who should be in consultations.
2. **Soften or defer §5's product/platform-constraints question** — it reads as a records request at a
   stage where trust hasn't been established.
3. **Decide the §8 form** — checkbox list *(as drafted)* or a short signed confirming paragraph. The
   paragraph would read more premium; the checkboxes are cleaner evidence of discrete understandings.

**Optional if length is a concern:** trim §4's objectives grid from eleven rows to six.
