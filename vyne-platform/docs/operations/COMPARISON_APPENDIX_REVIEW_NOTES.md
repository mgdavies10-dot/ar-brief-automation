## 3 · Three worked examples

### 3.1 · Model-level comparison

**Type:** model-level · **Options:** stay in the current employee model · another employee firm ·
supported independence · independent RIA · **Five dimensions.**

> **Dimensions used:** capabilities · ownership · economics *(structure, not amounts)* · operating
> responsibility · transition requirements.
> **Dimensions deliberately omitted:** client impact and timing — on this engagement they did not
> materially differentiate *between models*; they differentiate between firms, and that is a later
> comparison.

**Standing statement carried:** *"A model-level comparison does not establish how any specific firm will
perform."*

**Economics dimension** presents structures, not numbers: employee models compensate through a grid with
a deferred component; supported independence retains a larger share of revenue against advisor-borne
expenses and platform fees; independent RIA carries the full expense base with the fullest ownership
outcome. **Common basis stated:** *how compensation is structured and who bears which costs* — **not**
projected income, which cannot be compared at model level without firm-specific terms.

**Material differences established:** ownership and operating responsibility. **No material difference
established:** capabilities, at model level — *"capability differences arise between specific firms and
platforms, not between models as such."*

**No ranking. No specific-firm conclusion. No implicit winner** — options are presented in the order the
advisor raised them, and the Appendix says so.

### 3.2 · Firm-level comparison — asymmetric source strength

**Type:** firm-level · **Options:** stay · **Firm A** *(written proposal in hand)* · **Firm B**
*(public materials and one verbal conversation the advisor reports)*.

**The design problem:** Firm A has documented advisor-specific terms; Firm B does not. **A naive table
would make Firm B look worse simply because less is known about it.**

**How the Appendix handles it — economics dimension:**

| | Stay | Firm A | Firm B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payout structure | current grid, documented | **documented** in the written proposal dated ___ | firm's **public materials** describe a range; specific terms **not established** |
| Transition package | not applicable | **documented**, with stated retention and vesting conditions | the **advisor reports being told** a range; **not confirmed in writing** |
| Deferred component | current plan, documented | **documented**, with vesting schedule and forfeiture terms | **not disclosed** |

**The standing rule appears directly beneath:**

> *"Missing or unverified information must not be treated as a disadvantage merely because another
> option has more complete information."*

**And the material-difference line is honest about what can be concluded:**

> *"A material difference between Firm A and Firm B has not been established. Firm A's terms are
> documented; Firm B's are not. The difference in what is known is not itself a difference in what is
> offered."*

**Each unknown carries a decision consequence** (§10): *whether Firm B's specific terms would change the
comparison — resolvable only through an authorized inquiry.*

**§13 boundary is live here and stated:** Firm B appears based on public materials and the advisor's own
account. **Comparing it authorizes nothing**, and obtaining its actual terms **would require a separate
firm-specific authorization.**

**Internally (8B):** Firm A's economics are source category **2** *(written advisor-specific proposal)*;
Firm B's are categories **5 and 6** *(public material; verbal representation)* — **and that hierarchy
never appears advisor-facing.**

### 3.3 · Normalization failure — the example that matters most

**The situation:** Option A presents a **gross nine-year transition package**. Option B presents
**current annual cash plus equity**. The advisor, reasonably, wants to know which is worth more.

**Why the available information does not support a defensible common basis:**

| | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Period | nine years | annual, ongoing |
| Form | gross, largely forgivable-loan proceeds | cash compensation + equity |
| Certainty | contingent on retention and production conditions | cash is current; equity value is contingent and unvalued |
| Tax character | forgiveness recognized over the term | ordinary income; equity treatment depends on structure |
| Terminal value | none stated | equity, unvalued on current information |

**Five distinct mismatches** — multiyear vs. annual · gross vs. net · guaranteed vs. contingent ·
current income vs. terminal value · differing tax character. **Normalizing would require assumptions
about retention, growth, equity valuation, and tax treatment that the current information does not
support.**

**What the Appendix says — verbatim model:**

> **"These figures are not directly comparable on the information currently available."**
>
> *Option A's figure is a gross multiyear amount, largely structured as forgivable loan proceeds,
> contingent on retention and production conditions over nine years. Option B's figure is current annual
> cash compensation, alongside an equity interest whose value is not established on current information.*
>
> *A single comparable figure would require assumptions about your retention, production growth, the
> equity's value, and tax treatment. We do not have a defensible basis for those assumptions today.
> **What would be required to compare them:** the written terms and conditions of Option A's package, a
> basis for valuing Option B's equity, and a decision about the time horizon over which you want the
> comparison made.*

**No total. No winner. No "roughly equivalent."**

**8B §5 records:** `NORMALIZATION DEFENSIBLE? ☐ NO` — which is what forces the statement above rather
than leaving it to the consultant's discretion.

> **This is the example that justifies the whole artifact.** A table showing "$X over nine years" beside
> "$Y per year plus equity" would look rigorous and would be actively misleading. **The refusal to
> normalize is the analysis** — not an absence of it.

## 4 · Critique

- **Excessive width?** The four-to-seven dimension rule and two-to-four option rule are inherited from
  the Record and are the right constraints. **Residual risk:** a firm-level comparison with four options
  and seven dimensions is a 28-cell table, and every cell needs a source, date, and verification status
  in 8B. **The advisor-facing width is bounded; the internal cost is not**, and it scales
  multiplicatively.
- **False precision?** Directly guarded — §8's fourteen checks, 8B §6's twelve integrity checks, and the
  defensible-yes/no gate. **Residual risk:** normalization done *correctly* still produces a number that
  looks more certain than its assumptions. §8 requires stating the common basis, which helps, but a
  reader may still take the normalized figure as fact.
- **Hidden ranking?** §11's design sub-rule is the strongest control here — naming colour, icons,
  ordering, bolding, check marks, totals, and badges. **Residual risk:** **column order.** It cannot be
  eliminated, only disclosed, and the Appendix requires saying what determines order where it could be
  misread. Worked example 3.1 does this.
- **Firm-marketing claims presented as fact?** §9's four model attributions and the non-conversion rule
  handle it. **Residual risk:** attribution fatigue — a dimension where every cell reads *"the firm
  states…"* becomes tiring, and the temptation is to attribute once and then stop. **8B §8's register is
  the check**, because it forces per-claim attribution to be recorded even where the Appendix reads
  smoothly.
- **Stale information?** 8B §9 plus the re-verify-before-issuance rule. **Residual risk:** the rule is
  judgment-based ("reasonable possibility that it has changed") rather than periodic. That is correct at
  launch — inventing refresh periods now would be arbitrary — **but it depends entirely on the
  consultant asking the question.**
- **Comparison of unlike economic terms?** The primary protection, and I believe the artifact handles it
  as well as a document can. **Residual risk:** advisor pressure. An advisor who wants a single number
  will ask for one, and *"these are not directly comparable"* can feel like evasion. **The mitigation is
  the sentence pattern in 3.3** — always pair the refusal with *what would be required to compare them*,
  so it reads as a path rather than a dead end.
- **Founder production burden?** **8B is the heaviest internal artifact in the sprint.** Per-cell
  sourcing, normalization records, and fourteen issuance controls on top of the Record's own assembly
  record. **This is real, and it is the strongest argument for the Appendix being absent by default.**
  §2's three-part test should be applied strictly.
- **Premium or spreadsheet-like?** The honest answer: **an Appendix is inherently more spreadsheet-like
  than any other VYNE artifact**, and that is acceptable because it is explicitly supporting material,
  not the deliverable. The premium quality lives in the Record. **What would make this bad** is a wide
  table with thin cells and no basis statements — which is exactly what §5, §6, and §8 exist to prevent.

## 5 · Recommendation — resolved

**APPROVED as a controlled operating draft (founder, 2026-07-25); revisions applied.** All three
revisions were adopted, in sharper form than proposed, plus two principles the founder added.

### Change log — v0.1 → v0.2 (2026-07-25)

1. **Material comparison points + Source Bundles** *(8B §3a, §3b)* — replaces per-cell metadata, which
   the founder correctly identified as unworkable and not productive of better analysis. *"A material
   comparison point is a value or statement that could meaningfully affect the advisor's understanding
   or decision. It is not automatically every visual cell."* Norm: **8–20 ordinary · 21–28 with a stated
   reason · >28 triggers narrowing, a separate due-diligence schedule, or division.** Source Bundles
   record metadata **once** and are referenced by identifier, with the standing rule that **bundling
   changes where the metadata lives, not the traceability requirement.** Tracking added for material
   points, bundles, and preparation/rework minutes. *This resolves my revision 1 better than I proposed —
   I suggested a tighter width norm; the founder changed the unit of measurement instead, which is the
   right fix.*
2. **Incomparability pattern moved into the template** *(8A §8 and 8B §5)* — the five-part A–E sequence:
   state the conclusion · state what each figure measures · explain why the difference matters ·
   **state what would be required** · **state what narrower comparison can still be made now.**
   8B §5's `NORMALIZATION DEFENSIBLE? NO` now drives a completion checklist for all five parts.
   **Part E is the founder's addition and is the most important one** — it prevents *"not comparable"*
   from becoming an escape hatch when something useful can still be compared. Paired with: *"A partial
   comparison may be presented only when its scope is explicit and it is not portrayed as a comparison
   of total value."*
3. **Neutral column-order rule** *(8A §11, recorded in 8B §1)* — current path first when it is the
   baseline · then alphabetical by displayed name · alphabetical throughout when there is no baseline ·
   advisor-requested order permitted with the reason recorded. **Never ordered by** recommendation,
   attractiveness, economic value, source strength, VYNE compensation, probability of selection, or
   perceived quality. Plus the formatting control barring override through colour, type size, badges,
   check marks, column width, or visual prominence. *This closes the last implicit-ranking channel and
   removes a recurring judgment call.*
4. **Two governing principles added verbatim:**
   - **"The refusal to normalize is itself an analytical conclusion when normalization would require
     unsupported assumptions."** *(8A §8 and 8B §5)*
   - **"Source strength affects how confidently and precisely information may be presented. It does not
     determine option quality. Better documentation permits more precise description; it does not
     establish that the better-documented option is substantively better."** *(8A §9 and 8B §1)*

   *The second was implicit in worked example 3.2 and is now stated as doctrine — which is where it
   belongs, because the asymmetric-documentation trap will recur in nearly every firm-level comparison.*

**Status: APPROVED as a controlled operating draft.** 8A and 8B at v0.2. Not production-frozen.

### Original recommendation (superseded, retained for the record)

**Approve with revisions.** The artifact holds the boundary the founder set: **VYNE's compensation and
commercial terms never appear; advisor-relevant firm economics do**, properly sourced, normalized, and
contextualized. The Appendix is subordinate to the Record, cannot be delivered without it, states no
conclusion, permits only three verdicts, and authorizes nothing. Missing information is structurally
protected from becoming a negative score. Most importantly, **the normalization control is built as a
gate rather than an instruction** — 8B §5's defensible-yes/no forces the incomparability statement
instead of leaving it to judgment under advisor pressure.

**Three revisions I would want:**

1. **Add a stated norm for the internal cost of firm-level comparisons.** 8B's per-cell sourcing scales
   multiplicatively with options × dimensions. A firm-level Appendix at the maximum permitted width
   (5 options × 7 dimensions = 35 sourced cells) may be impractical for a founder-led launch. Either a
   tighter norm for firm-level work specifically, or an explicit acknowledgement that the maximum is
   theoretical rather than operational.
2. **Add the incomparability sentence pattern to 8A §8 itself**, not only to the worked example. The
   pattern — *state that they are not comparable · state what each figure actually measures · state what
   would be required to compare them* — is what keeps the refusal from reading as evasion, and it should
   be in the template where a consultant will find it under pressure.
3. **Decide whether column order should be explicitly randomized or fixed by a stated rule.** §11
   requires disclosing what determines order, but does not say what *should* determine it. A stated
   default *(the order the advisor raised them, or the order they appear in the Record)* would remove a
   recurring judgment call and close the last implicit-ranking channel.

**One item for founder attention:** worked example **3.3** is the one to read. If the incomparability
language reads as rigorous rather than evasive, this artifact does its job. If it reads as VYNE avoiding
the question, the fix is the sentence pattern in revision 2 — and it matters more than anything
structural here, because an advisor under time pressure asking "so which is worth more?" is the exact
moment this control either holds or fails.
