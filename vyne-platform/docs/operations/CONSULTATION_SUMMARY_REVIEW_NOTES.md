## 2 · Field-by-field rationale

*R = required every Summary · C = conditional.*

### 6A · Advisor Consultation Summary

| § | Purpose | R/C | Upstream | Downstream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Header | identifies the session and dates the understanding; "as of" prevents it reading as permanent | **R** | Brief §1 | Record; engagement file |
| 2 Decision discussed | anchors to **the advisor's framing**, not VYNE's | **R** | Brief §2–3 | Recommendation Record Layer 1 |
| 3 Where things stand | the artifact's core job — status without direction; seven plain-language options replace internal levels | **R** | Brief §12; session outcome | Record; next-session Brief |
| 3 "does not create, amend, or finalize" | prevents the Summary being read as the recommendation | **R** | R&R §8 | — (control) |

### 6B · Internal Consultation Record

**Tier 1 (eight fields, every session):** date/participants · decision by Ontology reference · material
facts corrected *(with source and verification status)* · assumptions challenged and disposition ·
changes in objectives or confidence · unresolved issues · next steps with owner and timing ·
authorization decision including none.

**Tier 2 (eight triggers):** disagreement with both positions · decision-relevant advisor reactions ·
specialist dependencies · compensation disclosure made and when · conflict-sensitive item or gate trigger
*(recorded, not performed)* · prior firm or recruiter contact · return-to-assessment trigger · change in
recommendation direction with the supporting new information.

**Provenance and time:** Brief form and issue sheets used · pre-read sent and whether a working direction
was labelled provisional · consultation start/finish · consultant, Summary-preparation, and rework
minutes · Summary version and issue date with the two-day check · **three transfer/disposal
confirmations** (only decision-relevant notes transferred · working notes discarded · no client PII).

*The transfer confirmations are the operative link to the documentation boundary in 5C — without them
"temporary working space" is an assertion rather than a control.*

## 3 · Worked example 1 — initial consultation, E-26-031

*(Advisor originally asked whether to move; succession and ownership entered scope; the covenant requires
counsel; no direction supported; selected areas advance; no authorization.)*

> **ADVISOR CONSULTATION SUMMARY**
>
> **§1** Advisor: [name] · Consultation date: 12 August 2026 · Participants: [advisor], [consultant]
> Format: initial assessment consultation · **Our understanding as of August 12, 2026** ·
> ADVISOR-FACING · Version 1 · Issued
>
> **§2 · The decision we discussed**
> Your question, as you framed it: *"Should I move?"*
> What's in scope: whether a change of firm is the right step — and, following our conversation, also how
> succession and ownership are handled, and what your current agreements permit.
> What this consultation was for: to test whether the decision in play is broader than the one you came
> in with, and to agree what we should look at more closely.
>
> **§3 · Where things stand today**
> **We're not yet in a position to support a direction** — and we don't think it would be responsible to
> imply one.
> *This Summary records where the discussion left the decision. It does not independently create, amend,
> or finalize a recommendation.*
>
> **§4 · What changed or was clarified**
> - **We had this wrong:** we had treated your interest in "stepping back eventually" as a long-horizon
>   aside. It isn't. It's central, and it changes what we should be evaluating.
> - **New information:** your junior advisor has an expectation about succession or equity that has never
>   been documented, and you haven't yet had the conversation that would establish whether your
>   expectations match.
> - **Assumption revised:** we had assumed your frustration was mainly about platform capability. On your
>   account it's substantially about service and support, which a change of firm may or may not address.
> - **Confirmed as still accurate:** your practice profile, team structure, and the broad shape of your
>   client base as described in your Orientation.
>
> **§6 · The major trade-offs**
>
> | Path | What it gains | What it costs |
> |---|---|---|
> | Changing firms | may resolve the service and support issues; potential economic improvement | does nothing on its own about succession; transition risk to relationships; timing interacts with your unvested compensation |
> | Addressing succession where you are | deals directly with the thing you said matters most | leaves the service frustrations unaddressed; may require documenting arrangements that don't currently exist |
>
> **§8 · What remains unresolved**
>
> | The question | Why it matters | What would resolve it | Owner | Needs |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | What your current agreements actually restrict | several options can't be compared honestly until we know | review of the executed agreement by your attorney | you, with counsel | specialist |
> | Whether your junior advisor's expectation matches yours | changes both the team picture and the succession picture | a conversation you haven't yet had | you | time |
> | How much of your revenue would realistically follow you | the answer differs by relationship; averages would mislead you | a closer look at how relationships originated and where revenue concentrates | VYNE, with your input | deeper detail |
>
> **§9 · Where a specialist is needed**
>
> | Specialist | The issue | What it affects | |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Attorney | whether your non-solicit survived your firm's acquisition, and what it restricts | any conclusion about moving, and timing | needed before a conclusion that depends on it |
>
> *We don't summarize or predict what a specialist will say.*
>
> **§10 · Next actions**
>
> | Action | Owner | Target timing | Intended result |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Send your agreement to counsel | You | at your pace | establish what is actually restricted |
> | Have the succession conversation | You | when you're ready | establish whether expectations align |
> | Look more closely at portability and at ownership/succession options | VYNE | after you confirm | a real basis for comparison |
>
> **§11 · Authorization status** — **No firm-specific authorization was given.**
> *Any authorization referenced here exists only through the separate firm-specific authorization
> artifact. This Summary authorizes nothing.*
>
> **§12 · How we're paid** *(first substantive Summary — included)* — approved disclosure.
>
> **§13 · Closing** — *"Please tell us if this Summary is inaccurate or incomplete…"* + the four standing
> notes.

**Sections omitted:** §5 (nothing settled yet) · §7 (no material difference in view — the advisor
accepted the reframing).

**Internal Record for the same session** carries: Ontology references (★, **J**, **D**, **K**, **G**);
the corrected fact and its source/verification; the withdrawn capability assumption; confidence lowered
on "move resolves the problem"; three unresolved issues; next steps; **authorization: none**. Tier 2
triggers fired: specialist dependency; **conflict-sensitive item — advisor may be considering staying**
*(recorded, gate not performed)*; return-to-assessment trigger *(objectives changed)*. Time and Summary
version recorded; three transfer confirmations ticked.

> **ADVISOR CONSULTATION SUMMARY**

> Format: specialist debrief / focused follow-up · **Our understanding as of September 3, 2026** ·
> ADVISOR-FACING · Version 1 · Issued
>
> **§2** Your question: *"Where does the covenant leave me?"* In scope: what your agreements permit, and

> **§5 · What appears settled**
> - What your current agreements restrict. *(Settled on what we know now; material new facts could
>   reopen it.)*
>
> **§8 · What remains unresolved**
>
> | The question | Why it matters | What would resolve it | Owner | Needs |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Whether your junior advisor's expectation matches yours | it may determine whether this is a succession decision rather than a firm decision | the conversation we discussed | you | time |

>
> | Action | Owner | Target timing | Intended result |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Have the succession conversation | You | at your pace | establish whether expectations align |

**Sections omitted:** §6, §7, §9 *(dependency closed)*, §12 *(no trigger — not the first Summary, no new
firm, no change in compensation context)*. **Well under one page**, which is the point: a focused
follow-up should produce a focused Summary.

## 5 · Critique

- **Too much like meeting minutes?** Structurally avoided: there is no chronology, no attendee-by-attendee
  account, and no "we discussed X." Every section asks *what changed* or *what happens now*. **Residual
  risk:** §4 is one careless step from becoming a transcript — a consultant listing everything discussed
  rather than what materially changed. The word *material* appears in the instruction, but nothing
  enforces it.
- **Repetitive after every consultation?** Genuinely mitigated by modularity — example 2 runs five
  sections. **Residual risk:** §§2, 3, 11, 13 recur verbatim-ish every time, and an advisor receiving
  their fourth Summary may find the standing notes wearing. *A short-form Summary for routine follow-ups
  is a plausible later refinement; deliberately not invented now.*
- **Risk of implying agreement?** The most serious risk in an advisor-facing record. **Controls:** §7
  attributes both views and asks whether it may remain unresolved; §5 states settled is provisional; §13
  invites correction as normal. **Residual risk:** **silence in a Summary reads as assent.** A matter
  genuinely disputed but omitted from §7 will look agreed six months later. *The §13 invitation is the
  only real protection, and it depends on the advisor actually reading and responding.*
- **Risk of drifting into recommendation?** §3's seven statuses are deliberately status-not-direction, and
  the standing sentence blocks the Summary from carrying a conclusion. **Residual risk:** §6 trade-offs.
  Presenting trade-offs *is* substantive content, and trade-offs written with any asymmetry of care will
  read as a lean. The instruction says "both directions"; the discipline is the consultant's.
- **Burden of two records?** Real. The Internal Record's Tier 1 is eight fields and Tier 2 fires often
  early in an engagement; the Summary adds preparation time on top. **Mitigation:** substantial overlap —
  Tier 1 corrections, assumptions, unresolved, next steps, and authorization map almost directly onto
  Summary §4, §8, §10, §11, so the Summary is largely a rewrite for a different reader rather than fresh
  analysis. **Honest cost:** still meaningful, and now measured in the Pilot Time Log.
- **Is two business days realistic?** For a founder-led launch at low volume, yes. **At concurrent
  volume, probably not** — and this is the field most likely to slip first. *It is stated as a launch
  target, and the Time Log will show whether it holds.* Worth noting: **a late Summary is worse than a
  brief one**, because the advisor's memory of the conversation is what makes corrections possible.
- **Premium or bureaucratic?** The Summary reads as premium **when the sections are omitted properly**
  and bureaucratic when they are not. Example 2 is the model. **The single biggest determinant of how
  this lands is whether consultants have the confidence to leave sections out** — and a new consultant's
  instinct will be to complete the template.

## 6 · Recommendation — resolved

**APPROVED as a controlled operating draft (founder, 2026-07-25); revisions applied.** All three
revisions I recommended were adopted — each in a sharper form than I proposed — plus two founder
additions.

### Change log — v0.1 → v0.2 (2026-07-25)

1. **§8a "Material matters not addressed or intentionally deferred" added** *(conditional)* — matter ·
   why it wasn't addressed · whether it remains in scope · next step. Explicitly **only** for material
   **in-scope** deferrals, with the stated guard that it is *not* a list of every subject that did not
   arise and the Summary is *not* an exhaustive transcript. *Better than my proposed generic omission
   line: a broad "not covered" heading would have implied the Summary is a transcript, which is the
   failure mode the whole artifact is designed against.*
2. **§4 transcript-drift guard added** — *"records only information that materially changed, corrected,
   or clarified the decision record. It is not a recap of everything discussed."* With the explicit
   no-change formulation: *"No material facts, objectives, assumptions, or priorities changed in this
   session."* **That sentence makes a no-change session a valid, complete Summary** rather than an
   apparent omission — which is what would otherwise tempt a consultant to pad §4.
3. **Standing footer replaces repeated closing notes** — one compact "About this Summary" paragraph on
   every Summary. **Two deliberate exclusions:** §11 authorization status **stays in the body every
   time** (it is a decision record, not a standing note), and §12 compensation disclosure **stays
   conditional** under its four triggers and does not migrate into the footer.
4. **Silence-is-not-agreement clause added** *(founder addition, §13)* — *"Not responding to this Summary
   does not mean that you agree with every point. Any matter shown as disputed or unresolved remains so
   unless it is later confirmed or resolved."* **This closes the largest residual risk I identified.** My
   version relied on the correction invitation, which only works if the advisor responds; this states the
   default directly, so an unanswered Summary cannot later be read as ratification.
5. **Delivery standard sharpened** *(founder addition)* — **preferred: next business day; required launch
   target: no later than two business days.** The purpose is now stated in the artifact — *to preserve the
   advisor's ability to accurately correct the record* — with the operating consequence: **a short
   accurate Summary delivered on time is preferable to a late elaborate one.** Five-case review added
   (average delivery time · correction rate · whether advisors still recall enough to correct accurately ·
   preparation time · whether the artifact is being **overproduced**), with timing changes classed as
   **operating-version decisions**, not methodology changes.

*On my "before memory fades" suggestion: the founder was right to reject it as the operating rule. It is
the **reason** for the standard, not a measurable one — and an unmeasurable standard is not a standard.
It is now recorded as the purpose, with two business days as the rule.*

**Status: APPROVED as a controlled operating draft.** Both artifacts at v0.2. Not production-frozen.

### Original recommendation (superseded, retained for the record)

**Approve with revisions.** Both artifacts do what the premise requires: the Summary records what changed,
where the decision stands, what's unresolved, and what's next — without minutes, without a recommendation,
without internal apparatus, and without manufacturing agreement. The Internal Record carries the
governance trail with the transfer confirmations that make the 5C documentation boundary real. Modularity
is genuinely load-bearing, and example 2 demonstrates it.

**Three revisions I would want:**

1. **Add a "matters we did not resolve and did not discuss" line to §8, or an explicit omission note** —
   because **silence currently reads as assent.** If a disputed matter is simply absent, the record
   implies agreement that never happened. A single line — *"matters not covered in this session: ___"* —
   closes it.
2. **Add one sentence to §4 guarding against transcript drift** — that §4 records what *materially
   changed*, not what was discussed, and that a session which changed nothing should say so in a line
   rather than list the conversation.
3. **Consider a standing-notes footer rather than repeating §13 in full every time** — the four notes are
   necessary, but by the fourth Summary they should be a compact footer rather than a section, or the
   advisor stops reading them.

**One item for founder attention:** the two-business-day standard is the first thing that will break under
concurrent load. I'd rather see it measured and revised than quietly missed — the Time Log now captures
it, and the honest question after five cases is whether the target should be two days or "before the
advisor's memory of the conversation fades," which is the reason the standard exists.
