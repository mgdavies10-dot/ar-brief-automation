## 2 · Field-by-field rationale

*Designation: **E** = required every time · **A** = only when applicable.*

### Guide (4A)

| Element | Why necessary | Supports | E/A |
|---|---|---|---|

### Handoff (4C)

All fifteen fields are **required every time** — it is a one-page transfer document, and a partially
completed handoff defeats its purpose. Three carry particular weight: **§5** capped at the few areas that
genuinely matter (if everything is material, the classification failed); **§6** highest-impact unknowns,
the most useful line on the page; **§13** anticipated gate trigger, recorded for **visibility only** —
recording is not performing.

## 3 · Worked example

**Engagement E-26-031 · L1 · prepared 2026-08-04**

**Entry.** The advisor came in asking *"should I move?"* — a 19-year veteran at a regional firm, roughly
$310M AUM, ~$2.1M production, two junior advisors and a client-service associate. Orientation selected
scope: affiliation model · economics · book portability · timing.

**The scan (abbreviated to the modules that mattered).**

**Module 1 — objectives.** *Materiality High · Confidence Developing.* What emerged: the advisor's stated
"better" was *"a platform that doesn't cost me clients and a way to eventually step back."* The second
half had not appeared in Orientation. Decision participants: spouse, and a junior advisor the advisor
described as *"probably my successor, though we've never written anything down."*

**Module 6 — enterprise continuity and value.** *Materiality **High** · Confidence **Limited**.* The
succession thread from Module 1 opened here. There is an undocumented successor expectation, no equity
agreement, and no valuation ever performed. **This is a live succession decision that Orientation did not
surface and the advisor had not named.** Unknown: whether the junior advisor's expectation is
consistent with the advisor's. Specialist dependency: valuation (later), counsel (for any equity
arrangement).

**Module 10 — risk.** *Materiality **High** · Confidence **Limited**.* The advisor described a non-solicit
from memory as *"pretty standard, I think it's a year."* No copy on hand. Also: the firm was acquired
18 months ago and the advisor is unsure whether the covenant travelled or was replaced. **Specialist
dependency: counsel — blocking.** Verification status of the covenant terms: **unverified**.

**Module 3 — book portability.** *Materiality High · Confidence Limited.* Concentration: roughly 40% of
revenue in the top eight relationships (band). Origination mixed — several inherited from a retiring
advisor at the current firm, which the advisor had not flagged as a portability factor. Constraint: a
meaningful book of firm-proprietary managed accounts. **No estimate produced.** Recorded: *asset
portability ≠ revenue portability*, and that inherited relationships plus proprietary programs make the
gap between the two unusually wide here.

**Module 5 — transition economics.** *Materiality Moderate · Confidence Limited.* Categories identified:
unvested deferred compensation vesting in roughly 14 months; forfeiture exposure unquantified; proprietary
account repapering costs unknown. **No figures, no ranges.** CPA dependency noted for forfeiture treatment.

**Modules 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 11.** Acknowledged and classified. Module 4 (capability compatibility)
*Moderate* — no severe gap; the advisor's frustration is service-related rather than capability-related,
which **weakens the original "move" framing.** Module 8 (team) *High* — the junior advisor is both a
retention dependency and a succession participant. Modules 2, 7, 9, 11 *Low to Moderate*, each with a
recorded reason for not deepening.

**Module 12 — integrated conclusion (scope only).**

- **Decisions in scope:** revised from "move vs. stay" to **★ Strategic Outcome** *plus* **J Succession &
  Exit**, **D Ownership**, **K Risk/Legal**, **G Book portability**. *(The succession decision was not in
  the advisor's original framing.)*
- **Material areas:** Modules 6, 10, 3, 8.
- **Specialist dependencies:** counsel (**blocking** — covenant); valuation and counsel (later, for
  succession/equity); CPA (forfeiture treatment).
- **Recommended scope:** advance **Modules 3, 6, and 10** to L2; hold 5 at L1 pending the covenant answer.
- **Recommended disclosure progression:** selected areas to L2, **at the advisor's election.**
- **Consultation purpose:** test the reframing — that this may be a succession-and-ownership decision that
  a move would not solve — and resolve the covenant dependency before any option is compared.

**What VYNE did NOT do.** No directional recommendation. No statement that the advisor should stay, wait,
or move. No portability figure or range. No "leaning." No firm named or ranked. No suggestion that the
succession reframing is *the* answer — only that it is now in scope and untested.

**Handoff §6 (highest-impact unknowns), as written:**
> 1. *Whether the non-solicit survived the acquisition and what it actually restricts.* Resolved by:
>    counsel review of the executed agreement. **Blocking** — several options cannot be compared until
>    this is known.
> 2. *Whether the junior advisor's succession expectation matches the advisor's.* Resolved by: a
>    conversation the advisor has not yet had. Materially changes both the team and succession picture.

**Handoff §13:** *advisor may be considering staying* — anticipated gate trigger noted for visibility.
**Gate not performed.**

## 4 · Critique

- **Duplication of the Orientation?** The most serious design risk, and the one most likely to make L1
  feel like a bad experience. **Mitigations:** every module lists the Orientation content already
  available; the opening explicitly promises reuse; a four-condition rule governs re-asking; and prompts
  are written as *confirmations* ("your Orientation says ___ — still accurate?") rather than fresh
  questions. **Residual risk:** Modules 1–3 draw most heavily on Orientation and are where a careless
  consultant will re-ask. That is a training exposure, not a document defect — and training does not yet
  exist.
- **Excessive question volume?** Twelve modules × 3–5 prompts is **~45 prompts** on paper. That would be
  an interrogation if all were asked. **The design intent is that most are not:** immaterial modules are
  acknowledged and closed in a sentence. **Honest risk:** nothing in the artifact *enforces* brevity, and
  a conscientious consultant may work through all 45. *If a control is wanted, the strongest would be a
  stated expectation of L1 duration — deliberately not invented here, since it should come from the first
  real engagements.*
- **Consultant documentation burden?** Eighteen fields × twelve modules is the honest number, and it is
  substantial. **Partially mitigated:** immaterial modules need only module/Ontology/materiality/confidence/
  reason-not-to-deepen — five fields, not eighteen. **Residual risk:** this is manual until Phase 3, and
  skipped documentation silently voids the materiality record that scope decisions rest on.
- **False precision?** Actively guarded: no portability percentage, no economic figures or ranges, no
  valuation, no aggregate score, no weighting, no R/Y/G, and confidence expressed in words. **Residual
  risk:** the three-band materiality scale can itself imply more rigor than a light scan supports —
  *Unknown* is provided precisely so a consultant is never forced to guess a band.
- **Risk of drifting into recommendation?** The single greatest failure mode, since a consultant who has
  seen the pattern will feel the answer. **Four controls:** the governing rule; Module 12's explicit
  prohibition list; the initialled drift check; and the handoff's closing test — *if a reader could infer
  VYNE's preferred outcome from this page, it has drifted.* **Residual risk:** the *reframing* in the
  worked example (succession, not a move) sits close to the line. I judge it acceptable because it changes
  **scope**, not **direction** — but it is the exact place drift would begin, and it deserves attention in
  review.
- **Does the worksheet feel too clinical?** Yes, somewhat — and unlike the Orientation, **that is
  appropriate**: this is VYNE's internal instrument and the advisor never sees it. The premium experience
  lives in the conversation (guide §1 and §4.2), not in the worksheet. **The genuine risk is leakage** — a
  consultant whose questions start sounding like worksheet fields. The guide's rule that prompts are
  "preparation, not a script" is the only defence, and it is a soft one.
- **Information better deferred?** Two candidates. **(a)** Module 2's revenue-composition question edges
  toward L2; kept because portability and economics are uninterpretable without at least a broad split.
  **(b)** Module 3's origination question ("how did these relationships originate") is the most probing
  item at L1 — but the worked example shows exactly why it earns its place: inherited relationships were
  the decisive portability factor and the advisor had not flagged them. **Recommend keeping both.**

## 5 · Recommendation

**Approve with revisions.** The three artifacts implement the governing premise and rule faithfully:
all twelve modules are acknowledged with selective depth, materiality and confidence are separate axes,
the compensation-separated sequence is preserved, evidence classes are untouched with source and
verification recorded separately, and four independent controls prevent L1 from producing a direction.
No fee terms, no PII fields, no scoring, no neutrality claims, no implied authorization.

**Two revisions I would want, both about restraint rather than content:**

1. **Set an expected L1 duration** — the one control that would keep ~45 prompts from becoming an
   interrogation. I deliberately did not invent a number; it should come from the first two or three real
   engagements, then be written in.
2. **Add a short "immaterial module" completion path to the worksheet** — an explicit five-field minimum
   for modules classified Low or Out of scope, so the burden is visibly proportionate rather than merely
   implicitly so.

**One item for founder attention rather than revision:** the worked example's *reframing* behaviour —
where the scan changes what decision is in scope — is the most valuable thing L1 does and the closest it
comes to the drift boundary. Worth confirming that you read that example as scope-setting rather than as a
recommendation in disguise, because it will set the pattern.
