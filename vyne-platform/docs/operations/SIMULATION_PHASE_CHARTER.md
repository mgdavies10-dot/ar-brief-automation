# Simulation Phase Charter — proposed (rev 2)

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Status:** PROPOSAL for founder authorization · **Not begun.**

> ## What this phase is
> **The simulations test whether VYNE's consulting process is usable, disciplined, confidential,
> efficient, and capable of producing useful advisor decision support.**
>
> **They do not validate every future recommendation, replace firm intelligence, replace professional
> judgment, or establish that five synthetic cases represent the marketplace.**

> ## The correction this revision applies
> **The simulations are an internal quality-assurance process. They are not the VYNE product, they are
> not the sole basis for future recommendations, and the complete simulation record is not an advisor
> deliverable.**
>
> A prior draft of this charter treated the operating artifacts as though they *were* the analysis. **They
> are the container.** The analysis is advisor-specific understanding **plus firm and marketplace
> intelligence** plus professional judgment — and a simulation that exercises the paperwork while
> assuming the intelligence tests the wrong thing.

---

## 1 · What the simulations show — and what they do not prove

| **They show** | **They do not prove** |
|---|---|
| where the process feels repetitive | that a recommendation is correct for every advisor |
| which questions uncover useful information | that five simulated cases represent the marketplace |
| which documents are too long | that AI can replace VYNE's consulting judgment |
| whether confidentiality and authorization controls work | that every advisor should receive every artifact |
| whether the recommendation can be explained clearly | that firm intelligence is complete |
| how much founder time an engagement takes | that the process should run automatically |
| where human judgment is still required | |
| whether the deliverables feel premium or procedural | |

*A simulation is testing the playbook before using it with real clients. **It is not the client
engagement.***

## 2 · Founder review and editing checkpoints

> **The simulation must not proceed past a checkpoint until founder approval is recorded.**

**Each checkpoint is presented as a concise review packet** — not the engagement file. **The founder must
not be required to reread the entire file at every checkpoint.**

Each packet contains: **what changed · decisions required from the founder · key facts · unknowns ·
proposed next step · references to the detailed supporting artifacts.**

### A · After Orientation and Entry Review

**Founder reviews and may edit:** decision framing · scope · engagement suitability · disclosure level ·
participant needs.
*Founder may direct: proceed · narrow · pause · decline.*

### B · After L1 Assessment and Handoff

**Founder reviews and may edit:** materiality · areas requiring deeper analysis · areas that should close
· evidence gaps · specialist needs · **required firm intelligence**.
*Founder may change which areas deepen.*

### C · Before Consultation

**Founder reviews and may edit:** the one-page Consultation Brief · the three-to-five matters to test ·
session purpose · advisor-facing tone · questions to omit or add.
*Test question: does this feel like a real consulting conversation?*

### D · Before Recommendation Preparation

**Founder reviews and may edit:** options in scope · assumptions · material unknowns · **firm
intelligence required** · comparison dimensions · specialist dependencies · conflict-sensitive matters.
*Founder may direct: gather more information · remove an option · add an option · return to
consultation.*

### E · Before Advisor-Facing Issuance — select the minimum useful advisor package

**Founder determines:** whether any advisor-facing document should be issued at all · which document or
documents are useful · appropriate depth · what should be removed · **whether the recommendation and
tone reflect founder judgment.**

**Selectable:** *no formal document yet* · concise Consultation Summary · Decision Status or Closure
Record · Recommendation & Decision Record · Firm/Model Comparison Appendix · focused firm-intelligence
brief · due-diligence questions · economics analysis · next-steps roadmap · firm-specific authorization ·
another narrowly defined output.

**Record per case:**

```
 Documents proposed: ___________________________________________________
 Documents approved by the founder: ____________________________________
 Documents deliberately withheld: ______________________________________
 Why each approved document materially helps this advisor: _____________
 Why excluded internal artifacts remain internal: ______________________
 Total proposed advisor-facing page count: ______
 Does the package feel: ☐ premium ☐ useful ☐ excessive ☐ procedural
```

> **The complete set of artifacts created internally is never the default advisor package.**
>
> **Nothing is final until the founder approves it.**

## 2a · Founder edits vs. baseline artifact changes

> **The founder may revise the application of VYNE's judgment within each case — including scope,
> materiality, questions, options, intelligence requirements, conclusions, tone, and advisor-facing
> deliverables.**
>
> **These case-level founder decisions do not automatically amend the controlled artifact templates.**

| **A · Case-specific founder judgment** | **B · Proposed artifact or methodology revision** |
|---|---|
| applies to the current advisor case | recorded in the **Simulation Defect Log** |
| may be made at any checkpoint | **not implemented between cases** unless it qualifies as a **Critical Stop Finding** |
| **does not change the common simulation baseline** | reviewed in the final Simulation Findings and Amendment Report |

*This is what preserves the value of testing a common baseline. Editing judgment inside a case is the
point; rewriting the templates between cases means cases 3–5 test a different system.*

## 2b · Founder Intervention Log

**At every checkpoint, record:**

```
 Claude's original proposed direction or treatment: ____________________
 Founder decision: _____________________________________________________
 Founder edits: ________________________________________________________
 Rationale: ____________________________________________________________
 Category: ☐ case-specific professional judgment
           ☐ missing or inadequate firm intelligence
           ☐ advisor-experience improvement
           ☐ artifact defect
           ☐ control failure
           ☐ other: ____________
 Downstream effect: ____________________________________________________
 May the underlying artifact require revision after the simulations? ☐ yes ☐ no
```

> **Purpose: identify where founder judgment materially improves or changes the technology-assisted
> work.** Without this, the final output looks like an uninterrupted AI result — which would misrepresent
> where VYNE's value actually comes from.

**Never presented to the simulated advisor.**

## 3 · The simulation is not an advisor deliverable

> **The complete simulation file, case packet, internal worksheets, defect log, AI working material, Gate
> workpapers, internal compensation notation, and quality-control record are never advisor-facing.**

**The advisor-facing output is selected separately, at Checkpoint E.**

| **Possible advisor-facing deliverables** | **Never advisor-facing** |
|---|---|
| Advisor Consultation Summary | the simulation case packet |
| Decision Status and Closure Record | internal worksheets |
| Recommendation & Decision Record | AI prompts and working material |
| Firm/Model Comparison Appendix | hidden facts planted for the test |
| focused firm-intelligence brief | defect logs |
| due-diligence question list | Gate workpapers |
| economics analysis | internal compensation notation |
| next-steps roadmap | internal scoring or quality testing |
| firm-specific authorization | every artifact created during the process |

> **VYNE creates the internal record necessary to support professional judgment. The advisor receives
> only the documents that materially help them understand and act on the decision.**

**Not every engagement receives every artifact.**

## 4 · Firm intelligence — a separate and essential input

**The simulations test the consulting workflow. They are not a substitute for the firm-intelligence
engine, and the operating artifacts must not be mistaken for the analysis.**

**Each simulation must identify:**

```
 ☐ What public firm intelligence is needed
 ☐ What information is already available
 ☐ What information is stale
 ☐ What claims require verification
 ☐ What information requires advisor authorization to obtain
 ☐ What remains unknown
```

### Sourcing approach — real public intelligence, synthetic advisor

| | |
|---|---|
| **Synthetic in every case** | advisor · practice · team · book · objectives · constraints |
| **REAL current public information** | Cases 3 and 4, and elsewhere where useful: actual wealth-management firms, platforms, affiliation models, industry programs |
| **Synthetic advisor-specific proposals or confidential terms** | **only** where needed to test source asymmetry · normalization · transition economics · authorization · or recommendation controls |

**Absolute limits:** **no firm is contacted** · **no confidential or nonpublic real advisor information is
used** · **synthetic terms are labelled internally and are never represented as actual current firm
terms.**

**For every public firm-intelligence item, record:**

```
 Source: _______________________________________________________________
 Retrieval date: ________   Effective date, if known: ________
 Currency: ☐ current ☐ potentially stale ☐ unverified
 Type: ☐ firm claim ☐ public document ☐ regulatory source
       ☐ market observation ☐ other: ____________
 What decision question it informs: ____________________________________
 Would additional verification or authorization be required in a real
 engagement? ☐ yes — what: ____________  ☐ no
```

> **The simulation should test whether VYNE can locate, organize, evaluate, date, and appropriately
> qualify public firm intelligence — not merely whether the artifacts contain a field asking for it.**

**Every simulated conclusion must combine all five:**

1. **advisor-specific facts** — objectives · business model · book characteristics · team · economics ·
   succession · ownership · risk;
2. **public marketplace and firm intelligence** — affiliation models · capabilities · platform structure
   · recruiting activity · advisor movement · compensation structures · transition programs · ownership
   opportunities · custody and technology · documented firm claims;
3. **advisor-authorized firm-specific information** — obtained only under a valid authorization;
4. **specialist input** where required;
5. **VYNE professional judgment.**

> **A simulated conclusion that rests only on advisor-specific facts and the artifact structure is a
> defect, not a result.** Checkpoints B and D exist substantially to catch that.

## 5 · AI and human-judgment boundary

> **AI may assist with research organization, comparison, drafting, issue spotting, consistency checking,
> and scenario testing. AI does not independently select the advisor's outcome or issue VYNE's
> professional judgment.**

**Every simulated recommendation must identify:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What AI or structured tools assisted with** | |
| **What evidence supported the conclusion** | |
| **What judgments were made by VYNE** | |
| **What the founder reviewed, changed, or approved** | |

**Prohibited advisor-facing language:** *the algorithm recommends · AI selected · the simulation
determined · the model scored · the system chose the best firm.*

**Approved positioning:**

> **"VYNE combines confidential consulting, structured analysis, current marketplace intelligence, and
> technology-assisted research. Technology helps organize and test information, but VYNE's conclusions
> are formed through professional judgment and consultation with the advisor."**

*Technology is visible as an advantage. It is never the decision-maker.*

## 6 · Two output layers per case

### A · Internal Simulation Record *(internal only)*

artifacts used · time · defects · friction · controls that held or failed · **AI assistance** · **founder
edits at each checkpoint** · **firm-intelligence gaps** · process findings.

### B · Proposed Advisor Package *(tests what a real advisor might receive)*

**Only** the documents that would materially help this advisor · concise · polished · **no internal
methodology exposure · no AI working record · no simulation terminology.**

> **The founder decides whether the Proposed Advisor Package is something VYNE would actually deliver.**
> That judgment is itself a finding.

## 7 · Case-packet structure

**Maintain the full internal engagement file for traceability** — but it is a reference, not the review
experience.

```
 /simulations/SIM-__/
   00_case_brief/        the synthetic advisor, planted facts, intended stresses
   01_checkpoint_packets/  five concise review packets (§2)
   02_artifacts/         the real artifacts, produced at real length
   03_firm_intelligence/ what was needed, held, stale, unverified, unknown (§4)
   04_internal_record/   time, defects, friction, controls, AI assistance, edits
   05_advisor_package/   the Proposed Advisor Package only
```

**Presented to the founder:** checkpoint packets and, at the end, the Internal Simulation Record and the
Proposed Advisor Package. **Not a hundred-page file.**

## 8 · The five cases

*Chosen so each stresses a different control, and no two produce the same artifact set. Run **1 → 2 → 3 →
4 → 5**, sequentially — each informs how the next is observed. **Not in parallel.***

| # | Case | Primary stress | Firm-intelligence load | Gate | Expected end state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Stay** — advisor arrives wanting to move; evidence supports staying | no-fee outcome · **will VYNE actually conclude *stay*** | moderate — **real public** | **T3 TRIGGERED** | Record · **Gate triggered, expect Cleared** · **no authorization** |
| **2** | **Wait** — a vesting date and an unresolved covenant dominate | **conditions on page one** · specialist prerequisite | low–mod — **real public** | **T3 TRIGGERED** | **Conditional Record** · counsel dependency · no authorization |
| **3** | **Employee-firm move** — narrows to two named firms | Appendix · **asymmetric source strength** · normalization · authorization chain | **high — REAL public, two named firms** | likely T1 | Full Record · **firm-level Appendix** · Gate · Authorization D/E/F |
| **4** | **Supported independence** — capability-driven; a non-compensating alternative is live | **compensation separation** · **DD authorization *before* any Record** | **high — REAL public platforms** | **T1/T2 at recommendation stage** | **DD Auth (A/B/C) first** · then Record · Appendix |
| **5** | **Advisor chooses against the recommendation** | the three separations · tone without grievance | moderate | per facts | Full Record · advisor decision differs · **both positions recorded** |

### Gate expectations — corrected

> **A "stay" or "wait" conclusion is not automatically conflict-free merely because VYNE earns no fee.
> The Gate still tests whether the process and conclusion were treated consistently.**

**Case 1 — Stay.** **T3 is triggered** because the advisor is considering staying. **The Gate is
required.** Expected outcome may be *Triggered — Cleared* if the substantive and counterfactual tests
support the stay conclusion. **No authorization should be created merely to keep the engagement moving.**

**Case 2 — Wait.** **T3 is triggered** because the advisor is considering waiting. **The Gate is
required**, and it must test **whether waiting received the same evidentiary and analytical treatment as
a compensating move.** Specialist dependencies remain respected. **No firm-specific authorization unless
a distinct due-diligence need arises and is separately approved.**

**Case 4 — Supported independence.** Limited due-diligence authorization may occur **before** a
Recommendation Record; **that authorization does not itself require the recommendation-stage Gate.** Once
a directional recommendation is being prepared, **apply the frozen trigger screen** — and where
triggered, **the Gate and cooling controls apply before any later recruiting introduction, submission, or
transition-focused authorization.**

> **Do not treat a no-fee outcome as proof that no conflict exists.**

**Why this order.** Cases 1 and 2 are the least artifact-heavy and surface pacing problems early. Case 3
first exercises the Appendix and the full authorization chain. **Case 4 is the only case that begins with
authorization before a Record** — it is the direct test of the founder's due-diligence correction. Case 5
is the tone test and benefits from four prior cases of practice.

## 9 · What is measured

**Quantitative — into the Pilot Time Log:** each stage's minutes · total founder time · issue sheets ·
Brief form · consultations · material comparison points · Source Bundles · Appendix pages · Record pages
by mode · rework minutes · **founder edit volume at each checkpoint**.

**Qualitative — three or four sentences per case:** *Did the process feel focused, repetitive, premium, or
procedural? Where did time go that did not earn it?*

**Questions the artifacts themselves asked to have tested**

| Artifact | Question |
|---|---|
| L1 Guide §1a | Are 45–60 / 75–90 min realistic? How often is the shortened path used? |
| Consultation Brief | Is one page realistic? Is the short form used when it should be? |
| Consultation Summary | Two-business-day delivery achievable? Correction rate? Overproduced? |
| Record 7A | **Did page one carry the full decision?** Premium or bureaucratic? |
| Appendix 8B | Do material comparison points stay within 8–20? |
| Gate 9A | **Do the written counterfactuals show meaningful variation** — or the same sentence each time? |
| Authorization 10A | Advisor friction. Does 60 days create stale permission or unnecessary re-signing? |

## 10 · What is deliberately not done

- **No new artifacts or templates.**
- **No revisions to frozen documents** — findings recorded, not applied.
- **No design, UI, CRM, software, or automation.**
- **No calculator work** *(Phase-3 backlog, unaffected)*.
- **No counsel-dependent language finalized** — the Engagement Understanding stays a counsel-review draft
  and simulations run on it as drafted.

> **The discipline that makes this phase useful is not fixing things as you notice them.** A defect
> noticed in case 2 and fixed immediately means cases 3–5 test a different system, and the five-case
> pattern is lost.

## 11 · Output

**One consolidated findings report**, containing: what worked · what was repetitive · what was slow *(with
measured time, not impression)* · what was unclear · what was overly defensive · what was genuinely useful
· **where firm intelligence was missing or assumed** · **where AI helped and where human intervention was
required** · and proposed changes, each classified as an **operating-version change**, a **conforming
amendment**, or a **documented pilot finding requiring the frozen-doctrine amendment path**.

**Nothing changes until that report is reviewed.**

## 12 · Honest limits

- **Simulated advisors do not push back the way real ones do.** Cases 3 and 5 are the weakest simulations,
  because the founder plays both sides.
- **Measured time will understate real time** — no scheduling delays, no waiting on counsel, no advisor
  who goes quiet for three weeks.
- **The Gate cannot be honestly simulated by its designer.** **Case 4 should be run with a deliberate
  attempt to reach the answer VYNE would prefer, to see whether the counterfactuals catch it.** If they
  do not, that is a finding about the Gate.
- **Firm intelligence in simulation is synthetic.** The cases can test *whether the process demands the
  right intelligence* — they cannot test whether VYNE can actually obtain it.
- **Five cases is enough to find friction and duplication. It is not enough to validate judgment
  quality.** That requires real advisors.

## 13 · Founder authorization required

**This charter is a proposal.** Simulations do not begin until the founder separately approves — and may
approve a different case set, sequence, checkpoint structure, or measurement scope.
