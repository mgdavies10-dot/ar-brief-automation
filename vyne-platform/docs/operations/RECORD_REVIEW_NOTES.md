## 2 · Field-by-field rationale

*R = required · C = conditional. Upstream = where it comes from. Downstream = what consumes it.*

| § | Purpose | R/C | Upstream | Downstream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Header and identity | dates the judgment and states the mode plainly; the participation-≠-agreement clause prevents the Record being read as ratified | **R** | Internal Consultation Record; 7B §1 | engagement file; any later version |
| 2 Page one | **the artifact's core promise** — the whole decision readable in one sitting | **R** | all upstream | the advisor's actual decision |
| 3 Recommendation / supported status | mode-specific; carries the calibration honestly | **R** | consultation outcome; L1 handoff | advisor decision; next steps |
| 4 **Your decision** | **the founder's central addition** — separates what the advisor chose from what VYNE recommended, with no retroactive rewriting | **C** | session outcome | 7B §10; engagement history |
| 5 Authorization status | shows whether a separate authorization exists, without reproducing its terms | **R** | authorization artifact | 7B §11; submission control |
| 6 Options and paths | consistent dimensions, stay and wait included, missing shown as missing, **no compensation labels** | **R** *(scaled)* | worksheet; comparison work | advisor's evaluation |
| 7 Major trade-offs | the substance an advisor actually weighs, both directions | **R** *(Cond/Full)* | assessment | advisor's evaluation |
| 8 Decision basis and evidence | plain-language third layer; taxonomy stays internal | **R** | evidence index (7B §6) | advisor's confidence in the reasoning |
| 9 Material assumptions | assumptions visible and falsifiable, never buried in a calculation | **R** *(where any exist)* | issue sheets; worksheet | changed-facts triggers |
| 10 Material unknowns | what we don't know and whether it limits the conclusion | **R** | L1 handoff §6; consultation | next steps; scope |
| 11 Alternatives set aside | shows the work on rejection; not a silent omission | **R** *(Cond/Full)* | Brief §8 | advisor challenge |
| 12 Where our views differ | **a Record that hides a material disagreement is defective** | **C** | 7B §8 | advisor's own judgment |
| 13 Specialist dependencies | routes regulated questions; never predicts their answer | **C** | 7B §9 | referral; conclusion limits |
| 14 How we're paid | required disclosure, unmodified | **R** | Commercial Principles §5 | — |
| 15 What changed since prior | **the advisor never compares two documents to find the change** | **C** *(superseding)* | 7B §12 | version history |
| 16 Next steps | owner, timing, intended result; no manufactured urgency | **R** | consultation | engagement continuation |
| 17 Record details | lifecycle metadata and supersession | **R** | 7B §1, §12 | archive; traceability |
| 18 Corrections | invites factual correction while stating that neither participation nor silence implies agreement | **R** | Consultation Arch §3 | correction log |
| 19 Standing footer | compact standing notes; permitted-use flagged for counsel | **R** | AEU §16 | — |

**7B rationale in brief.** §2 references rather than duplicates — the governing rule. §3 is the
recommendation-ready gate. §4 holds the seven gate fields **and the binding rule that blocks issuance
while pending**. §5 keeps compensation notation internal. §6 indexes evidence by pointer. §7–§9 index
unresolved items, disagreements, and dependencies **each with an advisor-facing section reference** —
which is what makes "did we actually represent this to the advisor?" answerable. §13 is the issuance
control.

## 3 · Four worked examples

### 3.1 · Decision-Support Record — E-26-031

**Mode:** Decision-Support. **Target 1–2 pages.**

> **Page one.** *What we conclude:* **We are not in a position to recommend a direction, and we don't
> think it would be responsible to imply one.** *Confidence:* our understanding of your objectives is
> now solid; our understanding of what your agreements permit is not. *Why:* two questions dominate this
> decision and both are open — what your non-solicit actually restricts after your firm's acquisition,
> and whether your junior advisor's succession expectation matches yours. *What gives us pause:* the
> question you came in with may not be the question that matters most. *What would change this:* answers
> to either open question. *Immediate next step:* your attorney reviews the executed agreement.

**§6 — paths still in scope, unranked:** stay · internal succession · move · external sale.
**§10 — unknowns are the centre of gravity**, with the covenant marked as limiting the conclusion.
**§13 — counsel: required before a conclusion that depends on it**, with the note that unaffected areas
continue.
**§4 omitted** (no decision forming). **§5:** no authorization issued. **§7, §9, §11, §12 omitted** at
this mode.

**No portability figure. No lean. No firm named.**

### 3.2 · Conditional Recommendation Record

**Mode:** Conditional. **Target 3–5 pages.**

> **Page one.** *What we conclude:* **Subject to the three conditions below, the stronger direction
> appears to be to wait rather than move now.** *Confidence:* moderate on timing, lower on portability.
> *Why:* the vesting date materially changes the economics; the covenant question is now answered and is
> less restrictive than we allowed for; your capability frustration is service-related and a move may
> not resolve it.
>
> **Conditions — on page one:**
> 1. Revenue portability holds within the range in §6. *If it does not:* the economics of moving weaken
>    materially and waiting becomes stronger still.
> 2. Your succession conversation confirms alignment. *If it does not:* this becomes an ownership
>    decision and the comparison changes.
> 3. No change to the vesting schedule. *If it changes:* timing reopens.
>
> *What gives us pause:* waiting has a cost — the service issues persist, and a year of frustration is
> not nothing. *Immediate next step:* the succession conversation.

**§7 trade-offs** state both directions, including what waiting costs.
**§9 assumptions** carry the portability range with what would confirm it.

### 3.3 · Full Recommendation Record — advisor chooses differently

**Mode:** Full. **Target 5–8 pages.** *This example exists to demonstrate the three separations.*

> **§3 — our recommendation.** A supported-independence path, for reasons of capability compatibility
> and enterprise value, with the drawbacks stated: more operational responsibility, a longer runway to
> stability, and real transition risk.
>
> **§4 — your decision.** ☑ *You selected a different legitimate direction:* an employee-firm offer with
> a larger transition package.
> *Your decision does not retroactively change what we recommended. Where the two differ, both are
> stated separately.*
>
> **§12 — where our views differ.** *Our view:* the capability gap in lending and alternatives is
> material to your client base, and the employee model forecloses the enterprise value you said you
> wanted. *Your view:* near-term liquidity outweighs both, and the capability gap is manageable through
> referral. *Basis for each:* stated. *Practical consequence:* if our reading is right, the value
> question returns in five to seven years. *May this remain unresolved:* yes — it is a weighting
> difference, not a factual one.
>
> **§5 — authorization status.** ☑ **No firm-specific authorization has been issued.**
> *This Record authorizes no firm contact… Any authorization exists only through a separate
> firm-specific authorization artifact.*

**The three separations, visible on one document:** VYNE recommended A · the advisor chose B · **no
authorization exists**, and choosing B did not create one. The reservation appears **once**, in §12, and
is not repeated as pressure.

**Internally (7B):** compensation notation records that the recommended option was *non-compensating*
while the chosen one was *compensating* — **and that notation appears nowhere in the advisor-facing
Record.** Gate: not triggered by the differential alone.

### 3.4 · Decision Status and Closure Record

**Mode:** Closure. **Target 1–2 pages.**

> **§3 — Closure form.** *What was evaluated:* whether to change firms, and — as the work developed —
> whether this was a succession and ownership decision. *What we could conclude:* your agreements permit
> more than you assumed; your objectives are clear and consistent. *What we could not conclude:* whether
> a move improves your position, because the succession question remains open and it governs the answer.
> *Why this is concluding:* you have decided to address succession internally first, and that work is
> yours to do. *What should cause you to revisit:* the succession conversation not resolving · a
> material change in your firm's platform or economics · an unsolicited offer you want tested.
>
> **§4 — your decision.** ☑ You paused or concluded the engagement.
> **§5 — authorization status.** ☑ **No firm-specific authorization has been issued.**
> **§10 — material unknowns preserved**, so a future engagement does not start from zero.

**Closure invents nothing.** There is no direction here because none was supported — and the Record says
so as its substance, not as an apology.

**7B for this Record** confirms: unused authorizations revoked *(none existed)*; the advisor owes VYNE
no fee — **recorded internally as the operating position, not stated in the advisor-facing Record**,
which is not the place for it (that language lives in the Engagement Understanding §17).

## 4 · Critique

- **Excessive length?** Nineteen sections reads long, but modularity is real: example 3.1 uses ten,
  example 3.4 uses eight. **The genuine risk is Full mode**, where all nineteen can legitimately apply
  and 5–8 pages is optimistic if §6's comparison runs wide. *The comparison appendix exists precisely to
  absorb that — but it isn't drafted yet, so Full-mode length is currently untested.*
- **Too legal or defensive?** The Record now carries: a participation-≠-agreement clause, an
  authorization disclaimer, a corrections-and-non-agreement statement, and a standing footer. **Four
  protective statements in one document is close to the limit.** They are individually justified — each
  closes a real gap — but a reader could experience the aggregate as a firm protecting itself.
  *Mitigation:* three of the four are single sentences and two sit in the footer or header rather than
  the body. **Watch this in the pilots.** It is the most likely source of "bureaucratic" feedback.
- **Too much methodology?** Actively guarded — no evidence-class taxonomy, no module numbers, no level
  labels, no gate fields. §8 conveys the substance in plain language. **Residual risk:** §9 and §11
  (assumptions, alternatives-set-aside) are methodologically flavoured by nature; written badly they
  read as showing off the process rather than serving the decision.
- **False precision?** Guarded: no numeric confidence, no bare scores, missing data shown as missing,
  no portability figure at Decision-Support. **Residual risk:** §6's consistent-dimension comparison
  invites a table, and tables invite the appearance of rigor the underlying evidence may not carry.
- **Duplication of upstream artifacts?** 7B's governing rule handles this well — reference, don't copy —
  and §§7–9 index by pointer with an advisor-facing section reference. **Residual risk sits in 7A §8**,
  which necessarily restates evidence already in the worksheet and issue sheets. That restatement is for
  a different reader and is justified, but it is the place where a Record starts absorbing the
  engagement file.
- **Founder production burden?** **This is the heaviest artifact in the sprint**, and Full mode is the
  heaviest mode. Combined with the L1 benchmark (75–90 min) and consultation preparation, a full-cycle
  engagement now plausibly runs several hours of founder time before delivery. **The Pilot Time Log will
  answer this; estimating it here would be guessing.**
- **Advisor usability?** The page-one discipline is the strongest usability control in the whole system —
  the advisor gets the entire decision in one sitting and depth is opt-in. **The untested question is
  whether page one can genuinely carry a Full-mode decision** with drawbacks and reasons in few enough
  words. That is a writing problem, and the pilots will expose it.
- **Premium or bureaucratic?** **The determinant is §2.** A Record whose page one reads like a
  thoughtful letter and whose remainder is available-but-unobtrusive is premium. A Record where page one
  is a summary of the sections that follow is bureaucratic. The skeleton is built for the former; nothing
  enforces it.

## 5 · Recommendation

**Approve with revisions.** The artifact does what the premise requires and, most importantly, makes the
**three separations structurally visible** rather than merely stated — §3, §4, and §5 are distinct
sections, worked example 3.3 demonstrates all three on one document, and 7B mirrors them internally.
Closure mode works without inventing a recommendation. Gate fields stay internal and block issuance.
Compensation notation never crosses into the advisor-facing document. Conditions live on page one.

**Three revisions I would want:**

1. **Audit the four protective statements for aggregate tone** — participation-≠-agreement (§1),
   authorization disclaimer (§5), corrections-and-non-agreement (§18), and the footer (§19). Each is
   justified; together they may read as defensive. I would keep all four but consider whether §1's clause
   can move into §17 Record details, where it belongs logically and intrudes less on the opening.
2. **Set an expectation for §6's comparison width** — "consistent dimensions" currently has no bound, and
   twelve dimensions across five options is a table that will not fit any page target. A stated norm
   *(the dimensions that materially differentiate, typically four to seven)* would do it.
3. **Decide whether Full mode's page target survives without the comparison appendix.** 5–8 pages
   excluding the appendix is only meaningful once the appendix exists to absorb §6. Until Artifact 8 is
   drafted, Full-mode length is untested and the target is provisional.

**One item for founder attention:** worked example 3.3 is the one I would most want you to read closely.
It is where VYNE's positioning either holds or fails — the advisor chose against the recommendation, and
the Record records both without editorializing, without repeating the objection, and without letting the
disagreement quietly disappear. If that document reads right to you, the artifact is sound. If it reads
even slightly aggrieved, the fix is tone, and it matters more than any structural change here.
