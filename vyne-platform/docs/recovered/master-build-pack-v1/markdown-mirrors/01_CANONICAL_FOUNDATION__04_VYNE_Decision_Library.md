Volume IV establishes the Decision Library as VYNE's institutional knowledge system. It is not a book to be read once. It is a governed library that future consultants, models, AI agents, Advisor Studio and VYNE OS can all use.

4. Decision Science Library
Decision Science gives consultants a common language for understanding how advisors decide under complexity. It should be practical, not academic.
Concept
Advisor expression
VYNE response
Optionality overload
Too many credible paths increase comparison burden and make deferral feel safer than choice.
Reduce the field using the Future-State Mandate before deep comparison.
Decision fatigue
Repeated meetings, documents and new variables reduce cognitive quality.
Sequence work, identify decision-changing variables and pause when needed.
Loss aversion
Potential losses can feel more important than larger long-term gains.
Make both the cost of action and cost of inaction visible.
Anchoring
The first offer, payout or firm comparison becomes the reference point.
Define objectives and alternative set before headline economics dominate.
Status quo bias
Familiarity is mistaken for safety.
Model the future cost and risk of staying.
Confirmation bias
The advisor seeks evidence supporting an emerging favorite.
Prepare a challenge case and strongest opposing argument.
Social proof
Peer moves or industry trends substitute for personal fit.
Return to the advisor's own priorities and constraints.
Overconfidence
The advisor underestimates implementation, client or team risk.
Use downside cases and evidence-based readiness gates.
Ambiguity avoidance
The advisor chooses the path that feels easier to understand.
Clarify unknowns instead of rewarding the least examined option.
Identity attachment
Professional identity becomes fused with a firm, model or historical success.
Separate what the advisor values from the structure currently delivering it.
5. Decision Standards
The following standards apply to every Decision Card and every VYNE recommendation.
Define the decision before evaluating destinations.
Protect what is working before solving what is not.
Define the future before comparing offers.
Include staying, strengthening and waiting when credible.
Separate verified facts from reported claims and assumptions.
Model downside, not only expected or best-case results.
Treat clients, team, family and implementation as decision variables.
Disclose material VYNE and third-party economic conflicts.
Do not use a score to replace accountable judgment.
Document what could change the recommendation.

7. Founding Decision Cards
Version 0.1 includes ten founding cards. These cards establish the standard that future cards must follow.
CARD 01 — Stay as Currently Structured
DECISION: Whether the advisor should remain without pursuing material internal change.
TYPE: Stay and strengthen | High consequence | Reversible
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: A recruiter inquiry, temporary frustration, market disruption, leadership change or curiosity about outside economics.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor can distinguish transient frustration from persistent structural issues.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Current economics, platform strengths, service gaps, leadership conditions, client and team impact.
REQUIRED MODELS: Stay-base case, cost-of-inaction case and future-state gap.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Unresolved structural friction, deteriorating service, succession risk or material economic decline.
COMMON BIASES: Status quo bias, familiarity effect, sunk cost and fear of disruption.
RED FLAGS: Staying is chosen solely because comparison feels overwhelming.
BEST QUESTIONS: What is working? What will still be true in five years? What problem remains if nothing changes?
DECISION GATE: A stay decision is responsible only when the future-state gap is acceptable or intentionally accepted.
DELIVERABLES: Current Reality Record, Stay Rationale, review triggers and date.
CARD 02 — Stay and Strengthen
DECISION: Whether the advisor can reach the desired future through internal change rather than external movement.
TYPE: Stay and strengthen | High consequence | Partially reversible
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Persistent friction that may be negotiable, service gaps, local leadership issues, team capacity or economics.
READINESS SIGNALS: Specific problems have identifiable internal remedies and accountable owners.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Internal flexibility, leadership authority, budget, timing and prior attempts.
REQUIRED MODELS: Current versus improved economics, capacity impact and implementation cost.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Firm-wide structural limits, non-negotiable ownership restrictions or repeated failed remediation.
COMMON BIASES: Optimism bias, avoidance of difficult conversations and false equivalence between promise and commitment.
RED FLAGS: The plan depends on vague assurances or no internal decision-maker is accountable.
BEST QUESTIONS: What exactly must change? Who can authorize it? By when? What happens if it does not occur?
DECISION GATE: Written commitments, owners, dates and measurable outcomes are established.
DELIVERABLES: Strengthen Plan, Internal Diligence Record and review trigger.
CARD 03 — Move to Another Employee Firm
DECISION: Whether another employee platform more responsibly advances the advisor's future state.
TYPE: Employment and affiliation | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Platform gaps, leadership instability, compensation changes, client capability needs or cultural misalignment.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor's objectives are defined before specific firms are compared.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Platform capability, compensation, deferred treatment, transition terms, legal conditions, client fit and team support.
REQUIRED MODELS: Stay versus move income, deal schedule, deferred loss, client transfer and long-term wealth.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Client portability, team division, restrictive obligations, implementation complexity and contingent economics.
COMMON BIASES: Anchoring on upfront deal, social proof and problem substitution.
RED FLAGS: A target firm is selected before the strongest stay case and at least one alternative are evaluated.
BEST QUESTIONS: What becomes possible there that cannot be achieved here? What is being sacrificed? What must be documented?
DECISION GATE: Decision-changing claims are verified and downside scenarios remain acceptable.
DELIVERABLES: Firm Comparison, Economic Model, Constraint Map and Decision Record.
CARD 04 — Supported Independence
DECISION: Whether the advisor should pursue greater ownership and control within a supported platform.
TYPE: Ownership and independence | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Desire for enterprise value, flexibility and control without building every capability alone.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor wants ownership but values platform support and accepts business responsibility.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Payout, expenses, services, ownership rights, exit provisions, technology, supervision and capital needs.
REQUIRED MODELS: Net income, overhead, enterprise value, startup cost, transfer rate and downside liquidity.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Low operational readiness, limited team depth, insufficient capital or misunderstood support boundaries.
COMMON BIASES: Overweighting payout and underweighting operating complexity.
RED FLAGS: The advisor treats 1099 payout as net economics.
BEST QUESTIONS: Which responsibilities do you want to own? Which must remain supported? What is the real cost of that support?
DECISION GATE: Business-owner readiness and support boundaries are explicit.
DELIVERABLES: Independence Readiness Profile, Economic Model and Operating Responsibility Map.
CARD 05 — Launch or Join an RIA
DECISION: Whether full or substantial RIA ownership is the right path.
TYPE: Ownership and independence | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Control, enterprise value, fiduciary alignment, technology choice or dissatisfaction with platform restrictions.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor accepts governance, compliance, operating and capital responsibilities.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Custody, compliance, technology, staffing, contracts, financing, client needs and ownership design.
REQUIRED MODELS: RIA P&L, startup and working capital, transfer, enterprise value and downside runway.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Underestimated operating burden, weak compliance infrastructure, partner misalignment or inadequate capital.
COMMON BIASES: Control illusion, overconfidence and platform-cost simplification.
RED FLAGS: Independence is framed only as higher payout.
BEST QUESTIONS: What business are you prepared to build? What must remain true when markets decline? Who owns each operating function?
DECISION GATE: Operating model, governance, capital and client transition plan are credible.
DELIVERABLES: RIA Readiness Assessment, Operating Model, Capital Plan and Decision Record.
CARD 06 — Acquire a Practice
DECISION: Whether an acquisition creates responsible strategic and economic value.
TYPE: Growth and transaction | High consequence | Partially reversible
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Growth ambition, succession opportunity, geographic expansion or team leverage.
READINESS SIGNALS: The buyer has integration capacity and a clear acquisition thesis.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Revenue quality, client concentration, retention, staff, contracts, seller role, financing and cultural fit.
REQUIRED MODELS: Purchase price, debt service, retention sensitivity, earn-out, integration costs and break-even.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Capacity limits, client mismatch, weak diligence, seller dependence and financing risk.
COMMON BIASES: Growth optimism, deal momentum and underestimating integration.
RED FLAGS: The acquisition only works under near-perfect retention.
BEST QUESTIONS: What must be true after closing for this to be a good deal? Who integrates the clients and team?
DECISION GATE: Downside retention and integration cases remain financeable and operationally credible.
DELIVERABLES: Acquisition Scorecard, Valuation Model, Integration Plan and Decision Record.
CARD 07 — Merge with Another Team
DECISION: Whether combining teams improves capability, economics, succession and client outcomes.
TYPE: Growth and governance | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Scale needs, complementary capabilities, succession, leadership burden or geographic opportunity.
READINESS SIGNALS: Strategic rationale exists beyond shared overhead or personal chemistry.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Roles, ownership, compensation, governance, client segmentation, brand and conflict resolution.
REQUIRED MODELS: Combined P&L, ownership economics, capacity and partner exit scenarios.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Ambiguous authority, unequal expectations, culture conflict and client ownership disputes.
COMMON BIASES: Halo effect, conflict avoidance and optimism about governance.
RED FLAGS: Difficult decisions are deferred until after the merger.
BEST QUESTIONS: Who decides when you disagree? How is value created and divided? How does someone leave?
DECISION GATE: Governance, economics, roles and exit provisions are documented before commitment.
DELIVERABLES: Merger Thesis, Governance Map, Combined Economics and Decision Record.
CARD 08 — Sell or Monetize the Practice
DECISION: Whether and how the advisor should convert business value into liquidity or succession.
TYPE: Transaction and succession | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Retirement, fatigue, concentration, health, family priorities, market opportunity or capital proposal.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor understands the difference between headline valuation and realized after-tax value.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Valuation basis, buyer quality, payment security, earn-out, employment, client continuity and tax treatment.
REQUIRED MODELS: Cash and contingent proceeds, taxes, note risk, employment income and alternative timing.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Buyer financing, retention conditions, earn-out dependence, identity transition and client fit.
COMMON BIASES: Headline multiple anchoring, scarcity and urgency distortion.
RED FLAGS: Most value is contingent on conditions the advisor cannot control.
BEST QUESTIONS: What are you selling? What are you retaining? What happens if the buyer underperforms?
DECISION GATE: Realized value, risk transfer and post-transaction role are understood.
DELIVERABLES: Sale Comparison, Proceeds Model, Risk Map and Decision Record.
CARD 09 — Internal or Family Succession
DECISION: Whether ownership and leadership should transition internally or to family.
TYPE: Succession and governance | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Retirement horizon, continuity needs, next-generation interest or key-person dependency.
READINESS SIGNALS: Successor readiness and founder willingness to transfer authority can be evaluated honestly.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Capability, client trust, economics, ownership funding, timing, governance and family dynamics.
REQUIRED MODELS: Purchase funding, compensation, cash flow, ownership transfer and contingency.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Role ambiguity, entitlement, financing weakness, unequal family expectations and delayed authority.
COMMON BIASES: Legacy bias, optimism and avoidance of difficult performance assessment.
RED FLAGS: Relationship is treated as evidence of successor capability.
BEST QUESTIONS: Would this successor be selected without the relationship? When does authority actually transfer?
DECISION GATE: Capability, economics, governance and fallback plan are credible.
DELIVERABLES: Successor Readiness Assessment, Ownership Path and Transition Plan.
CARD 10 — Take External Capital or a Strategic Partner
DECISION: Whether external capital improves the business without undermining the advisor's objectives.
TYPE: Capital and partnership | Transformational | Difficult to reverse
TYPICAL TRIGGERS: Acquisition strategy, liquidity, infrastructure investment, succession or growth acceleration.
READINESS SIGNALS: The advisor can define the specific problem capital solves.
REQUIRED EVIDENCE: Valuation, governance rights, liquidity, control, return expectations, time horizon and exit provisions.
REQUIRED MODELS: Ownership dilution, proceeds, growth requirements, distributions, downside and exit scenarios.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS: Misaligned time horizon, control transfer, growth pressure, leverage and unclear exit.
COMMON BIASES: Valuation anchoring, prestige effect and underweighting governance.
RED FLAGS: Capital is accepted before the operating use and partner rights are understood.
BEST QUESTIONS: What does the capital require the business to become? What decisions will no longer be yours?
DECISION GATE: Use of proceeds, governance and downside remain aligned with the Future-State Mandate.
DELIVERABLES: Capital Partner Scorecard, Dilution Model, Governance Map and Decision Record.

Team / merger

Production, expense allocation, role changes and partner departure
Every model must identify source, date, owner and assumption classification.
Guaranteed, contingent, estimated and modeled amounts must be visibly separated.
Cash flow and taxable income must not be conflated.
A base case without a downside case is incomplete.
Model precision must not exceed the quality of the inputs.
Advisor-facing outputs require plain-language interpretation and limitations.

15. AI and Agent Use
The Decision Library should become the primary retrieval source for future VYNE agents.
Agents may identify the relevant Decision Card based on advisor context.
Agents may prepare questions, checklists, missing-evidence lists and draft model inputs.
Agents may compare current work against canonical standards and flag omissions.
Agents may not convert a card into an automatic recommendation.
Agents must distinguish canonical standards from provisional or incubator content.
Any external advisor output requires human review and controlled publication.
