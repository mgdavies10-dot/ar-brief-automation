V Y N E S T R A T E G I E S
VOLUME II
THE VYNE FRAMEWORK
and VYNE's 9 Methodology Canon
An original decision system for advisors facing expanding options, channels and uncertainty
CORE PROBLEM
The advisor's challenge is not a lack of options. It is the difficulty of making a high-consequence decision when the number of viable channels, firms, ownership structures and strategic paths has multiplied.
VERSION 0.1 | JULY 2026 | CONFIDENTIAL — FOUNDING METHODOLOGY DRAFT
Originality and Methodology Boundary
This methodology was developed from VYNE's Constitution, institutional architecture and first-principles analysis of advisor decision complexity. It is not intended to reproduce a competitor's wording, sequence, assessment, visual model or branded methodology.
The Framework begins with the advisor's decision, not with a recruiting destination.
Its four-part architecture is built around reality, direction, constraints and responsible choice.
VYNE's 9 is an original diagnostic canon designed to support that architecture.
Competitor research may be used to understand market conditions, but not as a drafting source for protected expression.
Names, marks and public-facing framework labels remain subject to legal and trademark review before launch.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
VYNE should not sound like a more polished version of another recruiting firm. It should sound like the institution that created a distinct category: advisor decision quality.
1. The Problem VYNE Solves
The wealth-management landscape has expanded faster than most advisors' ability to evaluate it. The advisor may now choose among employee models, independent employee arrangements, independent contractor platforms, supported independence, RIA affiliation, hybrid structures, internal succession, external sale, merger, acquisition, ensemble formation, minority capital, majority capital, affiliation, aggregation and combinations of those paths.
More choice can improve outcomes. It can also create paralysis, comparison fatigue, false confidence and dependence on whichever party frames the decision first.
The central VYNE insight is therefore:
INSTITUTIONAL INSIGHT
Advisor decisions have become harder not because good options disappeared, but because credible options multiplied.
VYNE exists to organize that complexity into a decision an advisor can understand, evaluate and defend.
2. The VYNE Framework
The VYNE Framework is the permanent decision architecture of the institution. It is broad enough to govern a transition, succession, acquisition, sale, partnership, growth investment, team design or decision to stay.
THE FOUR QUESTIONS
1. What is true now? 2. What must the future make possible? 3. What stands in the way? 4. Which path closes the gap responsibly?

These are not four sales stages. They are four disciplines. An advisor may revisit any stage as new evidence emerges.
3. Stage One — REALITY
REALITY prevents the process from beginning with assumptions. It establishes the advisor's present position with enough honesty and evidence to understand whether the current model is working, where it is fragile and what is already valuable.
3.1 What REALITY examines
Business economics, production, assets, revenue mix and cost structure
Client profile, service model, complexity and portability
Team roles, talent, capacity, dependency and ownership expectations
Platform capabilities, friction, restrictions and support
Advisor energy, confidence, frustration and readiness
Contractual, regulatory, transition and relationship exposure
What is working well enough that it should not be casually sacrificed
3.2 REALITY standard
REALITY STANDARD
Do not confuse dissatisfaction with evidence that a move is necessary. Do not confuse comfort with evidence that staying is wise.
3.3 Required outputs
A plain-language description of the current business
Verified facts separated from reported facts and assumptions
Current strengths worth preserving
Material tensions and their severity
An initial statement of whether the problem is structural, situational, interpersonal, economic or still unclear
4. Stage Two — DIRECTION
DIRECTION defines the future before the market begins selling versions of it. The advisor must know what the next chapter must enable, protect and avoid.
4.1 Future-state dimensions
Dimension
Question
Clients
What must become better for the people the advisor serves?
Business
What scale, capability, profitability or focus should the business reach?
Control
Where is autonomy essential, and where is support more valuable than control?
Economics
What income, enterprise value, ownership and risk profile should the future create?
Team
What roles, opportunities, succession and culture must exist?
Life
What should the business make possible for the advisor and family?
Legacy
What should endure after the advisor's direct production declines or ends?
4.2 Direction standard
DIRECTION STANDARD
A future state is not a wish list. It is a hierarchy of outcomes, trade-offs and non-negotiables.
4.3 Required outputs
A concise Future-State Mandate
The three outcomes that matter most
The conditions that are important but negotiable
The conditions that would make a path unacceptable
A time horizon and definition of progress
5. Stage Three — CONSTRAINTS
CONSTRAINTS converts aspiration into a realistic decision. It identifies what can prevent the advisor from reaching the desired future or make an otherwise attractive path irresponsible.
5.1 Constraint classes
Class
Examples
Methodological treatment
Structural
Platform model, ownership limits, payout mechanics, capital requirements
Determine whether the constraint is inherent or negotiable.
Economic
Lost revenue, taxes, financing, transition costs, deferred compensation
Model range, timing, downside and break-even.
Client
Portability, concentration, complexity, consent, service interruption
Assess exposure and mitigation before assuming transfer.
Team
Partner alignment, retention, roles, employment, succession
Treat team consent and incentives as separate decisions.
Legal / regulatory
Contracts, covenants, protocol status, licensing, privacy
Escalate for qualified legal or compliance review.
Operational
Technology conversion, staffing, office, vendors, workflow
Build an implementation burden estimate.
Personal
Identity, fear, fatigue, family, risk tolerance, timing
Make the constraint discussable without treating emotion as irrational.
Informational
Unknown facts, inconsistent claims, unreliable estimates
Create a diligence plan rather than guessing.
5.2 Constraint standard
CONSTRAINT STANDARD
A constraint is not automatically a reason to stop. It is a condition that must be understood, accepted, changed or mitigated.
5.3 Required outputs
A ranked Constraint Map
What is fixed, what is negotiable and what is unknown
The assumptions most capable of changing the decision
The diligence required before comparison is credible
The decision's practical and emotional cost
6. Stage Four — CHOICE
CHOICE compares credible paths against the advisor's Future-State Mandate and Constraint Map. It does not ask which option is most exciting. It asks which path most responsibly closes the gap between present reality and desired future.
6.1 Minimum alternative set
Stay as currently structured
Stay and strengthen or renegotiate
Prepare and defer the decision
Move, affiliate, merge, acquire, sell or restructure through one or more credible paths
6.2 The Responsible Choice Test
Test
Question
Alignment
Does the path materially advance the Future-State Mandate?
Evidence
Is the conclusion supported by verified information and transparent assumptions?
Trade-offs
Are the costs, losses and foreclosed options understood?
Resilience
Does the choice remain sound under reasonable downside scenarios?
Readiness
Can the advisor, team and clients execute the path responsibly?
Ownership
Does the advisor understand and own the reasoning?
Durability
Would the decision remain defensible after the immediate economics or emotion fades?
6.3 Required outputs
A structured comparison of credible alternatives
A recommendation or decision state: stay, strengthen, prepare, move or continue diligence
The reasoning and evidence supporting that state
Conditions that could change the conclusion
A staged action path with permissions and review points

8. Question-by-Question Methodology
Question 1 — What is working that must be protected?
This question identifies the assets, relationships, capabilities and conditions that should survive any future decision.
Dimensions to examine
Client trust and continuity
Team strengths and key-person dependencies
Platform capabilities currently delivering value
Economic features worth preserving
Brand, community and family considerations
Personal confidence, flexibility and energy
Illustrative prompts
What would you regret losing even if the economics improved?
Which parts of the current model are genuinely difficult to replace?
What would clients say is working best today?
Primary output: Strength Protection List.
Question 2 — Where is the current model creating meaningful friction?
This question distinguishes ordinary frustration from persistent barriers to the advisor's future.
Dimensions to examine
Frequency and severity of friction
Whether the issue is local, firm-wide, temporary or structural
Business and client consequences
Attempts already made to improve it
The cost of allowing it to continue
Illustrative prompts
What problem keeps returning after reasonable attempts to solve it?
Where does the current model consume energy without creating proportional value?
What is becoming harder to explain to clients or the team?
Primary output: Friction and Tension Record.
Question 3 — What do your clients need from the next chapter?
This question protects the decision from becoming advisor-centric while still recognizing that a healthier advisor business can improve client outcomes.
Dimensions to examine
Service capabilities
Investment and planning complexity
Pricing and transparency
Technology and reporting
Lending, banking, trust, alternatives and specialized needs
Continuity, succession and team depth
Illustrative prompts
Which client needs are underserved today?
What capability is becoming more important over the next five years?
Which clients are most exposed to a change?
Primary output: Client Future-State Requirements.
Question 4 — What do you want the business to make possible?
This question defines the advisor's desired future across business and life rather than reducing success to payout.
Dimensions to examine
Growth and profitability
Control and support
Ownership and enterprise value
Team opportunity
Time and personal capacity
Succession and legacy
Risk and resilience
Illustrative prompts
What should be meaningfully different three to five years from now?
What does success allow you to stop doing?
What kind of business do you want to own, lead or eventually transfer?
Primary output: Future-State Mandate.
Question 5 — Which trade-offs are you unwilling to make?
This question establishes the advisor's boundaries before attractive offers begin reshaping them.
Dimensions to examine
Client disruption
Loss of control
Economic volatility
Cultural compromise
Team inequity
Capital burden
Brand or reputational exposure
Family and lifestyle cost
Illustrative prompts
Which gain would not justify the cost required to obtain it?
What condition would cause you to reject an otherwise attractive path?
Which compromise would make the future feel unlike your own?
Primary output: Non-Negotiables and Trade-Off Hierarchy.
Question 6 — What could prevent a successful change?
This question examines implementation risk before the advisor becomes emotionally committed to an outcome.
Dimensions to examine
Client portability
Partner and team alignment
Contractual restrictions
Financing and liquidity
Technology and operational conversion
Licensing and compliance
Timing, stamina and family readiness
Illustrative prompts
What has to go right for this path to work?
Who must agree, stay or act?
Where is the decision most vulnerable to an assumption?
Primary output: Constraint Map.
Question 7 — What information must be true before you can decide?
This question converts uncertainty into a diligence plan.
Dimensions to examine
Firm claims requiring verification
Economics and transition assumptions
Ownership and exit provisions
Service and platform capabilities
Recruiting or capital commitments
Implementation resources
Legal and regulatory interpretation
Illustrative prompts
Which unanswered question could reverse your current preference?
What are you accepting because it sounds plausible?
What should be documented rather than promised verbally?
Primary output: Decision Diligence Agenda.
Question 8 — Which paths deserve serious comparison?
This question ensures the advisor compares strategies rather than reacting to whichever opportunity arrived first.
Dimensions to examine
Stay
Stay and improve
Wait and prepare
Move to another employee model
Supported or full independence
Merge, acquire, sell or recapitalize
Hybrid or staged combinations
Illustrative prompts
What option would you consider if no recruiter had called?
What is the strongest case for staying?
Which path best matches the future rather than merely solving today's frustration?
Primary output: Credible Alternative Set.
Question 9 — What decision can you responsibly own?
This question moves the advisor from analysis to accountable commitment without forcing premature certainty.
Dimensions to examine
Understanding of reasoning
Acceptance of trade-offs
Readiness of team and family
Client protection plan
Ability to execute
Conditions that still require resolution
Confidence appropriate to the evidence
Illustrative prompts
Can you explain why this decision is right without quoting someone else's sales pitch?
What downside are you knowingly accepting?
What would cause you to pause or reverse course?
Primary output: Decision Record and Permissioned Next Step.
9. Interpretation Without False Precision
VYNE's 9 may support structured ratings, but the system must not imply that a complex advisor decision can be solved by adding nine numbers.
Ratings are directional signals. They should reveal where the decision requires discussion, evidence or diligence.
Rating
Meaning
Required response
0 — Unknown
The advisor cannot yet answer or evidence is unavailable.
Create a diligence item.
1 — Low concern / strong alignment
The dimension is working or well supported.
Protect it and verify if material.
2 — Manageable tension
A real issue exists but may be improved without major structural change.
Test stay-and-strengthen remedies.
3 — Material tension
The issue significantly affects clients, business, team or future state.
Evaluate alternatives and mitigation.
4 — Critical constraint or misalignment
The issue may make the current or proposed path unsustainable.
Do not advance without resolution or explicit acceptance.
SCORING RULE
The score never makes the recommendation. The evidence, trade-offs and advisor's objectives do.
10. Decision States
The methodology produces one of five decision states. These are more useful than a simple move / do not move conclusion.
State
Meaning
Typical next action
STAY
The current model remains the strongest available path.
Protect strengths and establish a review trigger.
STRENGTHEN
The current model can plausibly reach the future state if defined issues are improved.
Pursue internal remedies, renegotiation or business changes.
PREPARE
A future decision may be warranted, but evidence, readiness or timing is insufficient.
Complete diligence, team alignment, planning or capability work.
MOVE
A credible alternative more responsibly advances the future state and constraints can be managed.
Proceed through permissioned diligence, negotiation and implementation.
CONTINUE DILIGENCE
No path is yet sufficiently supported for a responsible conclusion.
Resolve decision-changing unknowns before commitment.
11. Bias and Decision-Risk Controls
The expansion of advisor options increases exposure to predictable decision errors. VYNE must recognize them without pretending advisors are irrational.
Decision risk
How it appears
VYNE control
First credible option bias
Treating the first attractive path as the benchmark.
Build the alternative set before preference hardens.
Deal salience
Overweighting transition money, payout or headline economics.
Evaluate total economics, durability, control and downside.
Status quo comfort
Treating familiarity as evidence that staying is safe.
Compare the cost of inaction to the cost of change.
Problem substitution
Solving today's frustration instead of the long-term decision.
Return to the Future-State Mandate.
Confirmation seeking
Collecting evidence that supports an emerging preference.
Assign a deliberate challenge case.
Sunk-cost attachment
Protecting past investment after the future case has weakened.
Evaluate from the present forward.
Social proof
Assuming a path is right because peers selected it.
Test fit against the advisor's own mandate.
Urgency distortion
Allowing deadlines or competitive pressure to narrow thought.
Separate real timing constraints from manufactured urgency.
Complexity avoidance
Choosing the easiest option because the comparison is overwhelming.
Sequence the decision and reduce it to material variables.

AI may strengthen each stage, but it must operate within the Constitution.

No agent may select a firm or path as the final decision-maker.

No agent may hide assumptions behind a proprietary score.
External communication requires appropriate human approval.
17. Quality Assurance
A VYNE engagement is complete only when the work meets the methodology and documentation standards.
The decision is stated clearly.
The current reality includes strengths as well as tensions.
The Future-State Mandate is prioritized.
Material constraints and unknowns are visible.
Staying, strengthening and waiting were considered where credible.
Alternatives were evaluated using consistent standards.
Material claims and assumptions are classified.
Conflicts and compensation are disclosed appropriately.
The advisor understands the reasoning and trade-offs.
Permissions and next steps are explicit.
18. What the Methodology Refuses
Beginning with a preferred destination
Treating headline economics as the decision
Manufacturing urgency
Using a score to replace judgment
Ignoring the strongest case for staying
Presenting verbal promises as verified capabilities
Assuming the advisor's partners, team or clients share the same decision
Moving into implementation before decision-changing unknowns are resolved
Confusing more information with greater clarity
19. Adoption Standard
The VYNE Framework and VYNE's 9 become canonical only after they have been tested through real advisor scenarios, reviewed for legal and trademark considerations, mapped into the advisor deliverables and CRM, and trained through case-based exercises.
Changes should improve clarity or decision quality. Additional language should not be added merely to make the methodology appear more sophisticated.
PERMANENT SIMPLIFICATION
Four questions govern the decision. Nine questions reveal what must be understood. Every tool, service and system exists to support those thirteen questions.
