# The VYNE Digital Experience Strategy

**Status:** DRAFT for founder review · **Date:** 2026-07-25 (rev. 2 — recruiter
relevance, direct-firm competition, book portability)
**Phase:** 1 — Expression · **Derived from:** The VYNE Constitution v1.0, the
Founder Alignment Memo, and The VYNE Story.

**What this document is:** the strategy that governs *every* digital surface
through which an advisor experiences VYNE — the website, email, research, reports,
the advisor portal, AI/answer search, and the eventual workspaces. The website is
one expression of it, not the whole of it.

**What this document is NOT.** No pages, navigation, sitemaps, wireframes, layouts,
fonts, colors, or UI. Those come later (Phase 2+) and derive from this. This is
experience strategy, written *before* UX on purpose.

**The one question everything answers:** *What should an advisor believe, feel,
understand, and do after each interaction with VYNE?*

---

## 1. Purpose — why the digital experience exists

The digital experience exists to **begin, extend, and deepen a trusted consulting
relationship** — never to sell software or manufacture a transaction. Every surface
is a place where an advisor can feel understood and think more clearly about a
consequential decision: before a human conversation, between conversations, and
long after. It is the consulting relationship *made continuous* — generous before
it asks for anything, always in service of the advisor's decision.

## 2. The competitive reality this strategy answers (2026)

Advisors have **more information and more options than ever.** Destination firms
(e.g. LPL Financial, Raymond James, and others) increasingly offer strong websites,
AEO strategies, capability content, affiliation-model explanations, payout
information, recruiting calculators, and direct online advisor-intake — so an
advisor can research firms, submit their business, talk to firm recruiters, and get
preliminary economics *without an outside recruiter.*

VYNE does **not** win by out-explaining LPL about LPL, or by owning raw information.
The traditional recruiting activities — providing firm lists, repeating firm
marketing, generic comparisons, introductions, matching advisors to whoever pays a
fee — are commoditizing. VYNE wins on the advantages that require judgment:

> **Interpretation · Independent comparison · Verification · Advisor-specific
> analysis · Portability · Professional judgment · Decision quality.**

**Governing strategic conclusion (preserve verbatim):** *Advisors have more
information and more options than ever. VYNE gives them a disciplined, confidential
way to determine what applies to their business, compare the tradeoffs, test firm
claims, understand how much of their practice could realistically transfer, and
decide whether any change is warranted.* And: *VYNE does not need to outperform LPL
at explaining LPL, or Raymond James at explaining Raymond James. VYNE must help the
advisor determine which claims matter, which require verification, how alternatives
compare under consistent standards, and whether the advisor should move at all.*

**The one-line positioning the whole experience serves:** *"Advisors do not need more
information or more options. They need a better way to determine which option — if
any — is right for their business, clients, and future."* This separates VYNE from
both traditional recruiters and software-driven matching platforms.

## 3. Design principles (derived from the Constitution)

- **Advisor first.** Every interaction improves the advisor's ability to decide. *(P2)*
- **Understanding before persuasion.** We demonstrate understanding before we ask. *(P8)*
- **Give before you ask.** Meaningful value whether they share little or much. *(P10)*
- **Evidence, not assertion.** Claims rest on evidence; uncertainty is named. *(P7)*
- **Interpretation over access.** We don't compete on owning information; we
  independently determine what it *means* for this advisor's actual business.
- **No preferred destination.** As comfortable leading an advisor to *stay* as to move. *(P3, P8)*
- **The relationship is the hero; technology is the quiet edge.** *(P4, P6)*
- **Calm over urgency.** No pressure patterns; consequential moments slow down. *(P8)*
- **One firm, everywhere.** Same integrity, voice, and judgment on every surface. *(P6)*

## 4. The Advisor Journey (ten stages)

Defined by *what changes for the advisor*, never by any page. Note the added
**Perspective** stage — where the advisor understands what applies to them *before*
being asked to decide.

> Awareness → Interest → Trust → Understanding → **Perspective** → Consultation →
> Evaluation → Decision → Transition / implementation *(if appropriate)* →
> Long-term relationship

Movement is never rushed, and reaching a decision to **stay** is a successful VYNE
outcome.

## 5. The Emotional Journey

At each stage: what the advisor genuinely **needs**; what they **think**, **feel**,
and **fear**; **how trust is earned** (observable behaviors); what VYNE
**demonstrates**; and what the advisor **leaves believing**.

### 1 · Awareness
- **Needs:** a reason to believe this isn't another pitch — something useful, no strings.
- **Thinks:** "Who is this — another recruiter?" · **Feels:** guarded curiosity. · **Fears:** being sold to.
- **Trust earned by:** neutrality, accuracy, and giving something genuinely useful for free.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** real expertise and independence — evidence-based insight that asks nothing.
- **Leaves believing:** *"This is different. They actually understand my world."*

### 2 · Interest
- **Needs:** more substance without commitment; proof the value continues.
- **Thinks:** "Maybe there's something worth my time." · **Feels:** cautious openness. · **Fears:** being pursued or listed.
- **Trust earned by:** substance without strings; no pressure to talk; respecting their pace.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** more genuinely useful material; a standing invitation, never a chase.
- **Leaves believing:** *"They lead with value, not a funnel."*

### 3 · Trust
- **Needs:** to know it is safe to be candid — that information won't be used against them.
- **Thinks:** "Could I talk to these people honestly?" · **Feels:** guarded confidence. · **Fears:** their information being shared or weaponized.
- **Trust earned by:** confidentiality, transparency about how VYNE works and is paid, consistency.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** plain disclosure, protected confidentiality, and no house answer.
- **Leaves believing:** *"I can be candid with them without it costing me."*

### 4 · Understanding
- **Needs:** to be understood *specifically* — their book, their constraints — not as a generic advisor.
- **Thinks:** "Do they get *my* practice?" · **Feels:** seen. · **Fears:** a generic assessment; being reduced to a number.
- **Trust earned by:** preparation, listening, remembering context, accuracy about their specifics.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** specific, accurate understanding of their situation, and honesty about what's unknown.
- **Leaves believing:** *"They understand my business better than anyone I've spoken to."*

### 5 · Perspective *(new)*
- **Needs:** to know which information actually matters, what their realistic options are, and how the tradeoffs apply to *their* book — *before* deciding anything.
- **Thinks:** "There's so much noise — what actually applies to me?" · **Feels:** clarity emerging from confusion. · **Fears:** acting on firm marketing that doesn't fit their business; portability surprises.
- **Trust earned by:** independent interpretation, testing firm claims, consistent standards across options, acknowledging uncertainty, distinguishing verified facts from claims and judgment.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** an independent read of what the firms, economics, restrictions, and portability *mean for this advisor* — which claims matter, which need verification, how options compare under consistent standards.
- **Leaves believing:** *"I now understand which information matters, what my realistic options are, and how the tradeoffs apply to my business."*

### 6 · Consultation
- **Needs:** measured, honest judgment on whether — and how — to act.
- **Thinks:** "Are they thinking carefully, or steering me?" · **Feels:** respected, unhurried. · **Fears:** being pushed toward a move; a rushed recommendation.
- **Trust earned by:** willingness to recommend staying, neutrality, and the discipline to slow down.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** measured judgment and a genuine willingness to say stay or wait.
- **Leaves believing:** *"They're thinking about what's right for me, not for them."*

### 7 · Evaluation
- **Needs:** to weigh the recommendation and its reasoning — and understand portability and economic consequences realistically.
- **Thinks:** "Can I trust this enough to act on it?" · **Feels:** clarity and the weight of the decision. · **Fears:** a hidden agenda; portability or revenue surprises after moving.
- **Trust earned by:** evidence, explicit assumptions, honest confidence levels, disclosure of compensation and conflicts.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** an explainable perspective — evidence, assumptions, portability ranges, what would change our view — and plain disclosure.
- **Leaves believing:** *"I understand not just what they think, but why — and I can weigh it myself."*

### 8 · Decision
- **Needs:** confidence and ownership of the choice — including the choice to stay.
- **Thinks:** "I know what I'll do, and why." · **Feels:** grounded. · **Fears:** regret; missing something.
- **Trust earned by:** supporting whatever they decide, with no pull toward a particular outcome.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** full support for the decision, including staying.
- **Leaves believing:** *"This was my decision, made with the best help I've ever had."*

### 9 · Transition / implementation *(if appropriate)*
- **Needs:** steady, competent support through execution; realistic expectations on portability and operational burden.
- **Thinks:** "Will they stay with me through the hard part?" · **Feels:** resolve mixed with anxiety. · **Fears:** being abandoned; chaos; losing clients, revenue, or service continuity.
- **Trust earned by:** presence, preparation, accuracy, and honesty about what's still uncertain.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** steady presence and competence; the same understanding carrying into execution.
- **Leaves believing:** *"They didn't disappear once the decision was made."*

### 10 · Long-term relationship
- **Needs:** continuity — to be remembered, and to have help ready for the next decision.
- **Thinks:** "They still know me and my business." · **Feels:** trust that compounds. · **Fears:** being forgotten; re-explaining everything next time.
- **Trust earned by:** remembering context, staying useful between decisions, continued neutrality.
- **VYNE demonstrates:** persistent, deepening understanding and useful presence.
- **Leaves believing:** *"VYNE understands my business better than anyone, and always will."*

## 6. The direct-firm digital path

Many advisors will arrive at VYNE **after** researching firms through AI and search,
visiting destination-firm recruiting sites, speaking directly with firm recruiters,
receiving preliminary proposals, using calculators, and forming an early preference.

**VYNE does not dismiss that research — VYNE helps the advisor test it.** The role is
to independently determine what those firm claims, economics, and restrictions mean
for *this* advisor's actual book: which claims matter, which require verification,
how the options compare under consistent standards, how much of the practice would
realistically transfer, and whether a move is warranted at all. VYNE respects the
advisor's homework and makes it *rigorous.*

## 7. The self-directed path

Not every advisor initially needs a consultant. The experience must provide genuine
**education, questions, comparisons, and frameworks** to advisors who want to
research independently — demonstrating value and trust **without fear, artificial
urgency, or withholding basic information.**

It should also make plain **when VYNE's consulting process adds real value** —
namely when the decision becomes complex, confidential, economically consequential,
difficult to compare, dependent on the composition of the advisor's book, exposed to
portability risk, or connected to ownership, succession, team, culture, or long-term
enterprise value. The self-directed path is not a lesser experience; it is the same
integrity, meeting an advisor who isn't ready for a conversation yet.

## 8. Content Strategy

Content exists to **demonstrate understanding and supply evidence** — never to
manufacture urgency or lead with VYNE.

- **What questions exist?** They escalate with the journey: *Is a change worth
  considering? What are my real options? What do this firm's claims actually mean
  for me? How much of my book would realistically transfer? What are the risks I'm
  not seeing? Who can I trust — and how are they paid?*
- **Who asks them?** Advisors at different depths — the curious, the weighing, the
  ready — and their future selves.
- **What answers them?** Independent interpretation and current market intelligence,
  honest analysis, tested firm claims, and — as trust deepens — analysis specific to
  *their* book. Never assertion; never volume for its own sake.
- **Where should it live?** Public, generous content earns awareness and trust;
  confidential, personalized analysis earns understanding, perspective, and
  consultation; ongoing research sustains the long-term relationship. Always: *give
  first, prove rather than claim.*

## 9. Consultation Strategy

- **When technology helps:** gathering and structuring understanding, surfacing
  relevant market intelligence, keeping the relationship continuous, making the
  consultant more informed and current, and doing the *give-before-you-ask* work at
  scale (research and honest self-assessment that help even if the advisor never
  speaks with us).
- **When humans step in:** for judgment, for the perspective itself, for every
  consequential conversation, and for anything requiring trust, nuance, or
  accountability. **The recommendation is always a human's judgment.**
- **When we deliberately slow down:** before a perspective hardens, and before a
  decision is acted on. Speed for *information*; slowness for *judgment*. We never
  manufacture urgency; sometimes, on purpose, we manufacture patience.

## 10. Experience Principles (testable, every surface)

Every page, email, meeting, report, and recommendation must:

1. **Demonstrate understanding before it asks for anything.**
2. **Be equally comfortable concluding "stay."**
3. **Carry evidence, and name its uncertainty** — distinguishing verified facts,
   firm claims, public information, advisor-provided information, VYNE assumptions,
   and professional judgment.
4. **Lead with the advisor and the relationship — never the technology.**
5. **Be transparent about how VYNE works and is paid.**
6. **Never manufacture urgency; slow the consequential moments.**
7. **Feel like one firm** — the same voice and integrity everywhere.

The test for any digital artifact is the foundation's review gate: *which
Constitution principle does this support? which part of the Memo guided it? how does
it reinforce The VYNE Story from the advisor's perspective?*

## 11. Synthesis — the answer to the one question

After any interaction with VYNE, an advisor should **believe** they've met a
genuinely different, independent firm with no stake in any particular answer;
**feel** understood, respected, and unhurried; **understand** their own situation —
including what applies to their book, what's still unknown, and how much would
realistically transfer — more clearly than before; and **do** the next honest thing,
never because they were pushed.
