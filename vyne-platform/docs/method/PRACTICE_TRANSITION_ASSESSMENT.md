# VYNE Practice Transition Assessment (concept)

**Status:** CONCEPT — signature consulting framework; methodology largely to be
developed · **Date:** 2026-07-25
**Type:** Operational — the flagship capability of the VYNE Method. Not a
foundational document. Bound by the Constitution and delivered to *The VYNE
Standard* (`THE_VYNE_STANDARD.md`).

> **Working title only.** "VYNE Practice Transition Assessment" is a working name, not
> approved brand language. No trademark. Final naming is a founder decision (below).

## The real question

An advisor is not asking *"how much of my book transfers?"* They are asking:

> **"What happens to my business if I make this move?"**

Book portability answers one part. The signature VYNE engagement answers the whole
question: what a potential decision — move, affiliation change, partnership,
acquisition, succession, or **staying** — means for the advisor's clients, book,
team, economics, operating model, ownership, enterprise value, risk, and future.

The ambition (long-term): VYNE becomes known for *one* deliverable advisors
immediately recognize as uniquely valuable — the way McKinsey has transformation
plans and Bain has due diligence. Every later thing we build (website, workspace,
intelligence, AI) exists to produce this deliverable **better and more
consistently.**

## The twelve areas

Each area lists its core question and what it evaluates. **Vocabularies and
classifications are captured where the founder specified them; the underlying
analytical *methods* are mostly not yet developed** — see "Methodology maturity."

### 1. Advisor objectives and decision criteria
*What is the advisor actually trying to accomplish?* The assessment **begins with
advisor-defined objectives, not destinations** — e.g. better client capabilities,
autonomy, economics, ownership, succession, less operational burden, better
technology, team constraints, culture, growth, enterprise value, less risk, or
*remaining where they are with greater confidence.*

### 2. Current-practice profile
A fair, structured understanding of the business today: assets · revenue ·
households · client segments · products/programs · team · service model · expenses ·
payout · operating responsibilities · growth · ownership · succession · technology ·
capabilities · constraints · enterprise-value considerations · **current-firm
advantages and frustrations.** The current firm is assessed **fairly**, not merely as
a baseline to escape.

### 3. Book portability and transferability
The existing six-dimension capability, preserved in full: client · asset ·
product/program · revenue · operational · capability portability. Ranges, confidence,
assumptions, open questions, graduated disclosure, evidence provenance, cross-model
comparison, and the option of staying — all retained. **Full detail:**
[`BOOK_PORTABILITY.md`](BOOK_PORTABILITY.md).

### 4. Business capability compatibility
*Distinct from technical product portability.* Not "can the account transfer?" but
**"can the advisor keep serving clients and running the practice at the level
required?"** Evaluate whether each destination supports the advisor's required
planning, portfolio construction, investment flexibility, alternatives, insurance,
trust/estate, banking, lending, securities-based lending, retirement plans,
stock-plan and institutional business, family-office services, tax-aware planning,
reporting, billing, technology, data, compliance, marketing, branding, team
permissions, service model, discretion, succession, ownership, and growth strategy.
Classify each: **fully supported · supported with limitations · requires a workaround
· requires outsourcing · requires approval · creates material friction · unavailable
· uncertain (requires verification).**

### 5. Transition economics
Beyond the headline recruiting package: transition assistance · forgivable loans ·
back-end incentives · payout · recurring revenue · expenses · platform/technology/
office/compliance/financing costs · staffing · revenue and asset attrition · product
replacement · high-level tax considerations *(not tax advice)* · cash-flow timing ·
break-even · downside cases · long-term economics. Compare **year 1 / 3 / 5 / 10**
where appropriate. Clearly separate **firm-provided economics · verified terms ·
modeled assumptions · advisor-provided information · VYNE estimates · unresolved
variables.**

### 6. Enterprise continuity and value
A distinct area. How each option may affect practice value · ownership · equity ·
valuation multiple · buyer universe · succession flexibility · financing ·
transferability of ownership · internal succession · external sale · team retention ·
recurring-revenue quality · brand equity · growth potential · concentration risk ·
long-term control. **No channel is inherently superior** — independence, employee
affiliation, equity, and ownership are right or wrong only relative to *this*
advisor's objectives, business, risk tolerance, and desired future.

### 7. Client impact *(a decision criterion, not just transition management)*
Continuity of service · account-opening burden · product/investment changes ·
potential client tax consequences · lending disruption · lost capabilities · pricing
· reporting · technology · communication · trust · advisor availability during
transition · operational-error and confusion risk · client-attrition risk.

### 8. Team impact
Retention · compensation · titles/roles · licensing · employment structure · benefits
· ownership · career paths · culture · workload · training · office · recruiting needs
· transition responsibilities · key-person risk · succession implications.

### 9. Operating-model impact
How daily operation changes: advisor responsibilities · staffing · compliance burden
· technology/vendor management · billing · trading · supervision · marketing · admin ·
HR · cybersecurity · books-and-records · client service · management time ·
outsourcing · governance. **Especially important when comparing employee,
independent-contractor, supported-independence, hybrid-RIA, and independent-RIA
models.**

### 10. Risk and uncertainty
Structured across legal · contractual · Broker Protocol · compliance · client and
revenue attrition · product compatibility · operational disruption · team retention ·
cultural fit · integration · financing · concentration · technology · vendor ·
reputation · execution · enterprise value · unknown/unverified claims. Each
significant risk identifies **likelihood · consequence · evidence · mitigation ·
confidence · unresolved questions.** Avoid false precision.

### 11. Due-diligence and verification plan
The assessment does not merely compare marketing claims. For each serious
alternative, identify: **what is known · what is claimed · what is assumed · what must
be verified · who verifies it · what documentation is required · which questions to
ask · which stakeholders should attend · what would disqualify the option · what could
materially change the conclusion.**

### 12. Integrated conclusion
Supports more than binary move-or-stay. Possible conclusions include: **stay · stay
and renegotiate · wait · conduct further diligence · move · change channel · join a
team · create a partnership · acquire · sell · pursue succession · restructure the
current practice · revisit after defined milestones.** Every conclusion explains
**why · supporting evidence · assumptions · confidence · material risks · alternatives
considered · what could change it · next steps** — i.e. it is delivered to The VYNE
Standard.

## Provenance discipline (throughout every area)

Every material statement is tagged by source: **verified fact · firm-provided claim ·
public information · advisor-provided information · VYNE assumption · VYNE professional
judgment · unresolved uncertainty.** This is non-negotiable and flows from Evidence
Before Opinion.

## Relationship to Book Portability, and internal dependencies

Book Portability (Area 3) is **preserved in full and repositioned as one major module**
of this assessment. Key dependencies to develop:

- Portability (3) feeds **revenue economics** (5) — asset portability ≠ revenue
  portability.
- **Capability compatibility** (4) can make an asset that *technically* transfers
  *unserviceable* — it constrains portability's practical meaning.
- Portability and capability gaps drive **client impact** (7) and **operating-model
  impact** (9).
- Enterprise continuity (6) depends on revenue quality, ownership, and succession —
  informed by 3, 5, and 9.
- Every area feeds **risk** (10), the **verification plan** (11), and the **integrated
  conclusion** (12).

## Methodology maturity (what is NOT yet developed)

This is a **framework and question set**, not a methodology. Deliberately *not*
invented here (to avoid false precision): the analytical methods, scoring or rating
approaches, economic and valuation models, data sources and firm/channel intelligence,
confidence-calibration standards, and any templates or formulas. Those are registered
as future work (see `README.md` and `BOOK_PORTABILITY.md`) and must be developed with
real evidence — not fabricated to make the framework look complete. Where a serious
engagement would need a method we don't yet have, the assessment should **say so.**

## Founder decisions required (surfaced, not assumed)

1. **Final name** of the signature assessment (working title only today).
2. Whether **The VYNE Standard** stays internal or eventually becomes public.
3. Whether the assessment is **one comprehensive engagement** or a **modular set** of
   analyses.
4. Which elements are **included in every engagement** vs. optional.
5. Which elements require **deeper (Level 2/3) disclosure** to produce.
6. Whether the conclusion includes an **explicit recommendation** or a
   **decision-support conclusion** (present the analysis, let the advisor decide).
7. **How much of the methodology** should eventually be explained publicly.
8. Whether **IP / trademark review** is warranted later.
