# Book Portability & Transferability Analysis

**Status:** Capability defined (v0.1) — methodology to be developed · **Date:** 2026-07-25
**Type:** Operational — a central capability of the VYNE Method. Not a foundational
document. Bound by the Constitution (Advisor First, Evidence Before Opinion,
Decisions Over Transactions, No Preferred Destination) and the review gate.

## Why this is central

A recruiting proposal built on current T12, revenue, or assets is **incomplete**
unless the advisor understands **how much of the underlying business could
realistically move** to that specific firm or channel. Portability is where VYNE's
independent, advisor-specific judgment is most valuable — and it is not something a
destination firm will assess objectively about itself. Portability is therefore a
core VYNE consulting capability, not a footnote.

**Portability is never one percentage.** It is evaluated across the dimensions
below, always as ranges with stated confidence — never false precision.

## The six dimensions

### 1. Client portability
Which clients and households are *reasonably likely* to follow the advisor.
Factors: strength and history of the advisor–client relationship; whether the
relationship is primarily with the advisor, team, branch, or institution; client
age, complexity, geography, and service expectations; team continuity;
communication restrictions; contractual restrictions; applicable Broker Protocol
considerations; relationship concentration and key-person risk.
*Do not guarantee that any client will transfer.*

### 2. Asset portability
Which assets may transfer in kind · require special handling · require liquidation
or restructuring · remain at the prior institution · require destination-firm
approval · be subject to custody, platform, product, or account restrictions.

### 3. Product & program portability
Whether each destination can support the advisor's current advisory programs ·
brokerage products · SMAs · mutual funds · annuities · insurance business ·
alternatives and private investments · direct-held products · lending and banking ·
retirement plans · trusts and custody · concentrated or restricted securities ·
options and specialized trading · proprietary products · legacy/grandfathered
arrangements. Classify each as: **fully supported · supported with restrictions ·
transferable but not actively serviceable · replacement required · additional
verification required · potentially nonportable.**

### 4. Revenue portability
How much revenue may survive after client attrition · asset attrition · repricing ·
payout changes · product replacements · temporary transition disruption ·
advisory-program changes · lost trails or commissions · platform expenses · staffing
changes · unsupported business · delayed billing or account conversion.
*Do not equate asset portability with revenue portability.*

### 5. Operational portability
The practical transition burden: account-opening and repapering · staffing demands ·
client communication · account complexity · product review · compliance · legal
limitations · training · data readiness · destination-firm transition support ·
expected service disruption · time to restore normal operations.

### 6. Capability portability
Whether the advisor can preserve the client experience and business model after the
move: planning · investment flexibility · lending · banking · alternatives ·
insurance · trust services · research · portfolio construction · technology ·
reporting · billing · team permissions · branding · marketing · succession and
ownership capabilities.
*An asset may technically transfer while the advisor's service model does not.*

## Consistent comparison across models

The **same** advisor book is evaluated under consistent standards across:
another employee/wirehouse model · regional firms · independent broker-dealers ·
supported independence · hybrid RIA models · independent RIAs · team/partnership
opportunities · **and remaining at the current firm.**

## The portability deliverable (eventual outputs)

- Estimated **ranges**, not false precision.
- High-confidence portable assets and revenue.
- Assets/products requiring additional verification.
- Potentially impaired or nonportable business.
- Client-impact risks · operational burden · material assumptions · confidence
  levels · unresolved questions · evidence sources · information that would improve
  precision.
- One-, three-, five-, and ten-year economic implications where appropriate.

Every output must clearly distinguish among: **verified facts · firm-provided
claims · public information · advisor-provided information · VYNE assumptions · VYNE
professional judgment · unresolved uncertainty.**

## Graduated disclosure (mandatory)

An advisor must **never** be required to provide a full client-level book or
client-identifying information to begin receiving value. *More information improves
precision; it is never the price of admission.*

- **Level 1 — High-level assessment.** From aggregated inputs (AUM, T12, household
  count, advisory-vs-brokerage mix, major product categories, alternatives, annuity
  and insurance business, lending, retirement-plan business, team structure, broad
  client characteristics). Produces preliminary portability ranges, risks, questions.
- **Level 2 — Redacted / aggregated analysis.** More precise by product category,
  program, account type, asset class, or business line — *without* client-identifying
  information.
- **Level 3 — Deeper confidential review.** More detail where the advisor
  *voluntarily* provides enough to improve precision.

## Boundaries

**VYNE does:** evaluate portability · evaluate platform compatibility · identify
transfer risks · model likely economic implications · develop verification questions
· support due diligence · provide professional consulting judgment.

**VYNE does not:** provide legal advice · guarantee client movement · guarantee
asset transferability · issue an actual destination-firm offer · make suitability
determinations for clients · physically transfer accounts or products · replace the
receiving firm's legal, compliance, operations, or transition teams.

Any economic or portability output is described as an **estimate, range, scenario,
or assessment — never a guarantee or firm offer.**
