# VYNE Brand Standards (strategy level)

**Status:** Accepted with minor revisions (founder, 2026-07-25); living · **Phase:** 1 —
Expression. *Deliberately left open:* the brand promise (§2), the signature-assessment
name, and validating bronze/ivory in digital products (§11).
**Derived from:** the Constitution, Founder Alignment Memo, VYNE Story, Digital
Experience Strategy, Advisor Journey, Practice Transition Assessment, and The VYNE
Standard. **Governs:** how VYNE *sounds, feels, presents itself, and behaves.*

**Scope.** This is the brand *strategy*, not the visual system. Typography, color, and
imagery here are **principles and direction, not final creative selections.** The
existing visual assets — palette and type in [`BRAND_TOKENS.md`](BRAND_TOKENS.md),
[`Brand_Guide_v1.0.md`](Brand_Guide_v1.0.md), and the approved logo
([`LOGO_USAGE.md`](LOGO_USAGE.md)) — are **evaluated, not replaced.** No pages, UI,
wireframes, final marketing copy, or new visual assets are produced here.

> **Governing principle:** *VYNE should look and sound like the quality of its
> judgment — calm, rigorous, independent, discreet, sophisticated, and human.*

---

## 1. Brand foundation

- **Who VYNE serves:** established financial advisors facing a consequential decision
  about the future of their **business, clients, team, and career.**
- **The problem:** advisors now have *more information and more options than ever* —
  and no disciplined, confidential, independent way to determine what actually applies
  to *their* practice.
- **What VYNE provides:** outside, evidence-based consulting that turns that
  overwhelming marketplace into a clear, advisor-specific decision — understanding,
  interpretation, impact analysis on the business, and calibrated professional judgment.
- **Why VYNE is credible:** evidence-based and neutral (Evidence Before Opinion), with
  no preferred destination, delivered to The VYNE Standard, by a firm whose only stake
  is the quality of the advisor's decision.
- **What VYNE must never become:** a software company, recruiting marketplace,
  lead-generation platform, broker-dealer, destination firm, operational transition
  vendor, or transactional recruiter.
- **Role of technology in the brand:** it makes the consultants more informed and
  consistent — it is the quiet edge, **never the identity.**
- **Desired emotional outcome:** *"They understand my business better than anyone I've
  spoken to, and I can trust their judgment."*

## 2. Brand promise (working — deliberately not locked)

VYNE helps an advisor reach a **clearer, better-supported, more confident decision**
about their business and future. It does **not** promise a better deal, a successful
move, guaranteed portability, higher compensation, a placement, complete objectivity,
certainty, or a move as the goal — and it stays true when the right answer is to *stay,
wait, or continue on their own.*

**Not yet locked — on purpose.** Advisors are not buying analysis; they are buying
**confidence** in one of the most important decisions of their career. The final
promise should carry that emotion, so we keep exploring before committing. Working
formulations, in two families (internal strategy, not website copy):

*Clarity / support-framed:*
1. "A clearer, better-supported decision about your business and your future."
2. "The disciplined way to decide what actually applies to your business."

*Confidence-framed (the emotion):*
3. "Confidence in one of the most important decisions of your career."
4. "Confidence in the future of your practice."
5. "Decisions about your practice you won't second-guess."

**Direction:** the final promise likely **blends** the clarity/discipline of the first
family with the confidence of the second — support *and* the feeling it produces. Left
open until Messaging Architecture pressure-tests it against real audiences.

## 3. Positioning (internal)

- **Vs. traditional recruiters:** VYNE is *not* differentiated by firm access, deal
  knowledge, introductions, knowing who's hiring, or making a match. It is
  differentiated by **impartial interpretation and advisor-specific judgment.**
- **Vs. destination firms:** VYNE does not out-explain a firm about itself; it provides
  the **cross-market comparison and advisor-specific interpretation a
  destination firm cannot credibly offer.**
- **Vs. software / matching platforms:** VYNE does **not** reduce a consequential
  decision to a quiz, algorithm, score, or automated match. Technology strengthens the
  analysis; it never replaces professional judgment.
- **Vs. going alone:** VYNE does **not** claim every advisor needs a consultant. It is
  most valuable when the decision is **complex, confidential, financially consequential,
  hard to compare, or dependent on the composition of the actual practice.**

**Positioning statement (internal):** *VYNE is the independent consultancy that helps
an advisor determine what a potential change — if any — means for their whole practice,
and make a decision they can trust.*

**Positioning narrative (internal):** The market has solved *access.* Advisors can find
firms, read payouts, run calculators, and talk to recruiters directly. What remains
unsolved is *interpretation* — turning all of that into a confident, advisor-specific
decision about clients, book, team, economics, operations, ownership, enterprise value,
and risk. VYNE owns that ground: impartial, evidence-based, confidential judgment,
willing to conclude "stay," and accountable to a defined standard.

## 4. Brand personality (the few traits that govern decisions)

Six traits, chosen to *decide things* — not a wall of adjectives.

- **Discerning** — sees what matters and ignores noise. *Appears as:* sharp
  prioritization; naming the two questions that actually decide it. *Not:* opinionated
  for its own sake. *Language:* "The decision really turns on three things."
- **Composed** — calm under a high-stakes, emotional decision. *Appears as:* unhurried
  pacing; no manufactured urgency. *Not:* passive or slow. *Language:* "There's no need
  to rush this — let's get it right."
- **Candid** — tells the truth, including inconvenient truth. *Appears as:* naming risks
  plainly; recommending "stay" when warranted. *Not:* blunt without care. *Language:*
  "We don't think the economics justify the operational risk."
- **Discreet** — protects confidence instinctively. *Appears as:* graduated disclosure;
  never name-dropping other advisors. *Not:* secretive or evasive. *Language:* "You
  decide how much to share; we'll do meaningful work at any level."
- **Rigorous** — shows its work. *Appears as:* evidence, assumptions, confidence,
  provenance. *Not:* academic or heavy. *Language:* "Here's what's verified, and what
  we're still assuming."
- **Human** (quietly confident) — a person, not an institution. *Appears as:* plain
  language; genuine attention; memory of context. *Not:* folksy or salesy. *Language:*
  "How's the team feeling about all this?"

## 5. Voice

VYNE's voice is **clear, calm, direct, intelligent, human, evidence-aware, neutral,
confident without overstatement, sophisticated without jargon, and honest about
uncertainty.**

It avoids: hype, exaggerated claims, consulting clichés, generic luxury language,
unnecessary complexity, fear-based selling, false urgency, certainty unsupported by
evidence, language that makes the advisor feel uninformed or incapable, language that
assumes moving is the goal, and the overuse of "proprietary / exclusive / revolutionary
/ AI-powered."

**Voice vs. tone vs. style:**
- **Voice** = who VYNE is — *constant everywhere.*
- **Tone** = the emotional register — *adapts to context* (see §6).
- **Style** = the mechanics (sentence length, formatting, terminology) — *consistent,
  lightly adapted to medium.*

## 6. Tone by context

For each: *desired tone · what the advisor should feel · what to avoid.*

- **Public education** — generous, useful, unhurried · *"this is genuinely helpful"* ·
  avoid a sales undertone.
- **Early confidential inquiry** — warm, reassuring, precise on confidentiality ·
  *"it's safe to talk"* · avoid over-eagerness.
- **First consultation** — attentive, curious, composed · *"they're listening to me"* ·
  avoid pitching.
- **Difficult finding** — direct, respectful, evidence-anchored · *"they'll tell me the
  truth"* · avoid softening that hides the issue.
- **Recommendation to stay** — affirming, unembarrassed · *"they're not just trying to
  move me"* · avoid apology or hedging that implies failure.
- **Conditional recommendation** — precise about conditions · *"I know exactly what has
  to be true"* · avoid implying more certainty than exists.
- **Full recommendation** — clear, confident, accountable · *"I can act on this"* ·
  avoid overclaiming.
- **Economic analysis** — plain, calibrated, range-based · *"I understand the numbers"* ·
  avoid false precision.
- **Risk disclosure** — candid, proportionate · *"nothing's being hidden"* · avoid
  burying or alarmism.
- **Due diligence** — methodical, neutral · *"this is rigorous"* · avoid advocacy for a
  firm.
- **Transition support** — steady, competent, present · *"they've got me"* · avoid
  disappearing or minimizing the hard parts.
- **Advisor uncertainty or fear** — grounding, patient · *"I'm not being pushed"* ·
  avoid urgency or pressure.
- **With destination firms** — professional, exacting, advisor-loyal · *(the firm should
  feel: rigorous but fair)* · avoid coziness that compromises independence.
- **With attorneys / accountants** — precise, scoped, deferential to their domain ·
  *(they should feel: VYNE knows its lane)* · avoid practicing law/tax.

## 7. Writing principles

Begin with the advisor's decision, not VYNE's capabilities · explain *why* information
matters · distinguish **fact / claim / assumption / judgment** · state limitations ·
plain language · specificity over slogans · avoid false precision · never bury material
risks · acknowledge reasonable alternatives · preserve advisor autonomy · imply no
preferred destination · make complex ideas understandable without making them simplistic
· say **"we don't know"** when true · don't overuse the VYNE name · never sound like a
financial institution's recruiting department.

*Before → after:*
- "VYNE's proprietary intelligence platform delivers exclusive market insights." →
  "Here's how three firms actually compare for a book like yours — and where we'd verify
  their claims."
- "Maximize your transition package." → "We'll model what you'd actually keep after
  attrition, repricing, and transition costs — not just the headline check."
- "This is the perfect firm for you." → "Firm A fits your objectives best, provided it
  confirms alternatives custody and team comp in writing."

## 8. Message hierarchy (recommended)

Evaluated the proposed order; it's close. Recommended:

1. **The advisor's consequential decision** (their world, first).
2. **The difficulty of applying abundant information to a specific practice** (the real
   problem VYNE solves).
3. **VYNE's consulting role** (who we are — impartial, advisor-side).
4. **The signature assessment** (how we help — the whole business, not just the book).
5. **Portability, economics, capabilities, enterprise value, and risk** (what we analyze).
6. **Marketplace and firm intelligence** (the evidence base — *support, not headline*).
7. **The VYNE Standard** (how our judgment is disciplined).
8. **Possible outcomes, including staying** (no preferred destination).
9. **Technology as supporting infrastructure** (last, quiet).

*Change from the draft:* marketplace/firm intelligence moved **below** the assessment
and analysis — leading with "intelligence" risks the commoditized "exclusive data"
positioning we reject. Interpretation leads; data supports.

## 9. Language architecture

**Own** (define VYNE's ground): practice transition · decision clarity · advisor-specific
analysis · impact on the business (not just the book) · independent comparison · evidence-supported judgment ·
portability · capability compatibility · enterprise continuity · calibrated recommendation
· *staying as a valid outcome.*

**Use carefully** (each carries a risk):
- *independent / objective* — true, but never claim **complete** objectivity; pair with
  disclosure of how VYNE is paid.
- *proprietary / intelligence / platform* — internal-leaning; overuse reads
  software-first. Prefer describing the *judgment*, not the tooling.
- *methodology / comprehensive* — fine if real; avoid implying finished methods we
  haven't built.
- *fiduciary-like / unbiased / neutral / conflict-free* — **hazardous / prohibited as
  self-description** (founder ruling 2026-07-25). "Fiduciary-like" risks legal
  misimplication; "unbiased," "neutral," and "conflict-free" **overclaim** given VYNE's
  hiring-firm-paid model. VYNE *manages and discloses* incentives, never denies them —
  prefer *independent · transparent about how we're paid · conflicts managed, not hidden*
  (see `../commercial/BUSINESS_MODEL_AND_COMMERCIAL_PRINCIPLES.md`).
- *confidential* — supportable and central; express as behavior (graduated disclosure),
  not a slogan.

**Avoid:** best firm · perfect match · guaranteed · exclusive deal · highest offer ·
maximize your transition package · frictionless move · unbiased · neutral · conflict-free ·
complete objectivity · paid regardless of the recommendation · AI matching · instant
recommendation · marketplace platform · move your book · "we'll get you the best deal" ·
"one-click / self-serve analysis."

## 10. Visual-direction principles (not final designs)

VYNE should feel **refined, calm, editorial, spacious, disciplined, premium, understated,
contemporary-but-durable, and human rather than technological.**

Avoid cues that read: fintech · crypto · private-equity aggressive · generic
wealth-management corporate · startup SaaS · mass-market recruiting · ornate luxury ·
nightclub/hospitality luxury · dark/secretive · sterile · overly masculine · transactional.

Balance: **authority ↔ warmth · sophistication ↔ accessibility · privacy ↔ openness ·
premium ↔ restraint · tradition ↔ modernity.** The differentiator is *warmth with
discipline* — never coldness dressed as premium.

## 11. Color principles — *evaluating the approved palette*

The existing palette (`BRAND_TOKENS.md`) is **well-aligned and should be kept, with
discipline:** Midnight Navy `#081B36` (authority/ink), **Ivory `#F8F5EF`** (the warm,
human canvas — *the key differentiator*), White (documents), **Warm Bronze `#B88A5A`**
(a restrained, warm accent), Stone Gray (neutral/borders), Charcoal (high-contrast ink).

- **Ivory, not stark white, and warm bronze, not literal gold, are precisely what keep
  VYNE from reading like generic bank/law-firm navy-and-gold.** Preserve that; do not
  drift to a whiter canvas or a metallic-gold accent.
- **Bronze is an accent, used sparingly.** Overuse tips into "luxury / wealth-management
  gold." Restraint is the brand.
- **Metallic gold:** avoid literal or simulated metallic gold. Warm bronze *is* the muted
  accent; it stays matte and quiet.
- **Accessibility is non-negotiable** — the existing accessible-bronze-text rule
  (`#8A6234` for small text on light) stays.
- **Screen and print** must feel like the same firm; ivory/navy/bronze translate well to
  print — verify bronze reproduction.
- **Avoid the bank/law/luxury-real-estate feel** through *warmth (ivory), restraint
  (bronze sparing), and editorial white space* — not more color.
- **Bronze on screen is a hypothesis to validate, not a settled conclusion.** Warm
  bronze is beautiful in print and on navy, but genuinely hard to use well in digital
  products (small UI, thin strokes, varied displays). Treat the bronze/ivory system as a
  **direction to prove in real applications** — validate legibility, restraint, and
  digital reproduction before treating it as immutable.
- *Open:* no "information" semantic hue is defined yet (uses navy) — a future founder
  color decision.

## 12. Typography principles — *evaluating the approved direction*

Current: a **controlled serif + sans pairing** — Source Serif 4 (display/long-form) +
Inter (interface/data), with commercial faces (Trajan / Cormorant / Suisse) deferred.
This direction is sound and should hold:

- A **serif** for authority, long-form research, and advisor deliverables signals
  credibility and an *editorial* (not SaaS) character — a deliberate differentiator from
  sans-only fintech/SaaS.
- A **clean sans** for interface, data tables, and mobile keeps readability high.
- **Timelessness over trend.** Avoid fashion-editorial faces that hurt usability,
  technical/monospace display, overly-traditional (engraved) faces, and generic
  corporate defaults.
- **Caution on the deferred commercial faces:** *Trajan* in particular risks a
  theatrical, luxury/"movie-poster" tone — inconsistent with quiet confidence. Any
  commercial upgrade must protect readability across long-form, tables, and mobile, and
  must not make VYNE feel like a luxury brand. The current pairing is a strong, timeless
  baseline; treat an upgrade as optional, not needed.

## 13. Photography & imagery principles (not sourced yet)

Communicate **thoughtfulness, discretion, and real human attention.** Avoid the clichés:
handshakes, skyscrapers, advisors staring at screens, conference rooms, chess pieces,
compasses, bridges, mountain summits, stock charts, people pointing at documents,
artificial luxury offices. Prefer: **restrained environmental portraiture, real working
environments, thoughtful human moments, architectural detail, editorial imagery, tactile
materials, and subtle metaphor used sparingly.** Human over technological. *Do not source
or generate images yet.*

## 14. Data & evidence presentation principles

Aligned directly with The VYNE Standard: **clarity before decoration · ranges, not false
precision · source labels · confidence labels · verified-vs-unverified distinctions ·
assumptions visible near conclusions · risks never visually minimized · consistent
comparisons · restrained use of charts · narrative interpretation alongside data · no
scoring theater · no misleading precision.** Data earns trust by being honest, not
impressive.

## 15. Confidentiality expression

The advisor should feel **protected, respected, in control, unpressured to disclose
everything, and clear about how information will be used.** Express confidentiality as
**behavior — graduated disclosure** (meaningful value at any level) — not as a slogan.
**Do not** use "100% confidential" or similar absolute claims unless legally and
operationally supportable. Confidentiality should feel like *respect,* never *secrecy.*

## 16. Brand behavior (proven before visual identity)

The brand is demonstrated by conduct: remembers advisor context · doesn't rush ·
discloses compensation · challenges unsupported assumptions · acknowledges uncertainty ·
recommends staying when warranted · prepares thoroughly · doesn't overstate access ·
doesn't pressure for full-book disclosure · follows up with substance · separates evidence
from opinion · protects the advisor's time and confidentiality. **If the behavior and the
visuals ever conflict, the behavior is the brand.**

## 17. Brand architecture (keep it simple)

One master brand; resist sub-brand proliferation.

- **VYNE** — the master brand. **"VYNE Strategies"** is the firm/formal name (per the
  logo). *Recommend:* "VYNE" as the everyday brand, "VYNE Strategies" as the legal/formal
  signature. *(Small open item to confirm.)*
- **Practice Transition Assessment** — *descriptive working title;* potentially
  protectable later; not yet approved brand language.
- **The VYNE Standard** — internal working language; *may* become public (open founder
  decision).
- **VYNE Method** — internal, descriptive.
- **Advisor workspace / firm intelligence / founder intelligence** — internal
  capabilities and tools, **not** public sub-brands or products.
- **Newsletter / research / public education** — branded *under* VYNE, descriptively; no
  separate identity.

Guidance: keep names **descriptive and few**; treat product-y names as working titles;
flag any candidate for later trademark review rather than minting sub-brands now.

## 18. Brand guardrails (non-negotiable)

Consulting first · Advisor first · No preferred destination · No unsupported claims · No
artificial urgency · No software-first positioning · No implication that every advisor
should move · Never obscure how VYNE is paid · Never reduce the decision to a score · No
visual excess substituting for credibility · No claim of certainty where uncertainty
exists · **When behavior and visuals ever conflict, the behavior is the brand.**

## 19. The brand decision test

Apply to any future website copy, article, newsletter, report, visual, presentation,
software, recruiting outreach, advisor communication, or firm communication. Does it:

1. Strengthen trust?
2. Improve advisor understanding?
3. Reflect professional judgment?
4. Preserve neutrality (no preferred destination)?
5. Communicate the correct business model (consulting, not software/recruiting)?
6. Feel consistent with VYNE (calm, rigorous, human)?
7. Avoid hype and unsupported claims?
8. Support the consulting relationship?
9. Meet The VYNE Standard where analysis is involved?
10. Would an exceptional financial advisor be **proud to share this** with another
    exceptional advisor? *(the craftsmanship test)*

If it fails any, it isn't ready.
