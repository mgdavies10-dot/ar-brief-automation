# Business Model & Commercial Principles

**Status:** PLANNED — future work item, not yet defined · **Registered:** 2026-07-24
**Type:** **Operational, not foundational.** This document sits *outside* the
foundation trio (Constitution / Founder Alignment Memo / VYNE Story). A business
model evolves on its own clock — faster than strategy, far faster than philosophy —
so compensation, conflicts, and disclosure live here, never in the Constitution or
the Memo. It is bound by the Constitution and never overrides it.

## Why this is a separate document

The Constitution is **compensation-independent by design.** It must remain correct
whether VYNE is one day paid by hiring firms, advisor retainers, enterprise or
succession consulting, M&A advisory, subscription research, licensing, executive
search, or family-office work. Putting a revenue model into the enduring documents
would date them. This is where the commercial mechanics belong.

## Questions this document will answer

- **Who pays VYNE, and why?**
- **Conflicts of interest** — where they arise, and how they are managed.
- **Disclosure philosophy** — what we tell advisors, and when.
- **Neutrality safeguards** — the concrete mechanisms that keep advice objective.
- **Fee philosophy.**
- **Future revenue lines.**
- **Guardrails.**

## Founder's starting material (2026-07-24) — to shape the full document

**The intended model.** VYNE is generally compensated by **participating hiring
firms under written agreements** — the model used by established transition
consultancies. Neutrality and trust are earned the way they are for law firms,
investment banks, executive recruiters, and management consultants (all paid by
one side): through **transparency, reputation, process, evidence, and integrity** —
not through who writes the check.

**Incentive Alignment — the load-bearing principle.** VYNE's commercial model must
*reinforce, not undermine,* its constitutional principles. Regardless of how VYNE
is compensated, the firm's incentives must encourage objective advice, intellectual
honesty, and recommendations made in the advisor's best interest. **When commercial
incentives and advisor outcomes conflict, the advisor's long-term interests take
precedence.**

**Disclosure statement (draft — for this document, public disclosures, legal, and
the website FAQ):** *"VYNE is generally compensated by participating hiring firms
under written agreements. Our compensation does not determine our recommendations.
Advisors are encouraged to remain where they are whenever that is the best outcome
for their clients, businesses, and long-term goals. We do not recommend a
transition unless we believe it is in the advisor's best interest."*

## Its relationship to the foundation

Bound by the Constitution — especially **Decisions Over Transactions**, **Advisor
First**, and **Evidence Before Opinion** — and by the three-question review gate.
Because the intended model is firm-paid, its neutrality safeguards are not
boilerplate: they are what make firm-paid economics genuinely compatible with
advisor-first advice, and they deserve real rigor when this document is written.
